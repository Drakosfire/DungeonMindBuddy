# Session 20 Reference: Stacey, Stuart, and Marla Brambleback

## Purpose
A quick-reference NPC packet for the Bonogo/Stuart/Stacey side scene in Mossford.

Use this to:
- give Stacey more texture than "mean kid"
- make Stuart sympathetic and playable
- make Stacey's mother memorable and useful
- preserve the humor without losing the crisis tone

---

## Stacey Brambleback

**Role:** local child menace, bugbear girl, sideways survivalist  
**Age:** about 11–12  
**Species:** bugbear

### Core read
Stacey is not evil. She is a sharp, physical, too-clever kid who learned early that if she controls the tone, nobody gets to laugh at her first.

She is one of those children who has already started becoming a town type:
- rude
- practical
- fast-thinking
- quietly scared
- determined not to show it

### Appearance
- wiry and long-limbed
- shaggy russet-brown fur
- large amber eyes
- already tall for her age
- patched practical clothes worn with total confidence
- usually leans instead of stands normally
- moves with unsettling bugbear quiet

### Vibe at the table
- unimpressed stare
- appears beside people instead of approaching
- talks like she is doing everyone a favor by explaining reality
- always seems to know more than she should

### What she does
- startles younger kids for fun
- steals or "relocates" useful supplies
- knows hidden paths, sheds, crawlspaces, and roof access
- has been building a secret survival cache during the crisis

### Secret competence
While adults are arguing, Stacey has already been organizing child-scale emergency thinking:
- lantern oil
- flint
- old bread
- blanket
- sling stones
- maybe smoked fish or dried meat
- hiding spots for younger children if the bridge clogs

This makes her useful, not just comic.

### What she wants
- to not be treated like a child
- to look brave in front of the other kids
- to prove she sees the crisis more clearly than most adults
- to never admit she is scared

### What she knows
- she saw Lysandra near the west boundary stones
- she knows back paths between sheds and drainage cuts
- she knows where several children are hiding or watching from
- she may know where supplies have gone missing because she moved some of them herself

### Ready dialogue
- "Stuart ain't crying. He just does that with his face."
- "If the town falls apart, it'll be because adults love shouting more than moving."
- "I didn't steal it. I relocated it to a smarter owner."
- To Bonogo: "Oh good. A bigger version of the problem."

## Stuart

**Role:** shy halfling boy, emotional contrast, easy to recruit  
**Species:** halfling

### Core read
Stuart is small, shy, eager to please, and very easy to fluster. He hero-worships bold people quickly and is the sort of kid who wants badly to belong.

Bonogo has probably become the coolest person in Stuart's world for at least the next day or two.

### Appearance
- small even for a halfling child
- nervous posture
- huge expressive eyes
- reactive face; his feelings are obvious immediately
- slightly rumpled clothes
- often holding something like he was sent on an errand

### Vibe at the table
- eager
- truthful until he is trying desperately to impress someone
- follows stronger personalities
- wants approval badly

### What he wants
- to be seen as brave
- to stop being embarrassed by Stacey
- to stay near interesting people
- to be included

### What he feels about Stacey
From Stuart's perspective, Stacey is:
- unfair
- terrifying
- embarrassing
- always where interesting things happen

A useful subtle truth: Stuart may secretly admire her courage and competence even while resenting her.

### Extra texture
If needed, Stuart's family situation can be a little loose or fragile:
- raised mostly by his gran
- parents absent, gone, dead, or away for seasonal work
- spends time around the inn and around adults rather than with a stable family unit

That makes him more emotionally vulnerable and more susceptible to orbiting stronger kids like Stacey.

## Stacey and Stuart: Relationship

### Surface dynamic
Yes, Stacey bullies Stuart.

She:
- startles him
- tricks him
- makes him carry things
- talks over him
- humiliates him in front of other kids

He:
- dreads running into her
- reacts visibly every time
- complains about her honestly
- still keeps ending up near her orbit

### Deeper truth
Stacey does **not** actually hate Stuart.

She has decided he is "hers" to push around before anybody meaner gets to him.
From her perspective, she is half tormenting him and half hardening him up for the world.

That does **not** make her behavior kind. It makes it believable.

### Important nuance
If some other child humiliates Stuart too hard, Stacey is likely to step in.
Not gently. Not kindly. But immediately.

She would never call it protection.
She would frame it as standards, territory, or annoyance.

Example internal logic:
- "Only I get to make him miserable."
- "That wasn't funny enough to count."
- "He's useless if you scare him that badly."

This gives them a messy, frontier-child bond instead of a flat bully/victim structure.

## Marla Brambleback

**Role:** Stacey's mother, striking local adult, immediately memorable presence  
**Species:** bugbear  
**Age:** late 30s to early 40s

### Core read
Marla is a load-bearing adult.
She is strong, competent, visible in town, and charismatic precisely because she is not trying to be.

She is the kind of person who enters a scene already belonging there.

### Suggested work
Pick one depending on need:
- cooper
- cartwright
- river-hauler's quartermaster
- heavy-labor craftsperson with a reason to be around barrels, wagons, tools, and supply movement

### Appearance
- large, broad-shouldered bugbear woman
- beautifully kept dark chestnut fur
- reed-thread and copper trinkets braided into it
- smells like cedar oil, leather, river water, and woodsmoke
- rolled sleeves, strong forearms, practical clothes that fit because they must
- usually carrying something heavy like it offended her personally

### Vibe at the table
- low laugh
- steady eye contact
- gives orders like suggestions that get obeyed anyway
- not flashy, just undeniable
- children go still when she uses their full name
- dogs like her

### Why "Stacey's mom has got it going on" works
Marla is attractive because she is:
- competent
- grounded
- physically impressive
- not trying to impress anyone
- clearly capable of handling herself and others

The town likely has stories about her.
Possible examples:
- pulled a wagon wheel from a ditch herself
- carried a barrel alone
- fixed something important during a flood or storm
- once shut down a roomful of nonsense with a single sentence

### What she wants
- Stacey alive
- the town functioning
- fewer idiots improvising with fire near essential supplies
- children accounted for if evacuation begins

### Relationship to Stacey
Marla knows **exactly** what kind of terror she is raising.
She is trying to turn Stacey from "future menace" into "future leader."

She does not excuse Stacey's behavior, but she understands it.
She is likely harsher with Stacey in private than anyone realizes.

### Relationship to Stuart
Marla sees Stuart clearly.
She knows Stacey picks on him because he is reactive, sweet, and available.
She disapproves.

She has likely:
- fed him before
- patched a sleeve once
- barked at Stacey for going too far

Example line:
- "If you make that boy cry again, I'll have you stacking timber till your arms fall off."

## Best use in Session 20

Use this trio to do more than deliver a joke.
They can:
- reveal Lysandra's west-stones sighting
- give Bonogo a sideways but useful clue scene
- humanize town stress through children
- add humor without puncturing tension
- show that even the kids are already adapting to crisis

### Most useful Stacey clue
She saw Lysandra near the west boundary stones, talking to herself, drawing in the mud, and warning people not to cross between markers.

That turns the Bonogo side scene into a meaningful path back into the main session.

## Bonogo scene tone

Bonogo should recognize Stacey quickly as:
- fellow goblinoid chaos element
- a menace with promise
- a child who thinks she is running a district

That lets him engage her as something closer to a peer than an adult would.

### Suggested Bonogo joke beat
Bonogo looks at Stacey, then at Marla, and says:

> "To be fair, Stuart, this all makes more sense now. Stacey's mom has got it going on."

### Suggested Marla response
> "You planning to survive this storm, or die making observations?"

That keeps the joke, keeps Marla strong, and makes the scene memorable without breaking tone.

## Fast play summary

### Stacey Brambleback
Young bugbear girl. Sharp, silent, bossy, practical, scared under the surface. Bullies Stuart, but is also organizing useful survival behavior among the children.

### Stuart
Shy halfling boy. Eager, honest, expressive, and easily flustered. Wants approval and bravery. Genuinely distressed by Stacey, but still pulled into her orbit.

### Marla Brambleback
Bugbear mother. Strong, charismatic, capable, and deeply rooted in Mossford. Knows her daughter is a menace and intends to shape that into leadership before the world shapes it into something worse.
