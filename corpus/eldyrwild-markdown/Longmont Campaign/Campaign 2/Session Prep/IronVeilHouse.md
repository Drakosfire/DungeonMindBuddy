## The Ironveil House

**Objective:** Establish the party’s recovery space as Lysandra’s childhood home and let the environment reveal parts of her life before Mirathorn.

**Pressure:** None. This is sanctuary.

**Role:** Spine.

The Ironveil house should become the party's temporary base while they decide what happens next.

This is not a dungeon.

There are no secret cult chambers underneath it unless later play deliberately creates that fact.

There should be things to touch, ask about, eat, repair, laugh at, and notice.

---

### Established family context

**ESTABLISHED**

The Ironveils are an old Mireward family tied to the wall, local responsibility, farming, craft, and civic vigilance.

Known family:

- **Lysandro Ironveil** — Lysandra's father. Precision-parts maker and merchant. Loud, affectionate, anti-authoritarian, mechanically minded.
- **Merrow Ironveil** — Lysandra's mother. Fuller. Known for mocking reeve speeches and expressing pride through criticism.
- **Torr Ironveil** — Lysandra's grandfather. Retired tithe clerk. Quiet. Has a hidden framed commendation connected to Lysandra.
- **Calyx Ironveil** — Lysandra's younger brother, approximately 22. Wants to join the Mirathorn Guard; his petitions have gone unanswered.
- **Sibil “Bib” Ironveil** — Lysandra's aunt. Shrine worker and patrol widow. Her husband Hest Voss remains associated with the long fen patrol.
- **Delwen Rast** — not family, but part of Lysandra's Mireward history; former almost-fiancée, now married to Orrin Kettle.

**UNRESOLVED**

The corpus does not establish exactly which relatives currently live under Lysandro's roof.

For Session 29, choose who is physically present according to the scenes you want. Do not assume every named Ironveil lives here.

### Back to the Ironveil House

**Situation**

After the immediate fighting and triage, the Questionable Company returns to the house where they have been staying.

This is Lysandra's childhood home.

She knows where everything is without looking.

That should be obvious before anybody says it.

**Read aloud**

> The Ironveil house is still standing.
>
> At this point, that feels luxurious.
>
> Mud has been tracked across the entry. Someone has stacked three borrowed shields beside the door. Damp coats hang from hooks that clearly existed long before tonight.
>
> Lysandra enters ahead of you.
>
> She does not look around.
>
> She reaches past a cabinet without breaking stride, takes a key from a hook hidden behind it, opens the inner door, catches a loose floorboard with her heel before anyone trips over it, and says:
>
> “Boots there.”
>
> Then she stops.
>
> For just a moment, Lieutenant Ironveil looks embarrassed.
>
> “Sorry. Habit.”

**GM only**

Do not explain that this is her childhood home after the read-aloud unless a player asks.

Let recognition land through behavior.

Lysandra:

- Knows which chair wobbles.
- Knows where the good cups are kept.
- Automatically ducks beneath something taller PCs do not need to duck beneath.
- Knows where Lysandro keeps tools he insists are “not lost.”
- Can navigate the house in darkness.
- Occasionally begins doing something from childhood muscle memory and catches herself.

This is one of the few environments where **“Lieutenant Ironveil” is visibly not the oldest version of her identity.**

### The House

**PREP LOCK — physical layout**

The exact house layout was previously undefined. Use this for Session 29.

The Ironveil house is an old, heavily modified **two-story stone-and-timber home** built near one of Mireward's older wall roads.

It has been expanded repeatedly rather than designed as a whole.

Nothing quite lines up.

#### Ground floor

**Front Mudroom**

- Heavy benches.
- Too many boots.
- Wall hooks at several heights.
- Old lanterns.
- A rack for walking sticks, spears, umbrellas, and things that are ambiguously all four.
- Chalkboard beside the door currently covered in wall-watch notes.

A faded set of height marks runs up one doorframe.

Several have names and dates.

One line marked **LYSA** jumps upward dramatically one year and has:

> “LIAR — BOOTS” 

written beside it in a different hand.

Do not explain who wrote it unless asked.

---

**Family Kitchen**

The emotional center of the house.

- Large scarred wooden table.
- Mismatched chairs.
- Iron stove.
- Drying herbs.
- Kettle always somewhere near boiling.
- Shelves filled with preserved food, chipped crockery, mechanical odds and ends, and objects that migrated here from other rooms years ago.
- Hooks under the table where children once hung bags and tools.
- A large window looking toward the road.

This is where planning naturally happens.

Not because it is a council chamber.

Because this family solves problems at the kitchen table.

---

**Sitting Room**

Small and overfull.

- Patched furniture.
- Family photographs / magical portrait plates.
- Maps of Mireward from several generations.
- Shelves holding books, old repair manuals, civic records, cheap novels, and things Lysandro swears he will return to their rightful owners.
- One wall has framed family objects that are not arranged with any aesthetic discipline whatsoever.

Include:

- An old section of wall-stake.
- A retired patrol whistle.
- One of Lysandro's first precision gears.
- A child's badly made wooden sword.
- A school slate with **Lysandra Ironveil** written with absurdly careful handwriting.

---

**Lysandro's Workshop**

Attached to the rear of the house and accessible from both inside and outside.

This is not the Ironveil Warehouse.

It is his precision shop.

The room smells of:

- Cutting oil.
- Hot metal.
- Wood.
- Brass.
- Tea someone forgot about.

Workbenches are covered with:

- Gear teeth.
- Pressure screws.
- Bell fittings.
- Latches.
- Valve assemblies.
- Springs.
- Half-repaired household objects neighbors dropped off.

Everything appears chaotic.

Lysandro knows exactly where everything is.

Probably.

Useful downtime affordances:

- Repair damaged mundane gear.
- Improvise brackets, locks, braces, signaling devices, or mechanisms.
- Work beside Lysandro.
- Search for tools.
- Ask what a strange piece does and receive a twenty-minute answer.
- Let Baergrom or another mechanically inclined PC make something.
- Build improvements for Mireward.

**No free magical crafting is implied.**

---

**Back Yard**

Small, practical, partially overtaken by emergency supplies.

Features:

- Clothesline.
- Rain barrel.
- Vegetable beds.
- Scrap metal.
- An old practice post scarred by weapons.
- A short section of freestanding timber wall used for testing hinges, brackets, and repair designs.

The practice post has old marks lower down than expected.

Lysandra learned to fight here.

Whether Lysandro, another relative, or Mireward's general culture taught her is **UNRESOLVED**.

---

#### Upper floor

**Parents' Room**

Private unless invited.

No important plot object.

It should remain ordinary.

---

**Calyx's Room**

If Calyx still lives here:

- Half practical young adult.
- Half kid trying very hard to look like he has already left home.
- Guard manuals.
- Clean boots.
- Training gear.
- Drafts of applications to the Mirathorn Guard.
- Several unanswered or returned petitions.

This creates an immediate tension with Lysandra:

Calyx wants the life she chose.

Lysandro is not thrilled.

Lysandra now knows exactly what that life costs.

Do not decide for her how she responds.

---

**Torr's Room / Study**

If Torr resides here, this is the neatest space in the house.

- Ledgers.
- Fine pen nibs.
- Old tithe records.
- Perfectly folded clothes.
- Small repair kit.
- Nothing displayed frivolously.

One drawer contains a framed commendation or notice concerning Lysandra.

**ESTABLISHED HANDLE:** Torr keeps a framed commendation hidden.

**UNRESOLVED:** What exact achievement it commemorates.

If discovered, choose an innocuous early-career achievement rather than secretly inserting a major historical event.

Example:

> “For distinguished service during the East Dock evacuation.”

Only use a specific text if the GM chooses to make it canon.

---

**Lysandra's Old Room**

This room matters.

Nobody converted it into a shrine.

Nobody preserved it untouched.

But nobody really erased her either.

**PREP LOCK**

The room has slowly become a spare room and storage space, but several unmistakably Lysandra things remain.

Current contents:

- A narrow bed.
- Folded spare blankets.
- Boxes of household storage.
- An old desk.
- A cracked washbasin.
- A window looking toward part of the wall.

Still hers:

- Small knife marks measuring distances along the desk edge.
- A faded map of Mireward drawn by hand.
- A cheap wooden practice sword with several careful repairs.
- An old civic-watch handbook filled with notes correcting it.
- A row of pegs where equipment used to hang.
- One badly painted little wooden soldier with its uniform painted the wrong color.

Inside the desk is a stack of childhood and adolescent papers.

Do not fill all of them in advance.

Use them responsively when players investigate.

Possible finds:

- Precise maps.
- Lists of chores.
- Arguments written in margins.
- Early attempts at guard procedure.
- A furious essay about a rule she thought was stupid.
- A very orderly plan for running away.
- A letter she started to someone and never sent.
- Training scores.
- An embarrassing drawing.
- Evidence she was funny before she learned not to show it so readily.

The key tone:

**Lysandra did not emerge fully formed as “the serious guard.”**

The room should contain contradictions.

### What happens when someone realizes this was Lysandra's room?

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:room-ask -->
- **Ask Lysandra about something openly.**

  Consequence: she answers selectively.

  She is more likely to answer concrete questions than “what were you like as a child?”

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:room-snoop -->
- **Snoop.**

  Consequence: there are absolutely embarrassing things available.

  If caught, Lysandra reacts according to the intrusiveness rather than turning it into a dramatic betrayal.

  Bonogo should be allowed to Bonogo.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:room-family -->
- **Ask a relative instead.**

  Consequence: different family members tell different versions.

  Nobody possesses the authoritative interpretation of Lysandra.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:room-leave -->
- **Leave it alone.**

  Consequence: nothing bad happens.

  Respecting privacy is also characterization.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:room-other -->
- **Do something unexpected.**

  Follow it.

### The Kitchen Table

**Situation**

This should be the default location for downtime conversations, planning, the Rocky Talkie call, eating, arguing, and deciding what happens next.

It is materially different from a council meeting.

People can get up.

People can cook.

People can leave halfway through.

Characters can split into side conversations.

Someone can fall asleep at the table.

**Read aloud**

> The table has probably hosted a thousand arguments.
>
> There are knife marks, ink stains, one burned corner, and a repaired split running almost the full length.
>
> Lysandro pushes aside a bowl full of loose springs, puts food where the springs were, puts the springs into somebody's empty cup, and says:
>
> “Right. Anybody bleeding badly enough that we care before breakfast?”

**GM only**

Make the kitchen **interactive**.

Things can happen concurrently:

- Rocky Talkie conversation with Zilla and Grobnok.
- Someone cooks.
- Someone cleans weapons.
- Thrin stares suspiciously at a mechanical egg timer.
- Lysandra falls quiet looking at something familiar.
- Lysandro works on a broken latch while talking.
- A local knocks asking to borrow a tool.
- A runner leaves a note and refuses to interrupt breakfast.
- Players wander into other rooms.

This is an excellent place to ask:

> “While everyone is settling in, where in the house do you end up?”

Then follow them.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:ironveil-family-soap -->
### Everyone Knows Lysa

**Situation**

If family members are present, they know Lysandra in ways the Questionable Company does not.

Use lightly.

The joke should never be “Lysandra's family humiliates her for an hour.”

The useful effect is perspective.

**Lysandro**

Already established.

Calls her **Lysa**.

Teases rank.

Respects her competence.

Can say:

> “She always did like a rule. Didn't have to be a good rule.”

Or:

> “First barricade she ever built blocked our own pantry.”

---

**Merrow — if present**

**PREP interpretation from established handle**

Merrow expresses pride through criticism.

She is not cold.

She is devastatingly maternal.

Possible greeting after the siege:

> “You look terrible.”
>
> Beat.
>
> “Good work.”

Possible line to the party:

> “She was exactly like this at twelve. Smaller. Worse at lying.”

Potential player interaction:

Merrow is willing to tell childhood stories if asked, especially ones Lysandra would prefer be forgotten.

She should also be genuinely interested in the people who have been traveling with her daughter.

---

**Torr — if present**

Quiet.

Minimal fuss.

Maybe he notices a torn strap on Lysandra's armor while everyone is talking.

Without interrupting, he removes it, fixes it, and hands it back.

If she says:

> “Granddad, I need that.”

He says:

> “Then stop breaking it.”

No emotional speech required.

---

**Calyx — if present**

This is the most immediately playable relationship.

He wants into the Mirathorn Guard.

He admires Lysandra.

He may admire the Questionable Company even more.

He wants stories.

He wants practical advice.

He wants to know what the city is really like.

He may ask:

> “Is Commander Ashenvale as terrifying as people say?”

Or:

> “How many times have you actually fought inside the city?”

Or, to a PC:

> “Does she really order you around?”

He creates a difficult question for Lysandra without intending to:

> “Do you think I could make it there?”

Do not prewrite her answer.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:lysandra-alone-at-home -->
### If Someone Finds Lysandra Alone

**Situation**

Use only if a player seeks her out or notices her withdrawing.

Do not force an emotional confession.

Possible locations:

- Her old room.
- Workshop doorway.
- Back yard practice post.
- Kitchen after everyone leaves.
- Front steps.

**Read aloud**

> Lysandra is sitting somewhere you have not seen her sit before.
>
> Not alert.
>
> Not on watch.
>
> Not positioned to see every entrance.
>
> Just sitting.
>
> She notices you.
>
> “What?”

**GM only**

Her being home does not suddenly make her emotionally articulate.

The change is that she has fewer defenses available because everything around her remembers her.

Good questions may get good answers.

Bad questions may get:

> “No.”

Possible subjects if players raise them:

- Why she left.
- Whether she wanted to come back.
- Calyx wanting to follow her.
- Her relationship with her father.
- Her promotion.
- Staying in Mireward.
- Stafl's Suggestion.
- What she wants rather than what the mission requires.

Do not turn this into mandatory trauma processing.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:home-as-base -->
### Use the House as the Downtime Menu

When the party reaches the open portion of Session 29, frame choices spatially instead of listing quests.

Example:

> The Ironveil house settles into a strange temporary rhythm.
>
> Lysandro is in the workshop.
>
> There are people coming and going from the kitchen with news from the wall.
>
> The Rocky Talkie is sitting on the table.
>
> Thrin has found the back yard and is staring at the vegetable beds like they have personally offended him.
>
> Lysandra disappeared upstairs a few minutes ago.
>
> From outside, you can hear reconstruction already starting.
>
> You finally have time.
>
> Where do you go?

Then let player choices activate:

- **Workshop** → Mireward recovery / crafting / Baergrom / Lysandro.
- **Kitchen / Rocky Talkie** → Mirathorn call.
- **Back yard / Thrin** → Witness Seed.
- **Upstairs / Lysandra** → family or personal material.
- **Town** → refugees / reconstruction / investigation.
- **Bed** → actual rest.
- **Somewhere else** → follow them.

This replaces an abstract downtime menu with a physical place the players can move through.

## House Tone

The Ironveil house should communicate three things without exposition.

### 1. Lysandra came from people who care deeply about responsibility

This explains part of her.

### 2. She chose hierarchy more strongly than the family around her did

This explains part of the conflict with Lysandro.

### 3. She was a person before she became the version the party met

This gives the players something new to discover.

The house is not here to reveal a secret tragedy.

It is here to reveal **ordinary history**.

That is more useful right now.
