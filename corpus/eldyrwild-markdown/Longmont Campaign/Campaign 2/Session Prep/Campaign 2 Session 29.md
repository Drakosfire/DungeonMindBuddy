# Campaign 2 — Session 29

## Session Intent

Session 29 is about **giving the table ownership again**.

The last several sessions have been dominated by a long, escalating siege. The immediate threat should end quickly. What follows is not another chain of emergencies. Mireward has survived long enough for the Questionable Company to decide what survival means.

The players should have opportunities to:

- Save the remaining people endangered by the attack.
- See concrete evidence that their earlier choices mattered.
- Help Mireward become capable of surviving without them.
- Reconnect with Mirathorn and receive genuinely good news.
- Learn that Thrin is changing.
- Pursue personal interests.
- Decide what problem they actually want to solve next.

Do **not** run every optional Beat. Offer them and follow player interest.

By the end of the session, the party should have changed the world in several small ways and chosen the shape of the next section of the campaign.

- Thrin has heard a psychic presence that wants him and somehow wants the migrating forest.

Of the 55 refugees:

## The Last Hands of the Siege

**Objective:** End the immediate crisis fast and show that the battle is actually ending.

**Pressure:** Two transformed refugees are escaping with sleeping victims.

**Role:** Spine.

This should feel like the last thirty seconds of a disaster, not the first round of another encounter.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:warehouse-tail -->
### Something Is Still Moving

**Situation**

The main assault has broken.

Smoke, churned mud, broken wall timbers, burned flesh, and exhausted townsfolk fill the Ironveil Warehouse yard.

Two transformed refugees remain active. Each is dragging an unconscious victim away from the warehouse and toward broken ground or a tunnel route.

**Read aloud**

> For the first time in what feels like hours, nothing new is crawling out of the ground.
>
> The quiet almost lands.
>
> Then someone screams.
>
> Beyond the smoke, two of the things that used to be refugees are moving away from the warehouse. Not charging. Not attacking.
>
> Leaving.
>
> Each has someone limp in its arms.

**Do now**

Ask:

> “They are getting away with people. What do you do?”

Do not immediately roll initiative unless the players choose direct combat and initiative would clarify resolution.

Reasonable actions include:

- Attack the transformed refugees.
- Cut off their escape.
- Rescue the sleepers instead of pursuing the creatures.
- Collapse or block the route ahead.
- Organize townsfolk into a rescue line.
- Follow them to discover where they are going.
- Use magic to separate carrier from victim.
- Let one go deliberately and track it.
- Something stranger.

**GM only**

- No reinforcements arrive.
- Do not inflate the creatures’ HP to extend the scene.
- Their objective is **escape with victims**, not defeating the party.
- A successful player idea can end this immediately.
- If one escapes, that is a consequence, not failure of the runbook.

**Relevant**

NPCs: Lysandra Ironveil, Lysandro Ironveil, Brin Holloway, surviving refugees, Mireward defenders.

Location: Ironveil Warehouse yard / collapsed western wall.

Threat: transformed refugee creatures.

**Missing reference**

No specific statblock/ID is locked for these final transformed refugees in this runbook. Use the creature reference already used for the transformations in Session 28. Do not generate a new statblock merely for this scene.

## The Rock Finally Brings Good News

**Objective:** Reverse the assumption that Mirathorn is collapsing and turn the city back into a source of support.

**Pressure:** Mireward has problems Mirathorn may now know how to solve.

**Role:** Optional.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:rocky-talkie -->
### “Oh Shit, the Rock”

**Situation**

Someone calls Grobnok.

Someone else answers.

It is Professor Zilla Cinderbranch.

There is obvious fighting or violent cleanup happening nearby, but she insists the city is no longer losing control.

**Read aloud**

> The rock answers into scraping metal, shouting, and something heavy smashing against stone.
>
> A distant voice:
>
> “Oh shit, the rock. Shit. Gotta answer the rock.”
>
> Something clatters.
>
> Then a breathless gnome voice comes clearly through.
>
> “Cinderbranch. Hello? Yes. I can hear you.”
>
> From farther away:
>
> “Professor! Left!”
>
> “I see him, Grobnok. Keep him upright.”
>
> There is a long hiss, a crash, and several people coughing.
>
> Zilla returns to the stone.
>
> “Despite how this sounds, things are actually quite under control.”

Continue naturally.

Zilla explains:

- They discovered the cure potion can be aerosolized.
- Teams are rounding up infected people and dosing them.
- The cure effort is working.
- Mirathorn remains damaged and busy, but this is containment and cleanup rather than uncontrolled collapse.
- Grobnok and Zilla have effectively become operational liaisons during the crisis.

> “Tealeaf’s potion works as a mist. We do not have to persuade every glassy-eyed idiot in Mirathorn to drink something anymore.”
>
> “We’re moving block by block.”
>
> “You do not need to turn around and rescue us.”
>
> “Tell me what is happening where you are.”

Optional Grobnok beat:

> “You are distractingly good at this, Professor.”
>
> “Then stop standing in front of the nozzle.”
>
> A pause.
>
> Grobnok laughs.
>
> Zilla does too, despite herself.

**GM only**

**PREP LOCK:** Mirathorn has genuinely gained control.

The soundscape should initially make players think the opposite.

The payoff is relief.

Do not immediately attach a new emergency request to the good news.

Professor Tealeaf can be brought onto the communication if the party needs technical cure information.

## Thrin Hears Too Much

**Objective:** Turn Thrin from repeated rescue target into a character with a changing relationship to the party and the forest.

**Pressure:** Psychic exposure has changed how the Witness Seed communicates.

**Role:** Optional.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:thrin-first-echo -->
### The Thought He Answers

**Situation**

During a quiet interaction, Thrin responds to something nobody said aloud.

Do not choose an intimate secret for a PC.

Give the player control over what leaked.

**Read aloud**

> Thrin is staring at nothing again.
>
> His hand is pressed over the strange place beneath his collarbone.
>
> Then he looks directly at you.
>
> “No.”
>
> A pause.
>
> “Not… no to words.”
>
> He frowns.
>
> “No to thing you thought.”

Ask that player:

> “What harmless thought, image, memory, or feeling might Thrin have accidentally caught?”

If the player does not want to supply one, default to something surface-level from the scene:

- Exhaustion.
- Hunger.
- Worry about the sleepers.
- Wanting a bath.
- Anger at the monsters.
- Wanting to leave.

**GM only**

**PREP LOCK:** The psychic attacks have disturbed the boundary between Thrin and the Witness Seed.

**PROPOSED MECHANIC — Surface Thought Echo**

Thrin can sometimes receive:

- A strong image.
- An immediate intention.
- A dominant emotion.
- A deliberately projected simple thought.

He does **not** automatically receive:

- Full sentences.
- Private history.
- Hidden memories.
- Objective truth.
- Reliable lies.
- Perfect identity or context.

Treat this as communication, not Detect Thoughts.

If used repeatedly under stress, he becomes overwhelmed rather than omniscient.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=choice id=choice:respond-thrin scene=scene:thrin-first-echo -->
### How do they respond to the new connection?

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:thrin-test activates=scene:seed-meaning -->
- **Deliberately test it.**

  Consequence: a PC can intentionally send an image or concept.

  Later effect: Thrin becomes easier to communicate with despite language barriers.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:thrin-boundaries -->
- **Help Thrin establish boundaries.**

  Consequence: focus on “ask before listening,” interruption, grounding, or how to tell his own thoughts from others.

  Later effect: increases trust and makes the ability less frightening.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:thrin-forest activates=scene:seed-meaning -->
- **Ask about the seed and the forest.**

  Consequence: Thrin tries to explain what little he understands.

  Later effect: opens Witness Seed lore without requiring a full exposition dump.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:thrin-fear -->
- **Treat the ability as dangerous.**

  Consequence: Thrin withdraws and attempts not to use it.

  This is not punished.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:thrin-other -->
- **Respond another way.**

  Follow the relationship.

### What the Witness Seed Wants

**Situation**

Only use this if the players pursue the subject.

Thrin does not possess a clean explanation.

**GM-only truth**

**ESTABLISHED/DEEPER WORLD LORE**

The Witness Seed is a living Fey construct tied to memory, continuity, thresholds, and eventual growth into a new forest node.

**PREP LOCK**

The deeper purpose in Thrin’s case is larger:

Thrin was chosen to wander beyond the existing migrating forest, eventually find a place where another forest could begin, and act as the living bridge through which that new forest learns how to exist.

Thrin does not understand this.

Thrin does not want this.

The forest does not understand why he does not want this.

He and the forest are effectively talking past one another.

His incomplete synchronization is one reason the deeper forest/druidic abilities have not fully manifested.

**Do not tell the players all of this automatically.**

Fragments Thrin might express:

> “Seed remembers roads.”
>
> “Forest says leave. Then angry when I leave.”
>
> “Seed wants… place?”
>
> “New trees. Maybe.”
>
> “I don’t want become tree.”
>
> “I don’t know if that is what it means.”

**UNRESOLVED**

- Does Thrin eventually accept the role?
- Can he redefine it?
- Can the seed become something else?
- Are his future druidic powers unlocked by obedience, understanding, refusal, or a new relationship?
- Is the psychic presence pursuing him specifically trying to hijack this future?

Do not answer until play earns it.

## What Is the Questionable Company For Now?

**Objective:** Let the party define the next campaign direction.

**Pressure:** The wider threat still exists, but no single destination is mandatory.

**Role:** Spine.

Do not run this Beat until players have had meaningful downtime choices.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:lysandra-stays -->
### Lysandra Chooses Mireward

**Situation**

Lysandra has concluded that Mireward needs her more than the Questionable Company does.

This is a chance to let her exist independently of the party.

**Read aloud**

> Eventually Lysandra finds all of you—or enough of you to make the point.
>
> Her armor is filthy. She has a fresh strip of cloth tied around one forearm. Behind her, two townsfolk are arguing over lumber and somebody is shouting for more nails.
>
> “I’m staying.”
>
> She waits for the obvious objection.
>
> “They need somebody who knows what these things do. Somebody who can organize the next few days instead of the next thirty seconds.”
>
> She looks back toward the damaged wall.
>

>
> “But I’m staying.”
>
> A beat.
>

**GM only**

This is not Lysandra abandoning the party.

It is her choosing responsibility.

Her existing relationships with Nera, Salla, Lysandro, and Mireward’s local systems let her contribute without becoming mayor or taking over the town.

Her presence makes leaving Mireward a credible choice.

If players strongly want her to accompany them, hear the argument.

Her default remains staying.

## Something Beneath the Route

**Objective:** Keep the underground intelligence available as an adaptable pressure without forcing another boss fight immediately after the siege.

**Pressure:** Something coordinated the burrowing assault and showed sustained interest in Thrin.

**Role:** Optional.

**PREP STATUS:** Proposed direction.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:meat-mind-wake -->
### The Meat Mind’s Wake

**Situation**

Use when players:

- Track a fleeing transformed refugee.
- Investigate tunnels.
- Travel away from Mireward and you want the underground threat to cross their route.
- Deliberately hunt the source of the psychic pressure.

They first find **evidence**, not necessarily the creature.

Possible evidence:

- Multiple tunnels converging and then diverging as if routes were being assigned.
- Flesh pressed flat against tunnel walls in deliberate patterns.
- A patch where every root points the same direction.
- Psychic pressure that Thrin notices before anyone else.
- Drag marks from abducted people.
- Burrowers that abandoned useful attack positions simultaneously.
- A chamber that was occupied recently but is now empty.

**Read aloud**

> The tunnel changes.
>
> Until now it has looked like something dug through the earth.
>
> Here, it looks like something **thought** through it.

**GM only**

**UNRESOLVED**

- Is the Meat Mind itself the intelligence Thrin heard?
- Is it a relay for Maelthor?
- Is it a siege-organism built for command?
- Does it want the Witness Seed for itself?
- Does it understand what the Witness Seed actually is?

Do not answer until useful.

Its location may remain flexible until the players establish it through observation.

Once observed, lock the location.

**Missing reference**

No authoritative Meat Mind statblock/ID is attached to this runbook.

If players force a direct combat before a statblock is added:

- Use an existing approved reference if one exists elsewhere in the project.
- Otherwise treat discovery as the scene climax and prepare the combat before resolving it.
- Do not silently invent a boss statblock inside PLAY.

## Shimmering Rain

**Objective:** Keep the storms strange and hazardous without turning every exposure into an infection scare.

**Pressure:** Weather complicates travel.

**Role:** Interrupt.

Only use if the party travels and the session needs environmental pressure.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=scene id=scene:shimmering-storm -->
### The Failed Rain

**Situation**

Another magical storm moves over the Reach.

Previous storms have produced:

- Shimmering rain.
- Savory smell.
- Strange planar/conjuration effects.
- Delayed reflections.
- Heavy hail.

Use one or two features.

Do not stack all of them every time.

**Read aloud**

> Rain begins as a fine silver sheen across the road.
>
> The first drops smell faintly wrong—less like wet earth and more like broth left too long over a fire.
>
> In the nearest puddle, your reflection moves a heartbeat after you do.

**GM only**

**PREP LOCK**

The storms are an early Maelthor strategy for distributing meat spores.

It largely failed.

The rain dilutes the material too heavily for ordinary exposure to reliably cause transformation.

The storm is currently:

- Bad weather.
- Planar contamination.
- A transport mechanism that did not work as intended.

It is **not** a hidden infection save.

Do not make players paranoid about every raindrop unless later campaign developments change the mechanism.

Useful travel hazards:

- Slick road.
- Flooded low ground.
- Hail.
- Reduced visibility.
- Frightened animals.
- Washed-away tracks.
- Difficult shelter decisions.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=choice id=choice:storm-response scene=scene:shimmering-storm -->
### How do they use or endure the storm?

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:storm-shelter -->
- **Shelter and wait.**

  Consequence: lose travel time, preserve safety.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:storm-push -->
- **Push through.**

  Consequence: maintain time at the cost of environmental difficulty.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:storm-cover -->
- **Use the storm as cover.**

  Consequence: stealth or pursuit may become easier while navigation becomes harder.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:storm-sample -->
- **Collect and study samples.**

  Consequence: careful analysis can eventually reveal that the contaminant is heavily diluted.

  This may be the first clue that the storm tactic itself is ineffective.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v2 kind=option id=option:storm-other -->
- **Use magic, engineering, or another plan.**

  Resolve naturally.

# GM Running Notes

## Protect the Change in Tempo

The main danger of this session is accidentally rebuilding the slog.

Avoid:

- Another surprise wave.
- A new mandatory council meeting.
- Six NPCs each assigning a quest.
- “Good news, but actually…” reversals.
- Punishing the party for taking a rest.
- Treating staying in Mireward as wasted time.
- Treating leaving Mireward as abandoning helpless people.

Mireward is hurt.

Mireward is also capable.

Mirathorn is hurt.

Mirathorn is also recovering.

The party has earned a moment in which their choices define the pressure instead of pressure defining their choices.

---

## Player Participation Target

Before `choice:next-direction`, try to make sure every player has done at least one of:

- Made a decision that changes Mireward.
- Had a personal conversation.
- Pursued a character interest.
- Learned something they chose to investigate.
- Created an Asset.
- Sent or received a meaningful message.
- Changed their relationship with Thrin.
- Chosen rest deliberately.

If someone has mostly watched other scenes, point the spotlight toward them before the final group decision.

---

## Likely Session End States

Any of these are successful endings.

**Mireward Recovery**

The party stays and begins turning the damaged settlement into a defensible refuge.

**Northbound**

The party leaves for Edge/Fen with Thrin while Lysandra remains behind.

**Into the Earth**

The party chooses the burrow network and begins hunting the intelligence underneath the siege.

**Southbound**

The party returns toward Mirathorn for reasons they chose themselves.

**Unexpected**

The party decides the interesting problem is something else.

Preserve it.

The Session 29 success condition is not a destination.

It is:

**The players should feel that the next part of the campaign exists because of decisions they made during this session.**
