## Scenes / beats

- Opening Scene
  > [!READ-ALOUD]
  > The bells and noise of [Mireward Reach](#dmb-ref:graph-node:loc_4) feel far away compared to the grinding beneath your feet.
  > 
  > For a moment, the wall stills. The monsters that dragged Thrin under are gone from sight, swallowed by the earth they came from. Dust hangs in the air in a pale curtain, settling over shattered stone and churned mud. Somewhere below, something shifts with a wet, heavy scrape — not gone, only hidden.
  > 
  > Then the ground gives a low, distant groan.
  > 
  > It starts as a vibration in your boots, almost easy to miss if you’re not listening for it. A slow tremor runs through the wall, through the packed earth at its base, through every loose stone left standing from the first impact. The rumble grows a little louder, a little deeper, like some vast thing turning over beneath the town and finding purchase again.
  > 
  > There’s just enough silence for the fear to settle in.

    - **Split the party.**  
    This is probably the most likely “smart panic” move. If they do, the consequence beat is not “they succeed or fail,” but “they solve two problems badly instead of one well.”
    - **Send a messenger / command the guards.**  
    They might try to delegate: hold the line here, send aid west, or warn the warehouse. This is a good way to keep the players in a tactical role while you advance the situation elsewhere.
    - **Try to collapse or seal the breach.**  
    If they think underground movement is the real threat, they may try to block tunnels, brace the wall, or keep the monsters from surfacing again. That’s a strong proactive beat.
    - **Focus on extraction / triage instead of combat.**  
    They might prioritize wounded, civilians, or the surviving PCs/NPCs over killing the hybrids. That can shift the scene from battle to disaster response without losing tension.
    - **Pursue information first.**  
    They may hesitate and use divination, perception, or a quick survey to understand whether the rumbling is localized or widespread. That creates a “reveal beat” before the next escalation.

    > [!GM-NOTE]
    > - The latchlings are not trying to win a straight battle at the wall. Their goal is to create chaos, isolate targets, and drag prey into the breach or any opening they can exploit.
