# Travel Dilemma d12

**Use:** Roll once per march day after the road encounter. Each row should become a choice, not a punishment roll. The party may solve, defer, exploit, ignore, or make it worse.

**Tone target:** About 4 benign/neutral, 5 pressure, 3 ominous. No row auto-starts combat.

**Row shape:** `{situation headline} · {2–3 player choices} · {consequence if ignored}`

**Repeat rule:** If a row repeats, reuse the theme but advance the world: more witnesses, worse weather, bolder signs, hungrier refugees, clearer supply rumors, or stranger static.

| d12 | Dilemma |
|-----|---------|
| 1 | **Lantern-cart in the mud** · A family hauling shuttered blue lanterns for a roadside shrine has sunk axle-deep. The party can spend time freeing them, pay a road crew, or press on and leave directions to the next hamlet. · If ignored, the family arrives later soaked and resentful, carrying useful gossip the party now hears secondhand. |
| 2 | **Refugees who do not want escort** · Two frightened travelers ask to walk near the party but refuse to say which town they fled. The party can welcome them, question them before allowing it, or send them toward a safer convoy. · If ignored, they follow at a distance and become easy prey for rumor, weather, or road cruelty. |
| 3 | **A child with a wrong lunch** · A child offers to trade dried meat for a song, a feather, or one of Bonogo’s pocket scraps. The smell is almost ordinary, almost savory rain. The party can inspect it, refuse gently, or ask where it came from. · If ignored, the meat vanishes into the road crowd and the next rumor arrives without proof. |
| 4 | **Shelter before dignity** · Sideways rain closes fast; the only dry structure is a cramped toll shed already claimed by strangers. The party can negotiate shared shelter, assert official priority, or camp outside and preserve goodwill. · If ignored, everyone gets wet and tired, but the strangers remember whether the heroes looked away. |
| 5 | **The stuck supply wagon** · A wagon marked for Mireward’s tithe barn has a broken wheel and sacks going damp. The party can repair it, redistribute supplies among travelers, or leave a marker for the next patrol. · If ignored, those supplies fail to arrive before the gate crush, making the arrival scene harsher. |
| 6 | **Lightning on the exposed ridge** · The road climbs high as static rises in hair, horns, badges, and wet bowstrings. The party can push fast, descend into longer mud, or let Thrin pick an ugly animal path. · If ignored, the storm chooses the route: loud, frightening, and visible to anyone watching the road. |
| 7 | **Retry Mirathorn in the lull** · For ten minutes, the static drops and the rockie-talkie sounds almost clean. The party can call Mirathorn now, wait for Lysandra to try alone, or save the chance for an emergency. · If ignored, the next static burst carries a half-voice that could be Frank, Sara, or only thunder pretending. |
| 8 | **A rider south** · A courier with a blown horse offers to carry one sealed message toward Mossford or Mirathorn, no guarantees. The party can send warning, send comfort, send nothing, or send a deliberately bland note to test whether it arrives unchanged. · If ignored, the south remains silent by choice as much as by weather. |
| 9 | **Pamphlets under a mile-stone** · Rain exposes oilcloth packets stuffed beneath a crown marker: polished slogans about order, safety, proper neighbors, and a shepherd’s crook worked into the border. The party can collect evidence, destroy them, mark the place, or leave them to see who comes. · If ignored, the next travelers may find them first and carry the language north or south. |
| 10 | **Ransacked-town rumor** · A limping tanner says two nights back a hamlet was stripped: animals gone, smokehouses emptied, doors painted with white hooks. The party can detour to verify, press toward Mireward, or send the rumor back down-road. · If ignored, the rumor reaches Mireward before the party does, swollen and less useful. |

**Row 10 grounding (read before play):** The **tanner** is a **road NPC in this row only** — no dossier, no fixed map pin. The **hamlet is unnamed** until table play. If the party **detours to verify**, use runbook **§9 Tollers Gap** kit (mile-marker seven, tithe apron to grass) **or** improvise a empty waystation from **R5 §31–40** texture — then **name it in Session 22 recap**. Do **not** treat Tollers Gap as pre-selected; the tanner's story and a road-table abandoned beat are separate channels until the party commits. If they **press north**, the tanner is last seen limping on; Mireward may echo **white hooks** as gossip only.
| 11 | **The meat cart with no flies** · A cheerful peddler’s covered cart smells of salt, smoke, and something too clean. No bugs land on it despite the wet heat. The party can inspect, buy a sample, warn travelers away, or shadow the cart without confrontation. · If ignored, the cart continues toward crowded mouths and the party keeps only suspicion. |
| 12 | **The chalk sign north** · A fresh chalk shepherd’s crook points toward a side road while an older refugee mark points away from it. The party can follow the crook, follow the refugee mark, split scouts briefly, or erase both and trust the crown road. · If ignored, the marks remain for the next desperate family, and the party loses the chance to learn who is steering whom. |

## Table notes

- Shepherd’s Flock contact is possible but not guaranteed: these rows use pamphlets, rumors, symbols, meat logistics, and social pressure rather than sermons or confirmed exposition.
- Combat can occur only if player choices escalate toward confrontation or pursuit.
- Gate-specific crowd choices belong in `mireward_gate_dilemma_d6.md`.
