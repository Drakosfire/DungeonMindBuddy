## Threnn-of-Second-Bloom

Branchbound Defector, Seed-Bearer

### Identity & Role

**Name:** Threnn-of-Second-Bloom
 **Age:** ~34 years (young by Branchbound standards; emotionally older than they should be)
 **Ancestry:** Half-elf (Branchbound lineage, canopy-born)
 **Class Frame:** Ranger (Fey-Touched Skirmisher)
 **Level:** 5

Threnn is not a rebel in the dramatic sense. They did not argue doctrine or challenge elders. They simply **failed to settle**.

Branchbound culture prizes impermanence, but it expects acceptance of that impermanence. Threnn never learned to let go cleanly. They remember faces too long. They return to the same vantage points. They check on things that should already have moved on.

That makes them unreliable.

That makes them dangerous.

### Visual Description

Threnn is slim and long-limbed, built for climbing rather than running. Their skin carries a faint green-brown undertone, as if the forest’s light never fully left it. Barklike scar tissue traces one collarbone and creeps faintly toward the sternum—easy to miss unless bare skin is visible.

Their hair is ash-blond, worn cropped short on one side and longer on the other, usually braided back with twine and seed-beads. Their eyes are pale amber, reflective in low light, and unsettlingly steady when they fix on someone.

They dress in layered canopy leathers: soft moss-dyed greens and greys, reinforced at knees and forearms. Their cloak is stitched from overlapping leaf-pattern panels that break up their outline when still. A simple antler toggle holds it closed.

When stressed, faint bark-veins rise beneath the skin near their neck and shoulder, like something pressing outward from within.

They smell faintly of sap and rain, even when dry.

### Where They Live (or Lived)

Threnn lived in **Second Bloom**, a semi-permanent Branchbound canopy cluster built around a seasonal flowering giant. The platform structures were meant to last no more than a decade.

Second Bloom is overdue to be abandoned.

Threnn stayed anyway.

They were a **Root-Tender**—someone tasked with maintaining traversal routes, inspecting living bridges, and pruning growth that threatened collapse. That role requires attention, memory, and care.

It also requires leaving when told.

Threnn did not.

### Relationships in the Branchbound

**Elder Saelthorn:
** The elder who approved Threnn’s implantation with a Witness Seed. Calm, patient, convinced Threnn’s “stickiness” was a solvable flaw.

**Ilyra-of-Falling-Leaves:
** Threnn’s former partner. Ilyra embraced migration fully and left Second Bloom years ago. She believes Threnn is already lost and refuses to speak of them.

**Kesh the Bridge-Knower:
** A mentor and quiet sympathizer. Kesh taught Threnn how to listen to roots. He warned them once, privately, to leave before the forest decided for them.

Kesh will notice Threnn’s absence.

### Personality

Threnn is:

Quiet, observant, slow to volunteer opinions

Intensely protective once they commit

Uncomfortable with authority but respectful of competence

Afraid—not of death, but of finishing incorrectly

They apologize too often for taking up space.

They do not apologize for choosing.

### Mechanical Stat Block (Level 5 Companion)

This is intentionally simple and robust. Threnn should be useful without overshadowing PCs.

**AC:** 15 (studded leather + Dex)
 **HP:** 44 (5d10 + Con)
 **Speed:** 35 ft, climb 35 ft

**STR** 10
 **DEX** 16
 **CON** 14
 **INT** 11
 **WIS** 15
 **CHA** 9

**Saving Throws:** Dex, Wis
 **Skills:** Stealth, Perception, Survival, Athletics
 **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft

### Combat Abilities

**Multiattack:
** Two attacks with leafblade shortsword or longbow.

**Leafblade Shortsword:
** 1d6 + Dex slashing
 Once per turn, if attacking a creature adjacent to foliage, roots, or natural terrain, deal +1d4 damage.

**Longbow:
** 1d8 + Dex piercing
 If attacking from partial or full concealment, first hit each combat has advantage.

### Special Abilities

**Canopy Step (Recharge 4–6):
** As a bonus action, Threnn moves up to 20 ft without provoking opportunity attacks, provided they start or end adjacent to natural terrain, roots, or trees.

**Root-Sense:
** Advantage on Perception checks to detect movement through natural terrain. Cannot be surprised by creatures moving through plants, roots, or canopy.

**Witness Seed (Passive):
** Threnn is affected by the Witness Seed at all times. Effects are detailed in Document II.

### Death Condition (Important)

If Threnn dies:

The Witness Seed **cannot** be removed safely.

Within minutes, roots begin to emerge from the body or the ground beneath it.

The site becomes marked.

# Encounter Beats

## 1. Threnn as Translator of Forest Intent (Low Stakes, High Trust)

### Hex Fit: The Canopy Paths (3), The Quiet Canopy (10)

## Encounter: The Almost-Wrong Step

The party approaches what looks like an obvious continuation of the path. Threnn hesitates, frowns, and gently touches a root with two fingers. He shakes his head.

“Looks right. Is wrong-right.”

If the party asks how he knows, he struggles to explain:

“Roots… tense. Like held breath.”

If they listen and follow his reroute, nothing dramatic happens—except later they avoid one random encounter or bypass a predator presence entirely. If they ignore him, they don’t die, but they burn resources or time.

**Why this works:
** Threnn isn’t heroic. He’s quietly correct. Players learn that trusting him saves friction, not that he “wins scenes.”

## 2. Threnn as Cultural Interpreter (Endearing, Not Expository)

### Hex Fit: The Root Argument (1), Moss Memory Ring (6)

## Encounter: The Wrong Question

At the Root Argument or Moss Memory Ring, one PC asks something totally reasonable like “What is this place?” or “Who built this?”

The forest responds with subtle pressure: sound distortion, tightening roots, emotional discomfort.

Threnn winces slightly.

“That question… pulls.”
 “Better ask… ‘May we pass?’”

If the party rephrases or follows his lead, the pressure eases. Threnn relaxes visibly. Later, he admits:

“Elders say words leave footprints.”
 “Yours were heavy. I helped make them light.”

**Why this works:
** He’s not lecturing. He’s protecting the party from a social mistake they didn’t know they were making.

## 3. Threnn as Someone Who Notices the Small, Living Things

### Hex Fit: Wet Root Lattice (8), Deadfall Cluster (9)

## Encounter: The Non-Combat Save

The party encounters a minor hazard: a trapped dinosaur hatchling, a creature tangled in roots, or something wounded but not immediately hostile.

Before anyone rolls initiative, Threnn moves instinctively. He cuts a vine, shifts a branch, or places himself between the party and a panicking animal.

“Wait. Not threat. Just… stuck.”

If allowed to act, he resolves the situation quietly. No XP. No loot. Just a moment where violence would have been easier.

Afterward, if asked why he intervened:

“Forest remembers how you end things.”

This echoes the ankylosaur scene without repeating it.

**Why this works:
** The players already showed this instinct. Threnn mirrors it, reinforcing alignment rather than introducing a new value.

## 4. Threnn as Social Cost Absorber (He Takes the Risk, Not Them)

### Hex Fit: Broken Crossing (11)

## Encounter: The Decision to Be Seen

The party reaches the Broken Crossing. Repairing it would help Mossford later, but risks attention.

Before the party can spiral, Threnn offers:

“I can touch it.”
 “If forest notices, it notices me.”

Mechanically, Threnn takes the “attention” consequence. Narratively, this advances the Witness Seed clock by one step instead of triggering immediate Branchbound or forest escalation.

Later, if the party asks why he volunteered:

“I already count wrong.”
 “You… still maybe count right.”

**Why this works:
** He protects the party by spending his own future, and he doesn’t dramatize it.

## 5. Threnn’s Pidgin as Emotional Honesty (Not a Gimmick)

### Hex Fit: Camp anywhere, especially after Moss Memory Ring (6)

## Encounter: The Sentence He Can’t Quite Say

During a rest, a PC thanks Threnn directly or says something like “You’re good to have around.”

He pauses for a long time, clearly assembling words.

“I… am useful.”
 (beat)
 “I am glad. Also.”

That’s it. He doesn’t elaborate.

Later, if the party is in danger and someone is hurt, Threnn suddenly speaks clearly, grammar perfect for once:

“Don’t die here.”

Then he goes back to pidgin.

**Why this works:
** The players will notice the contrast. His broken speech becomes associated with restraint, not incompetence.

## 6. The First Hint of the Seed (Subtle, Non-Threatening)

### Hex Fit: Storm-Scar Canopy (7), Edgeward Drift (12)

## Encounter: The Wrong Reaction

A storm residue effect or wild magic surge happens nearby. Everyone reacts normally.

Threnn does not.

He stiffens. His hand goes to his collarbone. The bark-veins show faintly. He says nothing unless asked.

If pressed:

“Seed listens hard, here.”
 “Doesn’t like storms. Or edges.”

He changes the subject immediately.

**Why this works:
** You’re foreshadowing danger without making it urgent. The players clock it, but they’re not forced to act yet.

## 7. The Hook That Seals It (The Bait, Clean and Honest)

### Timing: Right before the swampward push

## Encounter: The Question That Matters

As the forest thins (Edgeward Drift), Threnn finally asks directly:

“If I walk with you… past trees.”
 “You stop me if I change?”

(struggling for words)
 “Not kill. Not leave. Just… stop.”

He is asking for **accountability**, not protection.

If the party agrees, he nods, visibly relieved.

“Then I come.”
