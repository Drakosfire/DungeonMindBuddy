# Branchbound Encounter Seeds & Elder Sign

This document formalizes **six encounter seeds** that only resolve if players accept **indirect help**, followed by the design of a single Branchbound elder whose presence quietly predicts collapse.

These are not puzzles to solve. They are pressure tests. Players succeed by adapting, not by asking.

## Six Encounter Seeds That Only Work Through Indirect Help

Each seed is structured for table use:

**Surface Situation**: What the players think is happening

**Branchbound Action**: What help looks like

**Failure Mode**: What happens if players push for clarity, leadership, or explanation

**Resolution Condition**: What the players must notice or do, not be told

### 1) The Ladder That Isn’t There

**Surface Situation**
A storm has torn open a gap in the canopy. No bridge remains. A Branchbound scout watches from above.

**Branchbound Action**
The scout drops three lengths of vine at different heights. They are not tied together. The scout leaves immediately.

**Failure Mode**
If the party calls out, asks for help, or waits for instruction:

Wind strengthens

The vines sway apart

The scout does not return

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Tie the vines themselves

Choose the order without guidance

Move vertically without complaint

If successful, bark scuffs reveal a fourth vine could have been dropped. It wasn’t needed.

### 2) Food That Spoils Faster Than Expected

**Surface Situation**
A shared canopy meal. Food is abundant, fresh, and generously offered.

**Branchbound Action**
Branchbound eat lightly and stop halfway through. No explanation is given.

**Failure Mode**
If players ask why, suggest storage, or try to preserve leftovers:

Branchbound lose appetite entirely

Food is abandoned

No further meals are shared

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Eat only what they need

Let the rest be scattered or rot

Not comment on waste

Later, scavengers appear exactly where food was discarded.

### 3) The Path That Circles Back

**Surface Situation**
A Branchbound child leads the party along a winding canopy route that loops back to the same clearing.

**Branchbound Action**
The child says nothing. On the second loop, they leave behind a broken knot-marker and depart.

**Failure Mode**
If the party confronts the child or demands a real path:

The child vanishes

The clearing becomes disorienting

Navigation attempts worsen

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Stop following

Choose a new direction

Treat the broken knot as a warning, not a guide

The loop was deliberate. The original path became unsafe.

### 4) Storm Warnings Without Words

**Surface Situation**
A thunderhead forms. Branchbound begin dismantling platforms and loosening ropes.

**Branchbound Action**
One Branchbound cuts a rope the party believes is load-bearing.

**Failure Mode**
If players protest, repair, or argue:

The storm hits early

The repaired structure fails

Someone is injured

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Trust the dismantling

Follow the movement

Abandon a defensible position willingly

After the storm, the cut rope is found reused elsewhere.

### 5) The Gift That Is Too Useful

**Surface Situation**
A perfectly balanced glider-sling is left near the party’s gear.

**Branchbound Action**
It is left without comment. Eye contact is avoided.

**Failure Mode**
If players thank the Branchbound, ask who made it, or request more:

The item is reclaimed silently overnight

Trust degrades

No further gear appears

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Use it once

Improve it slightly

Leave it behind themselves

Later, the same item is seen in use, modified again.

### 6) The Night Fire That Goes Out

**Surface Situation**
The party lights a careful fire for warmth. Branchbound keep their distance.

**Branchbound Action**
One Branchbound quietly spreads damp moss near the fire before leaving.

**Failure Mode**
If players insist the fire is safe or necessary:

Branchbound relocate entirely

The party wakes alone

Tracks vanish

**Resolution Condition**
The party must:

Let the fire die early

Sit with the cold

Not replace it

In the morning, old burn scars are visible far from camp.

## Branchbound Elder: Ash-in-the-Canopy

This elder does not warn. Their presence predicts collapse.

### Identity

**Name:** Ash-in-the-Canopy
**Age:** Indeterminate
**Role:** None acknowledged

Ash is never introduced. Someone simply says, “Ash is here.”

### Physical Cues

Moves slower than others

Always arrives last, leaves first

Carries nothing not found that day

Breath rasps faintly when storms approach

No one helps Ash unless they fall. They have not yet.

### Behavioral Pattern

When Ash appears:

Camps move sooner

Platforms are dismantled earlier

Arguments end faster

Festivals shorten

No explanation is given. Younger Branchbound adjust instinctively.

### The Quiet Prediction

Ash only appears when:

A migration route is about to fail

A storm will permanently alter the canopy

A long-used perch must be abandoned forever

Ash does not say this. They simply arrive.

### The One Interaction Rule

If directly asked what is coming, Ash gives **one sentence only**, chosen by the GM:

“You’re standing where we used to stand.”

“This will not hold.”

“You should already be lighter.”

“We waited too long last time.”

Ash leaves the scene within the hour.

### Optional Mechanical Pressure

While Ash is present:

Attempts to create permanence take double time

Preserved resources risk spoilage

Plans longer than one day gain complications

This is not magic. It is pressure.

## Running Guidance

Reward adaptation, not curiosity.

If players:

Follow without asking

Stop optimizing

Let things go

The Branchbound remain.

If players demand clarity, leadership, or permanence, the forest answers instead.
