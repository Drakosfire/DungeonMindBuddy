# The Migrating Forest

## Executive DM Summary (Read Before Play)

This document is for **you**, the DM. Read it before the session. Let it tune your instincts. Some lines are meant to be read aloud. Others are for your spine, not the table.

The forest works if you feel it.

## The Vibe

This forest is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is **busy**.

It is under pressure from storms, migration, and the weight of its own memory. It does not hate the party. It does not care about them yet. What it notices is **weight**, **noise**, **delay**, and **permanence**.

The forest reacts the way a body reacts:

tightening around irritation

redirecting around pain

shedding what it cannot carry

If the players ask, “Is the forest alive?” The answer is not yes. The answer is: it behaves like something that needs to keep moving to survive.

## A Working History (Imprecise on Purpose)

Long before Mirathorn, before walls and councils, this forest learned how to move.

It learned because it had to. Storms came that did not pass. Roots rotted where they stood still. Predators followed fixed patterns and starved when the world shifted.

So the forest adapted.

Trees learned to loosen. Roots learned to let go. Ecosystems learned to migrate together instead of dying alone.

The Branchbound are not its creators. They are its **students**.

## What the Forest Believes (Without Belief)

The forest does not have morals. It has tendencies.

Still things attract storms

Heavy things sink

Straight lines invite correction

What is remembered too clearly will be rewritten

The forest is not trying to teach lessons. But lessons happen when you move through it.

## Read-Aloud: First Step Inside

The air changes before the light does.

The sounds of the world behind you dull, like fabric laid over a drum. Ahead, the forest is dense but not dark, layered instead of closed.

Roots rise where paths should be, then politely step aside. Leaves shift even when there’s no wind.

It feels less like entering a place and more like stepping onto something already in motion.

Pause after this. Let them respond.

## The Immediate Reaction

The forest does not attack on entry. It **adjusts**.

Within minutes:

paths curve

sound carries strangely

distance becomes unreliable

Nothing blocks the party outright. Nothing opens cleanly either.

If the players test the space early with fire, noise, or permanence, the forest remembers them faster.

## Quotes to Keep in Your Pocket

Use sparingly. These land harder when they are rare.

“The forest doesn’t stop you. It just makes the way you chose harder to keep choosing.”

“You get the sense this place has survived worse than you.”

“Nothing here looks ancient. It looks practiced.”

“The trees aren’t watching you. They’re watching what you do to the ground.”

“You realize the path didn’t disappear. It decided it wasn’t useful anymore.”

## How to Describe Forest Reactions

Avoid:

anger

malice

obvious intent

Lean into:

correction

pressure

inconvenience

redirection

Say things like:

“The ground settles differently after you pass.”

“The canopy seems to lean, not block.”

“You feel like you’re being herded, gently.”

## The Branchbound, in One Breath

They live here because they let go faster than the forest needs them to.

They are not guides. They are not rebels. They are people who stopped believing that staying put was the same as being safe.

When they help, it is sideways. When they vanish, it is final.

## Storms, Dinosaurs, and Escalation

Storms are not weather. They are **stress events**.

Dinosaurs are not monsters. They are **pressure valves**.

Neither exists to fight the party. They exist to force decisions:

move or stay

push or yield

take or leave behind

If combat happens, it should feel like something went wrong earlier.

## What Success Looks Like

Success is not clearing the forest.

Success is:

leaving fewer scars than expected

learning when to stop forcing progress

realizing the forest changed because of them, not for them

If the party exits saying, “I don’t think we were supposed to win that place,”

You ran it correctly.

## Final Note to the DM

Run this forest like a system under strain, not a villain.

When in doubt, ask:

What would make this harder without making it impossible?

The forest always answers that question.
