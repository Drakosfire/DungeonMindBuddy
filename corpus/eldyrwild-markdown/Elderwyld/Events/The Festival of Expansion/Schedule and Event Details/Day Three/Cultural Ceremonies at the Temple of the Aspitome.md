## Cultural Ceremonies at the Temple of the Aspitome

### I. Stories in Shadow

#### Setting & Sensory Details

**Space:** The main worship hall, high-arched and dimmed, walls adorned with drifting illusion-lanterns and hanging silks.

**Sights:** A long silk screen stretches across the altar, shimmering faintly with colors from the “bubble” outside. Shadowcasters move behind it, their figures shifting and dancing in the low candlelight. Occasionally, the wall itself seems to flicker with real light from the bubble—motes of blue and gold, sometimes red.

**Sounds:** Hushed voices, a low drumbeat, and the soft tap of mage-wands. As the story begins, voices echo: some are children, others old and trembling, others rich and strong.

**Smells:** Melted wax, cinnamon, faint burnt sugar, and a trace of strange “otherness” whenever the shimmer touches the wall.

#### Ceremony Structure

**Gathering:** People file in quietly, each receiving a small beeswax candle. Children cluster together. Elders nod knowingly. A subtle tension: some folks take this very seriously, others nudge and whisper.

**Opening Ritual:** The head illusionist, an ancient gnome named Master Tindlewix, steps forward, staff glimmering.

“Tonight, we let the past speak through light and shadow. If you wish, add your story—every shadow shapes our world.”

## Shadowplay Begins:

**Scenes:** The fall of the gods; a shadowy figure (Erathis) gathering heroes; the building of the Aspitome; the exodus through the shimmering bubble.

**Magic Users:** Mages and actors use Minor Illusion, Prestidigitation, and Mage Hand to craft fantastic images.

## Player Interaction:

PCs may be invited to join as guest shadowcasters (Arcana or Performance checks).

PCs may improvise dialogue for forgotten gods, lost peoples, or give a “voice” to a shadowy hero (Charisma or Deception/Performance).

If investigating (Perception, Insight), a PC might notice one shadow moving out of sync, or a whispered rumor about a secret being told tonight.

## Climax:

The screen glows as the bubble’s shimmer outside aligns with the shadowplay. For a moment, shadows blur and something alien flickers: a wolf-headed form, a spiral, or runes not part of the script.

Anyone succeeding on Arcana or Insight senses a surge of “old power,” perhaps a warning or plea for help from the boundary.

#### Player Hooks & Quest Leads

If the PCs help, they may find an audience with the head illusionist after (“I saw how you shaped that shadow—would you help us with a strange disturbance?”).

A child tells the party, “That shadow isn’t from our story. I see it in my dreams sometimes.”

A hidden symbol left behind on the screen matches a cultist sign, or one of the party’s personal mysteries.

### II. The Expansion Ritual

#### Setting & Sensory Details

**Space:** The heart of the temple, where a pillar of crystal (“Aspitome’s Heart”) stands on a circular dais. Smaller crystals are set in wrought-iron holders, faintly glowing, arranged in patterns that echo the shimmer seen outside.

**Sights:** Pulsing blue-white light, crackling sparks, glimmering runes inscribed in every language known to Mirathorn.

**Sounds:** Low, overlapping chants—some ancient and reverent, others in the dialects of the new city. Occasionally, a shudder runs through the hall as energy builds.

**Smells:** Heated stone, hot copper, faint ozone, and incense.

#### Ceremony Structure

**The Summoning:** The high cleric—draped in iridescent robes—raises a staff and calls for silence.

“As the Aspitome’s light grows, so does our world. Let each heart lend what it can, so that we remember where we began—and where we go next.”

## Collective Spell:

Attendees are invited, row by row, to step forward, touch a smaller charge crystal, and “will” a memory, hope, or bit of magic into it.

Some mutter old prayers, others just close their eyes. The crystals flare brighter with each new touch.

## Player Interaction:

PCs can contribute magic (Arcana, spell slot, or simply flavor).

PCs can channel emotion or memory (Performance, Persuasion, or Wisdom save to “impress” the ritual with sincerity).

If a PC is marked by fate (e.g., carrying a relic, touched by the Flock, etc.), their interaction may cause a surge—the crystal pulses, flickers, or even cracks.

## Ritual Twist/Complication:

If a crystal overcharges or cracks, something falls out (ancient rune, fragment of a goddess’s name, a forgotten relic).

A suspicious priest, cultist, or wizard pulls the PCs aside: “That was… unusual. Might we speak after?”

#### Player Hooks & Quest Leads

PCs might be recruited for a magical investigation (“The Aspitome is never supposed to react like that—unless…something is wrong.”)

The cracked relic contains a cryptic message, or points to a hidden chamber beneath the temple.

A vision flashes—perhaps of the Nameless God, the original exodus, or the boundary trembling in some future crisis.

## GM Cheat Sheet – Running Both Ceremonies

**Keep pacing flexible**: Let roleplayers and lore-hounds shine, but don’t require the whole party to participate in every ritual moment.

**Drop hooks through senses**: Unusual smells, sights, or sounds are great ways to hint at deeper mysteries or cult interference.

**Allow for gentle subversion:** PCs might spot, expose, or even assist with a heretical story or magical surge. Make this an RP reward, not a punishment.

**Reward curiosity**: Extra clues, Inspiration, or minor magical boons for anyone who investigates, helps the priests, or improvises their own story/magic.

**Plant at least one future hook**: A cracked crystal, a cult symbol in a shadow, a recurring child’s dream, or a cryptic vision—all can pay off in future sessions.
