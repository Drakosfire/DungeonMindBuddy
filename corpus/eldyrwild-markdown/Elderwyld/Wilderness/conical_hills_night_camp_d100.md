# d100 — Night camp on a conical hill

The party is camped **on the sharp rise**, in the bush, on or near one of the **pre-era conical hills** of the plains. The fire is dug into the slope; the bedrolls follow the curve of the cone; one watcher sits with their back to the cone's apex and the wind in their face.

This table is for **what happens overnight**. Most entries are **atmosphere** — Perception, Survival, or Wisdom prompts — that let the watch *do something* without waking anyone. Some entries are **unusual** (marked **[U]**) — strange but not threatening; trust them as flavour or as later breadcrumbs. A few are **events** (marked **[E]**) — they want a decision and may escalate to combat.

## How to use

- **One roll per watch.** With four watches a night, you'll roll a small handful — not all 100 hit a single night.
- **Order matters.** Take the rolled entry for the watch that rolls; don't rearrange.
- **[U] entries** can foreshadow geomantic drake activity, the cone's pre-era nature, or weather to come — pick what you've already seeded and use them.
- **[E] entries** assume **drakes are nearby**. If your campaign has no drakes, swap juvenile/elder drake calls for the nearest equivalent (lone wolf, scrub jaguar, fey stalker).
- **Distribution:** ~62 benign · ~33 unusual **[U]** · ~5 events **[E]**.
- **Related:** `Elderwyld/Wilderness/pre_era_conical_hills_d20_find.md`, `Elderwyld/Wilderness/geomantic_drake_juvenile_statblock.md`, `Elderwyld/Wilderness/geomantic_drake_nest_loot_d100.md`.

## 01–10 · Settling In

1. Fire catches on the third try — sap in the bushwood pops one bright spark, then steadies.
2. The rise puts the camp above ground mist; bedrolls stay dry while the plain below silvers.
3. Bedrolls find the lumps the cone hides; one watcher swears a slope-stone is shaped exactly like a sleeping dog.
4. A scrub hare freezes ten feet from the fire, decides the camp is furniture, lopes on.
5. Wind across the apex pushes smoke flat sideways — clean for breath, useless for warmth.
6. **[U]** Watch swap: the fire has burned itself a perfect ring of ash with the coals at dead centre. Whoever rebuilds it disturbs nothing.
7. The bush thorn in the second bedroll is impressively located. Strong opinions about it.
8. A horse downhill sighs in its hobbles; nothing else moves on the plain.
9. **[E]** *Bushline.* Something low and fast crosses the upwind side of camp. **Survival DC 14:** long stride, short steps, claws on stone — a **juvenile geomantic drake** working the perimeter for shinies (or for the camp). It will flee if challenged, parley with bait (one shiny, tossed), or strike if cornered. (`geomantic_drake_juvenile_statblock.md`.)
10. The first watch ends with no story to tell. That is itself a story to tell.

## 11–20 · Sounds in the Bush

1. A nightjar churrs from the slope below — long, long, then a hiccup and silence.
2. Dry grass ticks like a clock. Wind, then no wind. Wind again.
3. Distant lowing — cattle? wild oxen? — too far to count.
4. A frog choir starts on a hilltop pond and answers itself in canon.
5. An owl crosses the low moon, silent except for the small click of a closing wing.
6. **[U]** Three coyote yips, perfectly evenly spaced; a fourth comes two seconds late, **exactly** the same pitch. Not a coyote.
7. Small animal squabble in the bush — whatever lost is being eaten now, quietly.
8. A pebble shifts in the cone scree — nothing reveals itself when the watcher looks.
9. **[U]** A whispered word, just one, in a dialect the watcher knows from a guard captain back home. Nobody is there.
10. A rabbit panics across camp, hits a bedroll, bounces off, vanishes into the bush.

## 21–30 · Sky and Air

1. The bowl of stars over the cone is uncommonly clear; the milk-track sits east-to-west tonight.
2. **[U]** A star, low and steady, slides three finger-widths north of where it ought to be, then back. Once.
3. Heat lightning paints the horizon orange; no thunder. Twelve heartbeats apart.
4. A cool front rolls in fast — by midnight the sky stitches itself in racing cloud.
5. **[U]** The breeze on the apex turns and **only** the apex. Bushes ten feet downslope keep their original direction for a full minute.
6. Bats work the bug column above the fire; they thank no one and stay polite.
7. *(Storm-eye / weather sense, passive.)* Pressure dropping, slowly — weather tomorrow, not tonight.
8. Insects loudens, drops to nothing for a count of six, resumes. An owl somewhere, or not.
9. **[U]** Smoke from the fire braids itself, then unbraids, before going about its business.
10. Wind brings, faintly, the smell of wet stone — but the nearest wet stone is the hilltop pond above, and the wind is wrong for it.

## 31–40 · The Pond on the Crown

1. The hilltop pond is a flat black mirror; one frog sits where the moon lands, in silhouette.
2. Watch dips a waterskin and tastes — clean, slight mineral tang, drinkable boiled.
3. **[E]** *The pond.* A V-wake crosses the still water with no swimmer in front of it; long, slow, going somewhere. **Investigation** reveals **no creature** at the surface. **Arcana DC 14:** stored geomantic current discharging across surface tension. If a watcher wades in, the cone responds with a single cool pulse through their soles — *and* a startled **juvenile drake** drinking from the underside of the bank punches through and runs (combat optional; one juvenile, fights to flee).
4. Ripples spell a circle, then a line, then nothing repeatable.
5. A reed bends without weight, straightens, bends again two reeds over.
6. **[U]** When a coal pops in the fire, the pond's surface dimples in **the same instant** — not from sound; from sympathy.
7. The watcher sees their reflection blink first.
8. The pond holds heat better than the air; mist crawls along the rim only, nowhere else.
9. **[U]** A water-bird the watcher cannot name walks across the surface, leaves no print, settles on a tussock as if it always lived there.
10. Pond goes dead-flat: no breeze, no insects on it — for a long minute, then everything resumes.

## 41–50 · The Hill Itself

1. **[U]** A flat stone under the bedroll is suspiciously **flat**. **Investigation DC 12:** it's worked — a cone facing-stone gone rough.
2. **[U]** The slope hums faintly through the boots when a watcher stands still on it for a full minute. **Arcana DC 13:** pre-era resonance, harmless, not aimed at the camp.
3. Loose scree above camp settles a second time without anything moving it. Nothing falls.
4. A dug rabbit-burrow, much enlarged, slants down at a wrong angle for a rabbit.
5. **[U]** A handprint in the dust on a flat stone. Not the watcher's. Wrong size for any party member. Gone if anyone leaves and looks back.
6. The bush at the cone's flank holds three berries the watcher can't identify; the next bush, identical, holds none.
7. **[U]** A small, perfect spiral has been cleared in the dust beside the fire — pebbles arranged outward, none in. Nobody saw it happen.
8. The cone's apex catches first dew; in moonlight it shines like a small crown.
9. **[U]** Stepping onto a particular slab makes the watcher's hair stand on end. Stepping off, normal. The slab does this **only at night**.
10. **[U]** The stones have a heartbeat in them, just out of phase with the watcher's. Hold a hand there long enough and it argues.

## 51–60 · Visitors at the Edge

1. A pair of glowing eyes, low — fox, by the gait — exits stage left.
2. Deer downhill bed down within sight of the fire; they trust the rise more than they fear humans.
3. A moth too big for its season laps the firelight, lands on a bedroll, leaves a smear of pollen exactly where a brooch used to be.
4. Three ravens watch from the next rise, silent. Move on at false dawn.
5. **[U]** A barn owl drops a whole vole at the watcher's feet, considers the gift delivered, and leaves.
6. **[U]** A herd of small lizard-things passes through camp at ankle height, ignores everything, vanishes downslope.
7. Fox cubs — three — wrestle in plain view at the bush edge until the vixen barks them off.
8. **[U]** A **lone elder geomantic drake** lands two slope-lengths above camp, on the apex. It does **not** look at the camp. It picks something up — a single scale, a feather, a dropped coin — and flies. Combat only if the party escalates; otherwise this is a long, quiet stare in shared silence. (`geomantic_drake_juvenile_statblock.md` — scale up for the elder.)
9. A badger-shaped silhouette ambles past the back of camp, snuffles the latrine pit, ambles on.
10. Bats roost — briefly — on the lid of an open cookpot. Watcher and bats both decide to ignore each other.

## 61–70 · Sleep and Dream

1. A sleeper rolls to the cone's downslope side and stays there, cradled by the curve, very content.
2. **[U]** Two PCs, on different watches, dream the same thin man at the foot of a cone, hands open, not threatening. Compare notes in the morning if they speak of it.
3. A calming-tea pouch smells stronger in the dark — settles the camp for free.
4. A sleeper laughs once in their bedroll; nobody asks.
5. **[U]** A watcher's half-waking dream: looking **down** at the camp from above, very high, briefly. Then back. **Wis DC 12** to dismiss; otherwise, a quiet unease for the rest of the watch.
6. Snore symphony: somebody's rhythm syncs with the frog choir for thirty seconds.
7. **[U]** The fire dies down to coals; the camp gets warmer for a minute, then colder. Old folk would say the cone is breathing.
8. **[U]** A sleeping PC's hand lies on a cone-stone all night; in the morning the palm is faintly etched with a spiral that fades by noon.
9. The lightest sleeper opens an eye, sees nothing, closes it, falls back. Reports nothing.
10. A long, deep hour passes in which **nothing** happens. The camp is grateful.

## 71–80 · Lights and Lines

1. A single far light flickers on the plain — a fellow camp, half a mile out, almost certainly friendly.
2. **[U]** Three lights on three rises, each on the apex of a smaller cone, blinking in slow turn — left, middle, right, left.
3. A meteor draws a thin scratch across the bowl, ends with a tiny green flash.
4. **[U]** A blue-white pulse runs along the horizon at ground level, like sheet lightning **under** the dirt. Once.
5. A distant cart-lantern crawls along the road far below; honest pace, no urgency.
6. **[E]** *Intruders.* Two figures (scouts? scavengers? Saints copycats?) work the slope a hundred yards out, low and quiet, **heading uphill** to the camp. **Watch DC 15** to spot before they arrive. Could be a parley (curious, opportunistic), a robbery attempt, or — at worst — a cult-tinted ambush. Three to five humanoids; choose flavour from your campaign's threats.
7. The far horizon glows orange too long to be sunset memory. A grass fire, far enough not to matter, close enough to notice.
8. **[U]** A lantern ignites in the bush near the camp, held by no one. Lasts six seconds, gone.
9. **[U]** A line of fireflies moves in a clean, straight column — north to south — along the cone's flank. They do not stop and do not branch.
10. The northern sky takes on a pale veil; auroral flicker very rarely seen this far south. Brief.

## 81–90 · The Hill Speaks

1. A pebble, then another, then a small fall — nothing on the slope when the watcher checks.
2. **[U]** A thin ribbon of warm air rises straight from a crack between two cone-stones; cold air all around. Sustained.
3. Faint vibration through the ground for ten seconds — too gentle to be hooves, too even to be a tremor. Stops.
4. **[U]** A whisper exactly under a sleeping PC's bedroll, in a language nobody at the table speaks. Five syllables. Translate later — or never.
5. A small piece of pre-era brick (cleaner than the stones around it) has worked itself out of the slope and sits in the open.
6. **[U]** A chord — one — sounds from somewhere inside the cone. Three notes, plain, not threatening. Repeats once an hour later, the **same** three notes, the **same** order.
7. **[U]** The cone's shadow under the moon does not fall where geometry says it should. By an inch. Confirmed by lining up a stick.
8. **[U]** A pocket of cold pools in one specific spot at the camp's edge — a body's breadth — holds for an hour, dissipates.
9. The fire's smoke leans **into** the cone, not away from it, for the duration of one watch.
10. A seam in the stone weeps a single drop of clear water every minute, exactly. Counted, then disbelieved, then counted again.

## 91–100 · Things That Come

1. **[U]** A juvenile geomantic drake drinks from the pond at the far rim, ignores the camp, leaves with a small silver coin in its mouth that nobody put there.
2. A small predator (lynx-coded) studies the camp from a slope-stone for a long minute, decides against, goes.
3. A distant howl, single, answered by no other. Wolves uninterested in the cone tonight.
4. **[E]** *Drake fly-by with intent.* A juvenile (maybe two) circles the camp at low altitude, **eye on a shiny** in the party's gear (a buckle, a ring, a coin pouch). **Survival DC 13** to read it as **bait-able** — a discarded shiny may end this peacefully. Otherwise: a short fight with one or two juveniles. (`geomantic_drake_juvenile_statblock.md`.)
5. A snake transits the warm stone next to the fire; nobody hurt; no judgment from the snake.
6. **[U]** A great moth, palm-spread, lands on a watcher's chest, opens its wings: an **eye-pattern** that is, for one flicker, the eye of someone the watcher knows. Then gone.
7. A curious carrion-bird (raven or kite) noses the meat sack; shoo it, no harm done.
8. **[U]** A second wind passes through camp at chest height — warm, dry — that should not exist on the plain at this hour. Lasts a breath.
9. **[E]** *The cone answers.* A long, low note **inside** the hill — felt more than heard — shakes loose a section of facing-stone, exposing a **fist-sized marker** carved with a pre-era spiral. **Investigation DC 15** notices the stone behind the marker is hollow-sounding (a small cavity, not a doorway); **Arcana DC 16** reads the seal as **passive** — meant to mark, not bind. Inside the cavity (if pried open with care, **+1 watch of work**): one small pre-era find — roll on `pre_era_conical_hills_d20_find.md`. The stone reseats itself in any rain.
10. The clearest dawn the party has had in weeks: sky pink, frogs hoarse, fire gone to perfect ash; one extra **shed drake-scale** sits on the apex (~20 gp; see `pre_era_conical_hills_d20_find.md`).

## Optional dials

- **Drake-heavy night:** add **+10** to a single roll once per night to push toward the **91–100** range.
- **Quiet table:** subtract **−10** from the first roll if the party set a careful camp (no fire, low watch profile, no metal cookware noise).
- **Storm overhead:** if a real storm arrives during the night, treat any **[U]** entry as an **[E]** at the GM's discretion — the cone responds to weather.
