# d20 — Conical hill finds (Mireward Reach plains)

Roll when the party **commits** to climbing one of the **three** grassy cones or **probing** the **pond**. Re-roll if you want a different cone to feel distinct; **escalate** (deeper mud, sharper hum, angrier locals) if they dig.

**Player-facing read:** odd little hills with **still water** on top — pretty, lonely, maybe cursed, probably nothing. **GM-facing read:** every result hints at **geometry, fill, or purpose** without requiring a full dungeon tonight.

## Expanded entry 15 — Nest, not lair

**Player read-aloud (summit / berm near the pond):** The mud here is **too smooth** — as if something **heavy** turned in place, again and again. **Crescents** of **scale** catch the light: **blue-green** oil-slick at one angle, **dull bone** at another. They’re **thin**, **flexible**, and **warm** long after the sun should have cooled them. Pressed into the same mud are **chips** of shell — not like a chicken egg; more like **glazed pottery** snapped into **hex** shards, edges **too clean** to be river-worn.

**What checks reveal (pick what fits your table):**

- **Nature / Survival (moderate):** This is **molt**, not a fight — no **blood** spray, no **tooth** scores, no **quills** torn out. The spiral **follows the hill’s grade** deliberately, like someone **combed** the slope with a rake.
- **Investigation:** The scales are **new** (days, not years). Under the largest flakes, the soil is **packed** in **rings** — older than the shedding; **GM:** that’s the **engineered** core of the cone whispering through.
- **Religion (optional, DC 13):** If your cults use **geometry** in worship, the spiral + **hex** shell reads like a **tithe** or **beacon** — not proof, just **unease**.

**What creature’s scale is this? (table default)**  
Unless you substitute, treat the shed as a **young geomantic drake** — full **read-aloud**, **mechanics**, and **JSON** for a generator: `Elderwyld/Wilderness/geomantic_drake_juvenile_statblock.md`. — **not** a true dragon, but **draconic-adjacent** **Monstrosity** (or **Elemental** if you prefer) that senses **buried structure** and **grounds** excess **geomantic** charge by **molting** on **cones** and **wells**. The **“eggshell”** is **calcified casing** from the same line: **hex** tiles are how the brood **packed** heat while the parent was away.  
**Quick swaps (same clues, different story):** **fey wyrm** hatchling; **Shepherd-touched** chimeric beast; **pre-era bioservitor** “lizard” still breeding in the wild.

**Arcana (5e-style DCs — adjust for tier):**

| DC | On success |
|----|------------|
| **13** | These scales carry a **residual charge** — not magic items, but they **conducted** something; **not** normal reptile shed. |
| **16** | **Draconic-adjacent** life cycle: **molt** tied to **place**, not season — the creature **needed** this **hill’s shape** (or what’s under it). |
| **19** | Name the pattern: **geomantic nesting** — things that feed on (or bleed off) **old wards** / **buried workings** pick **conical** high points. The **hex** shell confirms **engineered** or **bred** stock, not wild **drake**. |
| **22** | Rare lore: ties this brood line to **pre-era** **terrace-ward** builders — the nest is **symptom** of a **still-active** layer under the sod. |

**Nature (alternative to Arcana for “what is it?”):** Same DC ladder but answers emphasize **behavior** and **habitat** (“nests on **earth-mounds** that **hum** after storms”) rather than **arcane mechanism**. Use **lower DC by 2** if the PC has **draconic** or **fey** expertise from backstory.

**GM truth (why it fits an artificial cone):** Whatever nested here wasn’t fooled by grass and sheep. It **felt** the old shape — **resonance**, **warmth**, or **leakage** from whatever the pre-era builders buried — and treated the summit like a **natural** cradle. The **parent** may not know the hill is fake; neither do the PCs.

**Where’s the parent?** Choose one (or roll d4):

1. **Foraging** — returns at **dusk** with a **wet** belly and **full** throat; not hungry for people unless cornered.
2. **Driven off** — **Wyvern**, **shepherd** spirit, or **storm** pressure; nest **abandoned** mid-season; scales are **stress-shed**.
3. **Below** — adult is **trapped** in a **vent** or **old shaft** under the pond; scratching sometimes **bubbles** mud.
4. **Never was “parent”** — these are **trade scales** and **ceramic** egg-fragments left as **fey toll** or **marker**; the “nest” is **performance**.

**If they disturb it:** **Rustle** in the grass; **one** scale **flips** and shows a **different** color underneath (alarm? camouflage?). **Stealing** shells may earn a **tracker** later — not necessarily the owner; could be **something** that collects **discarded** nests.

**Bonogo angle:** Let him **commit** to “dragon gold” for thirty seconds — then the scales prove **too small** for a wyrmling hoard fantasy, and the shell is **wrong**. The **win** is **curiosity**: this hill is **special** enough that **something** chose it on purpose.

**Combat only if you want it:** A **defensive** adult **skirmishes** to **lead them off** the summit, not to **wipe** the party — the nest matters more than pride.

## Using the three cones

Make **one** hill **mundane**, **one** **weird**, **one** **risky** — Bonogo stays **half right** without a guaranteed hoard.

- If a **double rainbow** is still in play, favor **wrong reflections**, **ice lens**, **storm bell**, **thunder glass**.
