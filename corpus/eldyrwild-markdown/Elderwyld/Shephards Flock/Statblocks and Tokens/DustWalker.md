## 🕯️ DUSTWALKER — INTERROGATION PROFILE

**Real Name:** Sorin Haldrim
 **Aliases:** The Dustwalker, The Spiral Voice, Maelthor's Echo
 **Age:** Late 30s
 **Race:** Human
 **Origin:** Originally from a border-town between the Lundayell Empire and the Everfen.

### 🎭 PERSONALITY SNAPSHOT

Dustwalker speaks in slow, poetic phrasing. He rarely answers questions directly and often turns interrogations into performances. He’s obsessed with music, rhythm, and hidden meanings. He doesn’t fear death—he fears being forgotten. While not entirely insane, his mind has been rewired by the Shepherd's sermons.

## Key Traits:

Speaks in riddles, verse, or cryptic metaphors.

Makes eye contact too long and smiles at inopportune moments.

Occasionally hums "The Shepherd’s Eyes" softly when stressed.

Hates mirrors and reflective surfaces.

### 🧠 WHAT HE KNOWS (AND HOW HE MIGHT SAY IT)

#### ❗Basic Knowledge (easy to extract)

**The Spiral Gate is a network** of converted underground bunkers and tunnels, hidden beneath Mirathorn and branching into Outtown, the swamps, and beneath Stormspire Academy.


 “The Shepherd’s voice echoes through the earth, through stone once carved by dwarves who feared the sky.”

**The Shepherd’s Flock recruits mostly disillusioned humans**, targeting those who feel abandoned by the city, especially veterans, laborers, and apprentices.


 “We are the marrow of Mirathorn, broken and boiled to feed its gleaming bones.”

**Maelthor is real, and near.** He refers to the Shepherd as a “godling made flesh,” not yet risen.


 “He is not here—not yet. But his fingers stir the soil, and his breath chokes the clouds.”

**He wrote “The Shepherd’s Eyes.”** It’s not just a song—it’s a summoning ritual.


 “Every verse, a blade; every chorus, a door. Sing enough times and something will answer.”

#### ❗Deeper Secrets (require persuasion, leverage, or magical pressure)

**The Fleshbarn Sermons are just one front.** There are multiple cells. One may be beneath an abandoned district aqueduct, another hidden among the floating traders at the Lake.


 “The Flock nests in many rafters. Burn one barn, and another fills with bleating.”

**He once trained at Stormspire.** He was an expelled bardic student for “unsanctioned experimentation with binding harmonics.”


 “I sang too sweetly to silence. And silence… sang back.”

**He has seen the Flesh Egg in a dream.** He believes he is the Shepherd’s mouth, but someone called “The Calf-Bearer” is the body.


 “In the dream, the yolk cracks. Something teethless and vast gnaws its way to the surface. I awoke with song in my throat—and blood on my tongue.”

**He fears Maelthor’s ****true form.** In lucid moments, he hints it’s not what they were promised.


 “We were promised wings and purpose. But what stirs beneath the swamp... it has so many mouths.”

### 🛠️ INTERROGATION TOOLS

## How to crack him:

**Music:** He can’t resist musical harmony or improvisation. A bard dueling him in song might draw out truths.

**Truth magic:** Zone of Truth works, but his phrasing will remain metaphorical unless called out.

**Empathy:** He was once a broken idealist. Showing they understand why he turned may unravel him.

## What he responds to:

Names of other cultists — especially “The Calf-Bearer” and “Vellin Thorne” (a false prophet rival he loathes).

Any mention of the original bardic showcase — he's proud the players remember his performance.

Subtle threats to destroy his unfinished hymns or instruments — he cares more about his voice living on than his own survival.

### 🎼 DUSTWALKER'S FINAL OFFER

If he believes death is near or that the Shepherd’s plans are truly endangered, he’ll whisper:

“You’ll never find the heart until you play the song backwards… in the storm, beneath the mirror lake.”

This cryptic line could refer to:

A ritual song.

A lost Flock stronghold under Lake Mirathorn.

A backdoor into Maelthor’s domain through magical reversal.

### 📜 The Fall of Sorin Haldrim, the Dustwalker

**Sorin was once a student at Stormspire Academy**, specializing in sonic magic and mythopoetic theory. He studied how song, spoken word, and harmonic vibration could shape the weave itself—his performances could calm riots or shake wine glasses from across a hall.

But his radical thesis, “Resonance as Ritual: The Hymn as Portal”, was mocked and dismissed. He claimed the voice could open things. Not metaphorically—literally. He said there were places **beneath** the world’s song that could be sung into waking.

He was expelled.

### 🎶 The First Note

He wandered Outtown. Performed on crates. Slept in alleys. He sang to empty streets.

Until one day, a girl in the slums sang the next line of his song back to him.

She was already Touched by the Flock.

She took him to a sermon. Not a bloody one—yet. Just a circle of broken people singing by candlelight. He saw faces lifted in pain and rapture, heard a resonance he had only theorized. When they all sang together, something shimmered at the edge of his hearing.

That night he dreamed of a vast mouth **beneath the city** that asked him, softly, if he would be its tongue.

He accepted.

### 🩸 Not Meatbound — Yet Marked

**He is not meat-corrupted**, not physically—yet. His body remains human, though thin and worn. But he has eaten from the Choral Flesh, a sliver of the Shepherd that gave his voice its unholy echo.

So now, when he sings, **his voice carries**. It vibrates in bone. It bypasses thought.

He is still himself—but tuned to a different key.

### 🧠 So, why did he join?

**Rejection** by the academic elite.

**The discovery** that the cult's music worked—it was proof of his theories.

**The worship** he receives within the cult gives him purpose.

**He believes** he is creating something beautiful, even if others call it monstrous.

“They called me mad when I tried to wake the world with music. But now I sing, and gods turn their heads.”
