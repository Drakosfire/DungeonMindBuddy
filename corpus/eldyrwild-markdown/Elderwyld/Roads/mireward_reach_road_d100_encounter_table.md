# d100 Road Encounter Table — The Mireward Reach

**The Mireward Reach** is the drove-road and wheel-way that carries trade, troops, and rumor north from Mirathorn toward the fen country and the swamp frontier — past Outtown, waystations, and towns like Mossford that earn their living from distance. It is not one king’s highway; it is a braid of maintained crown span, patched county line, and mud tracks honest cartwrights swear by.

Use this table for a day’s march or ride on the Reach when nothing specific is keyed, when the party pushes hard, camps too visibly, helps or harms someone on the road, or when you want the journey to bite back.

**Rule of Escalation:** If the same band or “feel” hits twice, do not repeat verbatim. Raise stakes (more witnesses, worse weather, sharper teeth), shorten time to react, or tie the beat to a PC flag (oath, bounty, cult history, kin).

Each entry is written to be usable raw, expandable in play, and resolved quickly.

## 1–10 · Crown, Mud, and Maintenance

Wheel ruts deepen after rain; the crown of the road holds, but shoulders turn to sucking mud. Animals labor. Someone must choose: ruin pace or risk a stuck axle.

A mile-marker post leans like a drunk; fresh chisel marks rename the next town — prank, warning, or land claim.

Ditch crews wave the party past with tired pride. They’re filling washouts before the next storm. Gossip is the toll: they want news from the south.

A bridge plank groans wrong. Repair timbers lie stacked nearby, unsecured. Use now or risk the crossing later when it’s dark.

Toll collectors (crown charter, county, or self-appointed) argue with a merchant about livestock count. The queue lengthens. Tempers shorten.

Smoke rises from a burn pile of diseased brush off the verge. The smell is sweet-rotten. Officials insist the road stays open.

A bell rings at a distant waystation — all clear, storm watch, or funeral. Locals disagree on which.

Gooseflesh on exposed skin; the sky is clear. Old drivers say the Reach is remembering a lightning strike that hasn’t happened yet.

Iron caltrops scattered on a bend, too fresh to be war. Someone wanted this convoy stopped — or anyone.

The road widens into what was once a market apron. Grass claims it. Foundations outline stalls that folded fast.

## 11–20 · Traffic, Convoys, and Strangers

A salt train crawls north, oxen lowing. The drovers will trade rumor for firewood or healing.

Pilgrims walk the berm, singing harmless hymns — unless your table knows better. They want blessing at the next shrine, not debate.

A rider blows past too close, cloak snapping. They drop a leather tube — map, message, or bait.

Two carters feud over right of way; both claim crown patent. A crowd forms in seconds.

Refugees in mismatched wagons, northbound when mercy says go south. They won’t explain. Fear explains enough.

A mirage of Mirathorn’s spires shimmers over hot mud. Veterans laugh. New hands go quiet.

Mercenary column marching south, faces closed. Their paymaster rides a grey horse and won’t meet eyes.

A child offers flowers tied with red string for a copper. An adult snatches them back — too late for something, or just in time.

Funeral traffic: black ribbons, slow wheels. Passing without respect earns curses; helping earns a name in a grateful family.

Circus colors — or Saint patches — on a wagon roof. The crew waves friendly. The pipes aren’t out yet.

## 21–30 · Weather, Sky, and Shimmer

Fog drops between hedgerows; hooves sound wrong — too many, or too few.

Rain comes sideways, then stops like a lid closed. Cloaks steam.

Heat without sun; metal burns to touch. Mirage water leads a half mile off road.

Hail dents helms; animals bolt. Cover is a ditch with its own rules.

Thunder answers no lightning — until it doesn’t, and then everyone flinches.

Shimmer in the raindrops — one PC recognizes it from Mossford weather. Magic is riding the front.

Wind tears a signboard loose; it skitters like a thing chasing the party.

Double rainbow over a bog; frogs go silent. Old folk say don’t make promises under it.

Stars too bright; constellations wrong for the season. Navigation by sky argues with road.

Static in hair and banner; sparks nip fingertips at metal touch. Storm inheritance, not forecast.

## 31–40 · Hamlets, Inns, and Half-Truths

A hamlet offers well water and bread — if someone fixes the mill sluice today.

Inn sign new, paint sticky. The name doesn’t match the map. The ale is honest.

Sheriff asks one question too many about Mirathorn. Politics wears a badge.

Market day collapsed early; stalls half empty. Merchants whisper requisition.

Shrine by the crossroads: candles fresh, no priest. Offerings untouched.

Blacksmith works late; weapons stacked for a buyer who hasn’t come.

Children play graveyard games with stick coffins. Adults pretend not to see.

Letter nailed to a post — bounty, marriage, warning about meat sold cheap.

Wedding spills onto the road; ribbon tangles in wheel spokes. Luck or delay — player choice.

Waystation ledger open on a desk; clerk gone. Tea still warm.

## 41–50 · Predators, Press, and Petty Cruelty

Wolves shadow the berm — normal, until too many eyes.

Bandits flag false distress; cutpurses work the rubberneck crowd.

Impress gang with dubious charter demands “volunteers” for ditch fortification.

Poachers flee across road; warden behind them demands the party stand aside.

Bridge troll metaphor made literal: a tax collector so fat and mean locals mythologize him.

Escaped beast — zoo, war, or experiment — charges down-grade; panic up.

Crows argue in rows along a fence; something dead ahead they’re queueing for.

Snare line across animal trail snags a rider who shortcut wrong.

Drunk soldiers arm-wrestle for beer; officer arrives at worst moment.

Fake cleric sells relics; one piece hums when cult-touched PC passes.

## 51–60 · Shepherd’s Shadow (Subtle)

Meat smoke wrong for the hour — too sweet, too long in the air.

Chalk symbol under new dust — shepherd’s crook or innocent brand? Both hurt someone.

Kind stranger offers free jerky — perfect salt, wrong chew. Animal refuses it.

Crowd hums one note before they know why; it passes. No harm? Or rehearsal?

Found coin — Mirathorn mint, filed smooth on one face. Touch tingles.

Widow asks party to read a letter she can’t burn. Second paragraph rhymes wrong.

Well water fine yesterday; today it tastes like copper and choir.

Livestock won’t cross a bridge at dusk — no visible reason.

Tavern song everyone knows — extra verse tonight nobody remembers learning.

Corpse by ditch — natural death, but smile too serene. Tokens gone, shoes left.

## 61–70 · Forks, Fences, and Bad Maps

Signpost spun — accident or setup? Mud shows fresh grip.

Crown map says bridge; county map says ford. Both exist; one lies in season.

Herders closed a gap in fence with thorn — legal, annoying, slow.

Detour adds half day but avoids tax and gossip both — or so claims a local.

River ate the old road; new path clings to bluff. Loose stones announce themselves.

Compass spins at one crossroads only — iron below, folk say.

Mile counts disagree between two guides — same destination, different pride.

Animal refuses north fork; south fork smells wrong to the ranger.

Ghost milestones — old stones with forgotten names; one matches a PC dream.

Road widens into battlefield grass — old violence composted into wildflowers and bad luck.

## 71–80 · Liminal Weird

Standing stones — sheep ignore them; dogs won’t bark near.

Abandoned cart, cargo spilled like someone sorted it first.

Single boot in road, laces tied double. No blood. No tracks off.

Hollow tree echoes wagon wheels that aren’t there.

Butterflies all one color — wrong for the week.

Night watch sees second moon — blink and it’s gone.

Puddle shows reflection of party — one face delayed half second.

Raven drops key — fits nothing they carry yet.

Field of stakes with ribbons — festival or claim or ward.

Bell in no tower rings once at noon — everyone pretends not to hear.

## 81–100 · Hard Turns (Major Beats)

Abandoned hamlet — doors open, kettles cool, livestock gone or dead in pens. Tracks north only.

Crown rider with sealed orders demands escort or stand aside — politics now.

Real battle spill — wounded retreating south, pursuers minutes behind. Road is chokepoint.

False checkpoint — uniforms almost right. Paperwork almost wrong. Violence or bribe or bluff.

Longhouse rumble through mud — pipes distant but getting honest. Crowd already gathered at next bend.

Flock procession crossing Reach — white ribbons, too many eyes agreeing. Detour costs a day; through costs something else.

Wildfire wind shift — smoke wall drops on road. Visibility nil. Lungs burn.

Flash flood from upland — ford becomes death. High ground fights you for space.

Plague banner on waystation — real or panic? Quarantine or run?

Deserters with crown wagons full of something that whispers in crates.

Mirathorn agent dead in ditch — pockets full of swamp sketches and meat samples.

Entire convoy frozen mid-step — not statues; time hiccup. Ends when party chooses a fixed action.

Mother of Fallen Branches sign — not her, but something watched the road here and left a print.

Sinkhole opens — reveals old road below this one. Voices carry up clearer than they should.

Saint and cult sympathizer tied to same wagon wheel — crowd demands a verdict the law won’t touch.

Dream bleeds into day — all PCs share one image: tower or pit or mouth — GM pick per campaign.

Lightning strikes milestone — new name burned into wood nobody present knows how to read.

The Reach itself closes behind — mudslide, orders, magic. Forward only until resolved.

Hymn rises from nowhere — full harmony, no visible choir. Counters or joins have consequences.

The road is meat under hooves for thirty paces — then normal again. Animals remember longer than people.
