# Lysandro Ironveil — Character Dossier

## Table Handle

**Lysandro Ironveil is what happens when an old wall-family produces a maker instead of a captain:** free-hearted, loud, persuasive, deeply allergic to hierarchy, and still too well raised to ignore a bad night on the wall.

He is **Lysandra Ironveil's father**, but he should not play as "Lysandra in older clothes." He is nearly her opposite. Lysandra finds comfort in systems, rank, city convenience, and the promise that authority can be made responsible. Lysandro believes people think best when nobody is standing on their neck, and that most rules are either folk agreements wearing boots or bad machines waiting to jam.

## Why He Is Out Tonight

Hester's Edge packet scared the people in Mireward who knew how to read it. Reeve Salla Vey can inventory grain, Mayor Orric Tane can keep a crowd from panicking, and Nera Coalstep can make a wall hold, but **Lysandro can make ordinary Mireward folk show up without feeling conscripted**.

He is out after tenth bell because he volunteered to help explain the new watch pattern, then stayed because everyone quietly relied on him. His immediate work is not formal command. It is neighbor-scale readiness:

- Make sure people know the bell pattern.
- Mark which carts can block which road-lanes without trapping families.
- Teach lantern signals to people who have never worn a uniform.
- Turn "guard duty" into "hold your own doorway and your neighbor's."
- Keep the south gate from becoming a panic knot while the north gate watches the fen.

Scene image: Lysandro stands on the south wall walk with a shuttered lantern, a chalk board, and a half-assembled bell-linkage gadget. He is explaining to two nervous locals why **watching** is not the same as **soldiering** when the party arrives.

## Family and Place

The Ironveils are among the older Mireward families: guardians, farmers, wall-stakeholders, people who remember that the town was not gifted by a crown but built through work, argument, and blood. Lysandro grew up inside that tradition of vigilance.

Peace changed him. Mireward has been safer than it was in his grandfather's day, and Lysandro let that safety become philosophy. He did not become careless; he became more egalitarian. If the wall stands because everyone has a stake in it, then the town should not need uniforms and salutes to remember itself.

The family is content to be left alone. That is a large part of why Lysandro is not mayor. He has the charisma and trust for it, but the Ironveil instinct is not to tell others what to do unless the need is dear and immediate.

## Work and Craft

Lysandro departed from the family line of direct guardianship and became a maker-merchant. He owns and runs a small manufacturing concern that produces finely crafted machine parts: gear teeth, pressure screws, latchwork, bell fittings, small valve assemblies, coupling pins, spring housings, and precision repair pieces for larger machines built elsewhere.

He studied in Mirathorn with goblin and gnome artificers, and he loved the work: libraries, brass shops, precise tools, arguments over tolerances, the way a mechanism tells the truth if you listen to it. But he came home because Mirathorn asked him to climb ladders he did not want, and Mireward offered sky, quiet, neighbors, and the freedom to build excellent things without becoming a city man.

His workshop language bleeds into everything:

- A town is "a clock that forgives bad teeth."
- A bad order is "a gear cut for somebody else's axle."
- A good neighbor is "a spring that remembers its shape."
- Panic is "heat in the housing; don't curse the gear, cool the box."

## Philosophy

Lysandro believes deeply in free thought and free action. He is content to let people make messes of themselves, argue badly, choose strange roads, and come back changed. His heart stays open to growth. He does not confuse a person's worst hour with their whole mechanism.

That mercy is not passivity. He is not stupid, not blind, and not stubborn for sport. When the old wall-blood wakes up, he acts. His difference from formal authority is method: he hates giving commands, so he phrases them as invitations, workshop observations, jokes, and civic dares that somehow get obeyed.

He is anti-authoritarian, not anti-responsibility.

## Lysandra Conflict

Lysandra's rebellion was not running away from duty. It was running toward rank.

To Lysandro, his brilliant daughter mistook hierarchy for purpose. She left a town that taught accountability without crown worship and became a well-respected rising member of the Mirathorn Guard. He had a hard time with it, partly because he feared the city would reward the parts of her that hid her tenderness, and partly because her success proved she had chosen that world honestly.

To Lysandra, Lysandro mistook looseness for principle. She saw how institutions could protect people when they worked, and she wanted to be one of the people who made them work.

They love each other. They also embarrass each other at maximum volume.

How to play the reunion:

- He teases her rank in public because he loves her and because rank is a costume to him.
- He respects competence immediately, including hers, especially when danger is real.
- He calls her **Lysa** before remembering she hates it in uniform.
- His face changes when he sees her, but his hand stays on the lantern chain.
- He can be a father and still be vigilant.

## Voice

Warm, too loud for tenth bell, workshop-poetic, charismatic, and maddeningly kind. He sounds like a man who can turn a complaint line into a work crew by making everyone feel like the idea was theirs.

Use:

- Maker metaphors.
- Neighbor language.
- Affectionate irreverence toward rank.
- Plain seriousness when someone is in danger.
- A drop to quiet only for Lysandra or when the wall tells him something is wrong.

Avoid:

- Making him a clown. He is funny, not foolish.
- Making him negligent. He is anti-authoritarian, not asleep.
- Making him secretly power-hungry. He avoids office because he values being left alone.
- Making him merely "retired guard." He knows the wall through family and civic duty, but his life is craft and trade.

## Table Lines

- "Road's open. Town's full. Wagons wait till dawn unless they're bleeding, burning, or useful."
- "No, friend, I'm not deputizing you. I'm asking whether your lantern works and whether you love your neighbors."
- "A bell is just a machine for making one person's worry everybody's business."
- "Lysa, if you salute me in front of the wall, I will be forced to become historically unbearable."
- "Uniforms are fine things. Good pockets. Terrible philosophy."
- "A town that needs permission to save itself has already lost a gate."
- "Don't shout orders at scared people. Give them a handle and somewhere to put their hands."
- "Panic is not cowardice. Panic is a gear spinning without load. Give it work."

## S23 Opening Use

The party approaches from the south near tenth bell. Lysandro is not "on duty" in the official sense. He is on the wall because Hester's packet made the night serious and because Nera Coalstep asked for watchers faster than Salla Vey could write authorizations.

He is explaining the peace-watch model when the party arrives:

1. Mireward has walls because old families bled to make this road livable.
2. Peace made the town less formal, not helpless.
3. No one needs a crown badge to hold a lantern, count wagons, or wake a neighbor.
4. The north is wrong tonight, so everyone does the small useful thing before asking who is allowed to do it.

Then he sees Lysandra. The father lands first; the watchman never leaves.

## GM Notes

Lysandro should make the players feel that Mireward's looseness is not weakness. It is a civic culture that has forgotten how much pressure it can bear. His job in the opening is to embody the best version of that culture just before the north tests it.
