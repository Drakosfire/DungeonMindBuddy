# Brin Holloway — character seed

**Role:** **Ostensible lead** of the **Edge support column** — civilians who marched north from Mireward weeks ago to prove the town still cared about Edge after years of embarrassing tax defiance. Now the exhausted vanguard fleeing south ahead of a **meat-monster flank** not needed at the main siege.

**Soap in one breath:** Used to be loud at charter meetings — *"Sky not spires, and we won't pay to arm a ghost fort."* Organized the relief train when Edge started looking **actually** run-down and Mirathorn stopped sending maintenance coin. Feels personally responsible for every person still walking and every person they left behind.

**Voice:** Fast when panicked, plain when ashamed. Quotes head-counts wrong because he keeps recounting the missing: **58** at the last mile marker, **51** still answering to their names, **fifty-odd** when he stops pretending the categories hold. Not a soldier; a **convincer** who became a march-captain by default.

**Look:** Road mud to the knees; banner staff broken but still tied to a cart; ink on fingers from relief ledgers; one sleeve torn where he dragged someone off a ditch lip.

**What he knows:** Edge is **pinned**. A **large band of meat-things** — too many for the siege line to bother corralling — is **herding** stragglers south. The flank is **minutes** behind the column, not hours. About **half** the column shows **glassy or worsening eyes**. Those who fall behind are not simply killed — witnesses describe **joining** the herd, standing up wrong, walking north again.

**What he needs from Mireward:** Open the **north gate** or the **ferry fallback**; beds for the clear-eyed; **chains or mercy** for the worsening; someone who believes him before the monsters round the bend.

**Table lines:**

- "We didn't go to fight. We went to cook, mend, tally, pray — because this town stopped paying for the wall and still wanted to feel righteous."
- "Don't look at their eyes if you love them. Half of us are still in there. The other half are watching themselves leave."
- "Not a few minutes. Minutes. We ran the last mile."
- "Fifty-eight when we crossed the last mile marker. Fifty-one if you only count the ones still answering to their names. Fifty-odd if you want the truth fast."
- "The things behind us aren't the whole army. They're what the siege didn't need. That's what makes this worse."
