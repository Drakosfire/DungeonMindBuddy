## A. One-sentence pitch (draft)

**Mireward is the last walled town on the Mireward Reach before the fen — a crown tithe-and-ferry knot where ex-city folk, stink-trades, and retired spears built a life on sky and road… and right now the garrison is thin, the festival has pulled the young south, and the north is filling the haylofts faster than anyone thought possible.**

---

## A2. Who lives here — synthesis *(locked sketch)*

**Pruned:** pure drove-rent monoculture, open-range ranching, pond-as-only reason, fen commuter villages, second-son charter romance, gossip-post spy towns as primary identity. Those happen **around** Mireward; they are not the town’s soul.

**Kept (mixed):** garrison culture · guild-dropout crafts · deliberate not-Mirathorn identity · festival/pilgrimage spillover.

### The baseline attitude

Reach folk are mostly **less city, not less civilized**. Mirathorn is lake, river, plains, and **crowd**. Out here: **grass, Stormspire west, road north**, charter bell, visible horizon. Generations grew up hearing that the **last time the edge of reality really troubled Elderwyld** was **long ago** — old songs, not living memory. Safety feels **earned and boring**, not fragile. That complacency is doing real work when the north breaks.

### Four legs of “why Mireward”

| Leg | Who it produces | What they get |
|-----|-----------------|---------------|
| **Garrison & crown service** | Retired spears, half-pay sergeants, patrol widows, tithe clerks, ferry guards | Pension plot, bell, charter protection, **meaning without spires** |
| **Stink trades** | Tanners, charcoal burners, lime kilners, fullers, dyers | Space, no guild master at the window; **worse temple, better air** |
| **Not-Mirathorn identity** | Lundayell-grandchild homesteaders, “sky not spires” families, boring proud human hamlets | Story, distance from density; **tolerance as regional self-image** (Orin’s kin — §F2) |
| **Festival & road spillover** | Innkeepers, shrine tenders, wagon-yard hosts, sleep-before-walk camps | Seasonal coin without city rent; **cheerful apron when the north is bleeding** |

### The garrison *(not rot — thin by coincidence)*

- Mireward has a **real palisade**, **real armory**, **real patrol culture** — not a ruin with a flag.
- **Patrols run weeks to months** depending on unit and task: fen-line ride, tithe escort, mile-marker and ditch audit, ferry watch, `[ long patrol name TBD ]`.
- **Right now (S21–22):** **Festival of Expansion** recalled a **slice of the garrison to Mirathorn** — young spears, show uniforms, city crowd control. What’s left is **competent but short-handed**: veterans, locals, a few green kids who drew the unglamorous post.
- **Also:** a **patrol or two is still out** on the long rotation — expected back **`[ window TBD ]`**, not yet overdue enough to panic. Combined with festival pull = **understaffed by coincidence + over-trust**, not abandonment.
- **Feel at the gate:** “We’re fine. The lads are in the city singing. The patrol knows the fen. It’s been quiet for **years**.”

### Who you meet (types, not a census)

- **Sergeant-emeritus types** — bought a plot with mustering-out coin; still read the wall walk every dawn; annoyed the festival took “the quick ones.”
- **Patrol widows & kin** — bell, shrine, and **The Last Dry Bed** tier; garrison is family memory, not abstract crown.
- **Downwind crafters** — smell honest; trade with drovers; **vote against city guilds** in conversation, not in elections (there aren’t any).
- **Homesteaders who left Mirathorn on purpose** — not refugees; **chose** charter noise over spire noise; Orin’s family sits here: **we don’t judge folk for being different** (Lundayell memory filtered through Reach pride).
- **Apron folk** — inns, ferry steps, tithe scale; **road looks profitable** from the south; they’re not lying, they’re **lagging indicators**.
- **New crush (refugees)** — not who Mireward *was*; who it’s **becoming** this month. See §B.

### Table read (one paragraph)

*Mireward is a pension town with a bell tower and a ferry — retired spears, stink-trades, and people who wanted sky instead of spires. The wall still stands; the patrols still ride; it’s just that the festival has the young guards in Mirathorn singing, one long patrol is still out on the fen rotation, and nobody alive remembers the last time the weird north really came for them. The road economy still looks fine from the apron. The haylofts tell a different story.*

## B. Why Mireward is packed (refugee logic)

| Pressure | At the table |
|----------|----------------|
| **Geography** | Last **named town** before open fen / swamp. Hamlets don’t count — people want walls, wells, a sheriff. |
| **Direction of flight** | Crisis is **north and fen-ward** (music, abandoned holds, storm, bad water). Refugees move **into** Mireward, not **through** toward Mirathorn. |
| **Road foreshadow** | Reach d100: abandoned hamlet (81–100), northbound refugees when mercy says south (11–20), Shepherd’s Shadow (51–60) — Mireward is where beats **accumulate**. |
| **Storm** | Open Reach is exposed; wagon folk **pile into town** between squalls (Session 21 storm building). |
| **Capacity failure** | Built for **tithe + ferry + Reach overnight**, not **3× population**. Hay in chapel, families in tithe barn, well queues. |

**Refugee mix (weight later):**

- Fen-edge farmers and reed-cutters  
- Hamlet folk from **north** of Mireward (closer than Edge of the World)  
- A few **southbound stragglers** who stopped here  
- **Not** a organized Mirathorn exodus (reserve for another beat)

**Emotional texture (locked with knob #2):** Crowd fear is **logistical** (no beds, no grain) layered with **northbound dread** (empty hamlets, bad water, wrong songs). At human scale, **The Last Dry Bed** family models **Lundayell-style non-judgment** toward Orin — which may be **compassionate and dangerously wrong** at once. See §F2.

## C. Why word hasn’t made it south

South isn’t ignorant because nobody cares; south is **structurally deaf**.

### C1. Nobody fleeing south carries the story

Refugees aren’t running toward Mirathorn. Gossip engine needs **traffic meeting traffic** — asymmetric flow = asymmetric news.

### C2. Festival gravity at Mirathorn (strengthened)

| Mechanism | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Festival resumed (S21)** | Celebration over crisis reporting; “we won the forest” narrative. |
| **Labor drain** | Drovers, guards, bards, clerks in the spires — **Mireward garrison youth recalled for festival duty** (§A2). |
| **Messenger priority** | Couriers carry festival freight and jerky-scandal paper, not fen headcounts. |
| **Comms overload** | Sara/Frank overwhelmed; Frank hung up on Caelynn; north callbacks fail too. |
| **Outtown inward turn** | Unrest, cult whispers — listening to road rumor is not the mood. |
| **Who’s left** | Mossford was **celebrating** when party passed; Marr had storm + Edge-of-the-World music rumor, not “how’s Mireward?” |

### C3. Mossford heard a parallel rumor

Marr: **Edge of the World** (~week north), cult **music** — different informant path (river-warden seed). Mireward’s crisis is **capacity + fen displacement**, not yet “music at the gate.”

### C4. Physical choke

Storm, mud, drake-nest detour — **days of delay** before a coherent report reaches Mossford.

### C5. Optional GM truth (knob — undecided)

Someone **did** report upstream and was ignored/buried — festival press, requisition officer, or sympathetic clerk. **Not required** for first visit.

**Table one-liner:** *The people who knew didn’t go south; the people who go south didn’t stay to count; and Mirathorn is too busy singing to ask.*

## F2. Anchor NPC — swamp escapee at the family inn *(working sketch; expand on the fly)*

**Purpose:** Human-scale knob for **knob #2** — the town’s fear is a **stack** (capacity, fen, meat/music undertone), but the **scene the table remembers** is this family misreading horror as difference.

### Working names *(rename freely at table)*

| Slot | Working name | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Inn | **The Last Dry Bed** | Reach overnight; “last hot meal before the smell.” Full rooms; family lives on-site. |
| Innkeeper (public face) | **Maera Vell** | Runs bar/floor; tired; protective. |
| Family on-site | **Maera + `[ sibling or spouse TBD ]`** | Own the inn; not villains — **wrong lens**, not malice. |
| Swamp escapee | **Orin Vell** | Maera’s **`[ kin relation TBD — brother? cousin? ]`**. Escaped fen/swamp community; **half-crazy**; kept in **upper room / back chamber** (not chained — family thinks they’re caring for him). |

### What the family believes

- Orin **got out** of the swamp folk’s settlement (exact path **`[ TBD — walked out? smuggled? ferry? ]`**).
- His talk (muttering, half-sentences, wrong rhymes) is **travel shock**, fever, or “the fen does that to sensitive folk.”
- **Lundayell memory:** Something the empire **did well** was **not judging people who are different** — odd customs, odd diet, odd worship, odd silence. Maera’s line is closer to **Mirathorn’s founding myth** than to Shepherd doctrine: *we don’t turn folk away for being strange.*
- They **do not** think there was anything wrong with the swamp community as a whole. Outsiders calling it a cult is, to them, the same prejudice their grandparents fled.

### What Orin might be (leave open — pick in play)

- **Garbled truth:** Saw meat-pit work, hymn rehearsals, or ferry traffic; can’t hold a linear story.
- **Wrong target:** Describes **Raucous Saints** or **Edge-of-the-World music** rumor, not the deep swamp.
- **Real cult exposure:** Touches Maelthor/Shepherd imagery; family explains it away as “bad water dreams.”
- **Expand hooks:** `[ letter in boot ]` `[ name he repeats ]` `[ scar / smell ]` `[ who helped him cross the causeway ]`

### Scene affordances (no prep lock-in)

- Party hears **singing through the floorboards** — family says Orin “always had a voice.”
- Maera offers **floor space** if party vouches; asks them **not to upset Orin** — “he’s been through enough.”
- Sheriff **`[ TBD ]`** may want to **question** Orin; family refuses; town split on **sanctuary vs suspicion**.
- **Contrast:** Refugee barn screams **capacity**; inn screams **denial** — both true at once.
