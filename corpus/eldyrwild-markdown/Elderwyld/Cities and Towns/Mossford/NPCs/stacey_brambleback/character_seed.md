# Stacey Brambleback — character seed (Mossford)

Pre–player-contact framing for **Stacey Brambleback**: about 11–12, bugbear, local child menace and sideways survivalist. She is not evil—she is sharp, physical, too clever, and learned early that if she controls the tone, nobody gets to laugh at her first. She is already becoming a town type: rude, practical, fast-thinking, quietly scared, determined not to show it.

**Appearance.** Wiry and long-limbed; shaggy russet-brown fur; large amber eyes; already tall for her age; patched practical clothes worn with total confidence; usually leans instead of standing “normally”; moves with unsettling bugbear quiet.

**Vibe at the table.** Unimpressed stare; appears beside people instead of approaching; talks like she is doing everyone a favor by explaining reality; always seems to know more than she should.

**What she does.** Startles younger kids for fun; steals or “relocates” useful supplies; knows hidden paths, sheds, crawlspaces, and roof access; has been building a secret survival cache during the crisis.

**Secret competence.** While adults argue, she organizes child-scale emergency thinking: lantern oil, flint, old bread, blanket, sling stones, maybe smoked fish or dried meat, hiding spots for younger children if the bridge clogs. That makes her useful, not just comic.

**What she wants.** To not be treated like a child; to look brave in front of the other kids; to prove she sees the crisis more clearly than most adults; to never admit she is scared.

**What she might know (GM hooks—confirm against recap).** Prep suggested she saw unusual activity near the **west boundary stones** (Lysandra, mud drawing, warnings not to cross markers). **Session 20 at the table did not surface that clue** (Bonogo went confrontational; Stacey ran home shaken). Treat the west-stones beat as **optional future hook**, not asserted table canon, until a recap or dossier pins it.

**Stuart (relationship).** Surface: she bullies him—startles, tricks, makes him carry things, talks over him, humiliates him in front of other kids. He dreads her, reacts visibly, complains honestly, and still ends up in her orbit. Deeper: she does not actually hate Stuart; she has decided he is “hers” to push around before anybody meaner gets to him—half torment, half hardening him up for the world. That does not make it kind; it makes it believable. If another child humiliates Stuart too hard, Stacey is likely to step in—not gently, not kindly, framed as standards or territory rather than protection.
