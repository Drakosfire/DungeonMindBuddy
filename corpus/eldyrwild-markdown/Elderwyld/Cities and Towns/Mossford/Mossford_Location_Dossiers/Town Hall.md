## Town Hall

Mayor’s offices, records rooms, and public chamber

# At a Glance

Town Hall is where Mossford turns opinions into policy and problems into paper. Compared to Mirathorn it is smaller, humbler, and less performative, but it still carries the quiet theatricality of public authority: long tables, stamped documents, deliberately measured speech, and the uneasy understanding that water and labor both ignore bad policy eventually.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The hall smells of drying paper, lamp soot, sealing wax, wet coats, and reed-pulp folders. Boots leave damp prints in the entrance even when the weather is fair. Voices remain low here, but the building carries them in a whispery way that makes private tension feel public long before anyone raises their voice.

# Physical Description

The building is one of the taller civic structures in town: a broad central chamber under a steep roof, side offices tucked under dormers, and a square stair tower that allows clerks to feel slightly more official than they are. The public chamber has a raised speaking floor and long benches instead of padded seats. A relief map of the reservoir and paddies dominates one wall, updated by hand whenever a channel, ferry line, or field boundary changes. The records rooms smell better than most archives because every clerk here is at war with mildew.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Mayor Sareth Mosscale — Mayor of Mossford

**Appearance: **An older dragonborn with green-bronze scales, a polished cane carried more as punctuation than support, and gold eyes that settle on nonsense like weight on thin ice.

**Smell: **Dry paper, clove oil, clean wool, and the resin he uses on the cane handle.

**Voice: **Measured, resonant, and patient by choice rather than temperament. He speaks in pauses that make other people reveal more than intended.

**Attitude: **Pragmatic, unsentimental, and allergic to waste—of time, labor, or political theatre.

**Mannerisms: **Waits half a beat before answering foolish questions; taps his cane once when a discussion has crossed from disagreement into waste; studies maps while others posture.

**What they want right now: **To prepare the town for bad outcomes without triggering the kind of public fear that makes them arrive faster.

**Play them as: **Quiet authority. He is never flustered in public; irritation appears as increased precision.

**Useful private note: **He keeps a private risk map marking the families least likely to evacuate and the roads most likely to fail socially before they fail structurally.

**Sample dialogue: **“I do not mind disagreement. I mind waste. Distinguish them before you continue.”

• “A town survives by planning for the day after pride becomes inconvenient.”

• “If you have come to impress me, don’t. If you have come to help, begin.”

## Olli Fen — Chief clerk

**Appearance: **A narrow-faced human man with tidy brown hair, ink-shadowed fingers, and the pale indoor look of someone who has won a long war against mildew and nearly no sunlight.

**Smell: **Dry paper, sealing wax, lamp soot, and mint lozenges.

**Voice: **Precise, nasal, and increasingly clipped the closer a situation comes to becoming administrative chaos.

**Attitude: **Fussy, competent, and genuinely comforted by forms, ledgers, and orderly margins.

**Mannerisms: **Adds columns when stressed; aligns quills before meetings; repeats requests back in a cleaner version than he received them.

**What they want right now: **To keep Town Hall legible while everyone else treats it like a place where feelings become policy by volume.

**Play them as: **Brittle competence with a hidden heroic streak. He looks trivial until you realize the whole machine runs on him.

**Useful private note: **He has noticed permit slowdowns that do not match the mayor’s public reasoning and is too cautious to ask directly.

**Sample dialogue: **“Yes, there is a form for that. No, you may not improve it.”

• “Chaos is not faster just because it is louder.”

• “Please start again with dates, names, and only one emergency at a time.”

## Constable Meret Rusk — Town constable

**Appearance: **A broad-shouldered half-elf with sun-brown skin, practical boots, braided dark hair, and a face built for patience until that patience ends completely.

**Smell: **Oiled leather, damp coats, road dust, and the bitter tea she drinks instead of sleeping enough.

**Voice: **Low and even, with the controlled warmth of someone who would rather settle a thing quietly than make a public display of it.

**Attitude: **Steady, skeptical, and deeply uninterested in dramatic lawkeeping.

**Mannerisms: **Leans on doorframes rather than desks; watches hands before faces; rubs the bridge of her nose when people mistake arrest for problem-solving.

**What they want right now: **To keep disputes from becoming incidents while deciding how much of the current unease is normal town friction and how much is not.

**Play them as: **Calm authority with hidden speed. She asks direct questions and remembers evasions better than answers.

**Useful private note: **She suspects someone withdrew a night-traffic complaint under pressure rather than by second thought.

**Sample dialogue: **“Most crime is impatience wearing boots.”

• “I prefer a solved problem to an impressive arrest.”

• “Tell me what happened, not what you hope the paperwork says happened.”

# What This Place Offers

Work permits, market stall assignments, bridge repair notices, burial permissions, flood claims, public hearings, and dispute mediation between farms, ferry crews, and moldyard contractors.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• The mayor is quietly consulting irrigation maps at hours he normally reserves for public callers.

• Permit approvals for new south-bank expansion have slowed without an official explanation.

• Someone filed a complaint about unusual night traffic on the bridge but withdrew it before morning.

# Hidden Tension

The mayor keeps a second wall map in his private office showing not property boundaries but risk zones: unstable ground, politically sensitive families, flood-failure scenarios, and the farm clusters least likely to evacuate without coercion.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A file goes missing from the records room, but only pages referencing an old flood relocation.

▸ Sareth asks neutral outsiders to inspect a reported breach because he does not fully trust whose interests are tied to it.

▸ A sealed packet from Mirathorn arrives and the mayor reads it twice before locking his office.

# Running the Location

Use Town Hall when you want Mossford to feel governed by weary competence rather than glamour. The stakes here are often social, material, and immediate.
