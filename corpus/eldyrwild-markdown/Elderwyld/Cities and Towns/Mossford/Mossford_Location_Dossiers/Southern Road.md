## Southern Road

The worked route through the paddies toward Mirathorn

# At a Glance

The Southern Road begins at the Stone Bridge and threads through the south-bank farms in a raised, carefully maintained route before continuing toward Mirathorn and the wider trade world. It is not merely a road; it is a moving line of labor, gossip, produce, carts, patrols, and weather reports carried on wheels and boots.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The road smells of trampled reeds, wagon grease, wet earth, horse leather, and cut greenery left too long in the sun. The air opens up here; sound carries further over the waterlogged fields. You hear frogs, wheels, distant workers, and the slap of irrigation gates being opened or shut. On hot days the road gives off a baked mineral smell that does not exist ten feet off it in the wet ground.

# Physical Description

Raised slightly above the paddies on a hard-packed and often re-graveled base, the road is bordered by drainage cuts, narrow farm spurs, and occasional pull-outs where loaded carts can pass. Small marker posts show distance not in miles but in work units and water turns. The road itself is visually ordinary, but the life around it never is: reed hats moving through green plots, ferries easing along side channels, children carrying lunch bundles, and wagon trains with the town’s economy strapped visibly to them.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Rul Dane — Road warden

**Appearance: **A quiet human official with sun-creased skin, a patched road cloak, and boots permanently gritted with white dust from the causeway.

**Smell: **Gravel dust, wagon grease, dry reeds, and the tar used on marker posts.

**Voice: **Low and level, with the patience of someone who has spent years saying practical things to excitable people.

**Attitude: **Stoic, dutiful, and suspicious of any damage that arrives without a story attached.

**Mannerisms: **Studies road shoulders more than faces; crouches to touch ruts while thinking; carries a measuring cord like a priest might carry beads.

**What they want right now: **To decide whether recent slumping at the road edge is ordinary wear or a sign the land beneath is shifting in ways the maps won’t like.

**Play them as: **Quiet gravity. He feels like a man who speaks rarely because he works in consequences.

**Useful private note: **He keeps one old spur road off the public charts because every time it is cleared, it leads someone curious to the wrong kind of old stone.

**Sample dialogue: **“A good road is proof that people believed tomorrow would arrive.”

• “Ruts are history written by weight. Learn to read them.”

• “If the shoulder gives way once, I repair a road. If it gives way twice, I ask the land what it wants.”

## Issa Fenbar — Carter and farm broker

**Appearance: **A broad-smiling dragonborn with yellow-brown scales, heavy bracelets, polished cart hooks at the belt, and the relaxed confidence of someone who makes a living knowing who needs what before they say it.

**Smell: **Horse leather, cut greenery, coin, and sun-warmed paddy water.

**Voice: **Rich, quick, and charming in a practical rather than elegant way. She sounds like profit with good manners.

**Attitude: **Personable, opportunistic, and genuinely helpful so long as help and margin can coexist.

**Mannerisms: **Counts on claw tips; pats loaded wagons affectionately; leans too close when sealing a deal, as if warmth itself were part of the contract.

**What they want right now: **To position herself where she can profit from consolidation without being blamed for knowing it was coming.

**Play them as: **A cheerful broker whose charm is real, not fake—she simply aims it where it works best.

**Useful private note: **She already knows which farm families are quietly moving goods north instead of sending them downroad.

**Sample dialogue: **“Everything becomes road business eventually. Grain, grief, marriage, panic—put wheels under it and there I am.”

• “If it’s late, I can move it. If it’s delicate, I can still move it. If it’s both, pay me properly.”

• “The road tells you what a town fears by what it rushes first.”

## Mikel Thorn — Bridge-to-road patrol rider

**Appearance: **A lean half-elf with a hard seat in the saddle, sun-faded cloak, and eyes always scanning ahead of the horse rather than around it.

**Smell: **Horse sweat, wet grass, oiled tack, and the tannic bite of strong roadside tea.

**Voice: **Short, controlled, and built for message carrying. He speaks in finished facts, not atmospheric concerns.

**Attitude: **Urgent, wary, and professionally impatient with obstacles, excuses, and slow listeners.

**Mannerisms: **Checks straps before dismounting; stands as though still riding; looks over his shoulder when giving bad news, as if measuring whether he’ll have to carry the next version too.

**What they want right now: **To keep the line between farms and town open long enough to move warning faster than fear.

**Play them as: **Taut and mobile. He isn’t rude; he simply has no spare room for social drift.

**Useful private note: **He has ridden stretches of intact road that no longer feel empty, and he hates not having a reportable explanation for that.

**Sample dialogue: **“If I’m riding hard, make room. If I’m riding harder, start asking what failed.”

• “The road ahead is open. That is not the same as safe.”

• “I deliver news, I don’t decorate it.”

# What This Place Offers

Cart passage, official notice relay, distance markers, maintenance crews, and access to farm clusters and southern branch tracks.

# Town News & Rumor

• A section of the road shoulder slumped into the paddy edge and was repaired before dawn so most travelers never saw it.

• More loaded carts are heading north into town than south out of it, suggesting people are consolidating goods rather than dispersing them.

• A farm matriarch claims the road posts on the eastern stretch are subtly misaligned, as if the land itself shifted between measurements.

# Hidden Tension

One old spur road, now mostly swallowed by fieldwork, still leads toward a stone foundation long predating Mossford. Rul knows it is there and keeps it off the public maintenance charts because every time labor crews clear it, someone starts asking dangerous old questions.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A whole train of carts stops moving because the lead ox refuses a stretch of perfectly sound road.

▸ A maintenance crew uncovers old dressed stone under the roadbed where there should only be fill.

▸ A rider arrives with mud to the saddle and a warning that the road ahead is intact but no longer feels empty.

# Running the Location

The Southern Road is movement made visible. Use it to show the scale of Mossford’s working life and to transition cleanly from town tension into field tension.
