## Stoneweir Dam

The Mosswall and Sluice Works

# At a Glance

Stoneweir Dam, called the Mosswall by locals, is the piece of infrastructure that made Mossford possible. It turns an unruly river into schedules, harvests, curing cycles, ferry lanes, and wages. Every district downstream depends on decisions made here, and every argument in town eventually traces itself back to a gate, a flow chart, or a bad season at the sluices.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The dam is louder than it looks. Water hammers the spillway with a deep, chest-felt roar while gate chains groan and vibrate through the stone. Everything smells of wet rock, iron, tannin, and algae. The spray leaves a cold mineral taste on the lips. Workers here always have damp cuffs and a permanent ring of river grit on their boots.

# Physical Description

From a distance the dam reads as a hard line of stone and timber bridging the valley throat. Up close it is a layered piece of old engineering: buttressed walls, inspection walkways, counterweighted gate towers, spill channels, emergency boards, and maintenance stairs slick with spray. Mosswood reinforcements have been bolted into later repairs, their dark green-brown grain visible against the older gray stone. Flood notches and chalk flow notes cover many interior surfaces because the workers trust writing where they can see it more than plans stored safely in drawers.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Harvek Sluice — Chief sluicekeeper

**Appearance: **A barrel-chested dwarf with a crushed old nose, a beard braided tight to keep it out of gears, and forearms thick from wrestling chains and gate levers.

**Smell: **Wet iron, tannin, algae, and the raw river grit that never quite leaves his boots.

**Voice: **Booming, abrasive, and curiously musical when he talks to machinery instead of people.

**Attitude: **Blunt, territorial, and deeply competent. He respects courage, precision, and anyone who admits they don’t understand the works.

**Mannerisms: **Swears at gates like livestock; spits over the spillway before hard decisions; taps stone with knuckles while listening for changes in echo.

**What they want right now: **To hold reservoir levels low enough for safety without causing a panic among the paddy foremen.

**Play them as: **A gruff engineer-priest of water pressure. He insults people as punctuation, not necessarily hostility.

**Useful private note: **He knows one buried intake tunnel is not as buried as the plans insist.

**Sample dialogue: **“The river isn’t angry. Anger implies judgment. This is just force with bad manners.”

• “If you step where I tell you not to, the water will educate you faster than I can.”

• “People think dams are walls. They’re arguments held shut with math.”

## Praska Nettlegear — Maintenance engineer

**Appearance: **A compact gnome in oilcloth sleeves, chalk-smudged cheeks, and protective lenses pushed up into wild hair. Her fingers are always nicked and ink-stained.

**Smell: **Machine oil, chalk, wet rope, and the bitter acid note of metal filings.

**Voice: **Quick, crisp, and needling. She talks fastest when she has already solved the problem and is now offended that others have not caught up.

**Attitude: **Exacting, impatient, and secretly delighted by difficult mechanical puzzles.

**Mannerisms: **Counts vibrations under her breath; scratches measurements onto any dry surface; fixes people with a withering stare when they say ‘normal’ too casually.

**What they want right now: **To confirm whether the latest vibration pattern is wear, sabotage, or something even more expensive.

**Play them as: **Fast and fussy, but never foolish. She is sharp because the dam punishes vagueness.

**Useful private note: **She kept the warped replacement gate-pin instead of returning it because she suspects it was made wrong on purpose.

**Sample dialogue: **“No, that vibration is not normal. It is merely familiar.”

• “If you hear a new sound and ignore it, congratulations, you’ve joined management.”

• “Stand back unless you can distinguish a problem from a symptom.”

## Leth Aru — Reservoir skiff pilot

**Appearance: **A lean half-elf with sun-dark skin, river-burned hands, and a habit of leaning like the deck under him is always moving.

**Smell: **Cold water, tar, damp leather, and reed smoke from shore lunches.

**Voice: **Easy, low, and deceptively casual. He sounds relaxed even when saying things that should make people less so.

**Attitude: **Watchful, diplomatic, and professionally discreet. He knows secrets because he rows people who assume the water is louder than it is.

**Mannerisms: **Keeps one hand on a coil of line at all times; answers questions after a measured pause; glances toward shore whenever someone lies badly.

**What they want right now: **To decide whether the unusual requests for private skiff passage are merely political or something darker.

**Play them as: **Laconic and sly. He never volunteers a full truth until he knows whether you’ll respect it.

**Useful private note: **He has overheard two separate officials give incompatible reasons for the reservoir being held low.

**Sample dialogue: **“You learn a town best by listening to what people say when they think the water is too loud to carry it.”

• “I row. I notice. I do not gossip cheaply.”

• “If you want me nervous, ask for a night crossing and pretend it’s routine.”

# What This Place Offers

Passage across the top works requires permission. Locals can request emergency water adjustments, flood advisories, reservoir skiff transport for official business, and written flow statements used by moldyards and paddies alike.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• The reservoir has been held half a foot lower than usual, and no one outside the works has been told why.

• A replacement gate-pin arrived warped from a supplier downriver; Praska says it was incompetence, Harvek says it was sabotage, and both men seem pleased to argue.

• There is talk of reinforcing the east release channels because the paddies are drawing harder than expected for the season.

# Hidden Tension

Harvek knows that one old intake tunnel under the western abutment still exists, though it was supposedly filled decades ago. It is too small for carts but large enough for a determined person who does not mind cold black water.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A gate opens during the wrong watch and no one on duty admits touching the release chain.

▸ An inspection hammer rings hollow on one section of the western wall.

▸ A body turns up caught in the trash racks with no sign of how it entered the reservoir.

# Running the Location

The dam should feel competent, dangerous, and essential. It is where calm professionals argue in precise language while standing next to enough force to erase a neighborhood.
