## Copper Moss Brewery & Waterwheel Mill

Brewhouse, mill, and social hinge of the north bank

# At a Glance

Copper Moss Brewery is part brewhouse, part mill complex, part public institution. Grain comes here to be cracked, moss sugar comes here to be tested, and laborers come here to remember that a town built on wet work needs dry humor. It is one of Mossford’s most beloved businesses because it makes money, makes drink, and makes the river sound briefly cheerful.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The place smells gloriously alive: mash steam, wet grain, hot copper, yeast, toasted hulls, wood smoke, and the sweet-green smell of rendered moss syrup. The waterwheel thumps through the floorboards in a steady rhythm that regulars claim they can hear even in their dreams. In the taproom the air carries nut-brown ale, onion broth, wet cloaks, and a little river fog every time the door opens.

# Physical Description

The complex straddles riverwork and commerce. The mill house juts over the millrace where the wheel turns in a spray of constant glittering runoff. Attached to it by a timber arcade is the brewhouse proper: copper kettles, steeping racks, cooling troughs, and a taproom with long benches rubbed pale by generations of elbows. Carved beam-ends shaped like curled moss fronds hang under the eaves. A black board behind the bar lists what is pouring, what ran out, and what batch went strange in an amusing rather than lethal way.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Jorren Coppermoss — Owner-brewer

**Appearance: **A red-bearded dwarf with burnished cheeks, a stained leather apron, bright suspicious eyes, and the kind of forearms that say he still hauls his own grain when annoyed.

**Smell: **Mash steam, copper, yeast, onion broth, and a little sweet moss syrup clinging to his cuffs.

**Voice: **Big, rolling, and joyful until someone insults his water, at which point it turns razor precise.

**Attitude: **Generous, loud, proud, and exacting about quality. He loves workers, hates waste, and treats sloppy brewing as moral failure.

**Mannerisms: **Judges people by how they hold a mug; sniffs grain sacks before greeting suppliers; laughs with his whole chest when genuinely pleased.

**What they want right now: **To figure out why one batch tastes faintly metallic before it ruins his reputation or proves something worse about the town’s inputs.

**Play them as: **Booming warmth over a core of uncompromising standards. He is fun until the subject becomes beer, water, or honest labor.

**Useful private note: **He keeps a rack of experimental storm-brewed casks under lock because one of them seemed to sharpen dreams.

**Sample dialogue: **“Bad water makes worse ale. Bad grain makes philosophy. Drink the first, avoid the second.”

• “If you want polite beer, drink somewhere else. If you want good beer, sit down.”

• “No one lies successfully in a brewhouse. The steam strips dignity off first.”

## Pela Reedbarrel — Cellarmaster

**Appearance: **A copper-scaled dragonborn woman with a scarred snout, immaculate sleeves, and the poised economy of movement of someone who can cross a slick cellar in the dark without spilling a drop.

**Smell: **Cool stone, oak casks, mineral ale foam, and the faint pepper of cellar mold kept just barely in check.

**Voice: **Dry, level, and controlled. She never sounds hurried, which makes drunk people assume incorrectly that they have more room than they do.

**Attitude: **Unflappable, sternly fair, and impossible to bully. She prefers order but enjoys watching fools reveal themselves.

**Mannerisms: **Counts mugs by glance alone; wipes condensation rings before they spread; cocks one brow ridge when someone tries charming her past a limit.

**What they want right now: **To keep the taproom orderly while quietly tracking which strangers are drinking for courage rather than pleasure.

**Play them as: **Minimal motion, maximal control. She wins social contests by refusing to rush.

**Useful private note: **She already knows which regulars would help barricade the brewery if the town panicked.

**Sample dialogue: **“You may have another. You may not have two more. I know the difference.”

• “Drink slower. I’m not saying it for your sake.”

• “I do not cut people off. I redirect them toward better decisions.”

## Nim Vatch — Mill boy and gossip courier

**Appearance: **A wiry human teenager with long wrists, patched trousers, perpetually damp boots, and a grin that appears exactly when he decides you’ve underestimated him.

**Smell: **Flour dust, river water, lampblack, and the apples he steals one slice at a time.

**Voice: **Quick, bright, and innocent enough to be dangerous. He can sound younger than he is when it profits him.

**Attitude: **Curious, opportunistic, and shamelessly pleased by secrets.

**Mannerisms: **Sweeps while listening; pretends to mishear names so they get repeated; perches on flour sacks like a crow on a fence rail.

**What they want right now: **To turn overheard fragments into enough social value that adults finally stop calling him ‘boy’ when they need something.

**Play them as: **Fast-eyed and disarming. He is never still in thought even when standing still in body.

**Useful private note: **He has noticed someone buying cheap meal in bulk and has not yet decided whether to tell Jorren, sell the fact, or follow the wagon himself.

**Sample dialogue: **“I do not eavesdrop. I work near conversations.”

• “People tell the truth while waiting for sacks to fill. You’d be amazed.”

• “If it’s urgent, say so. If it’s secret, say that too. Those cost different amounts.”

# What This Place Offers

Taproom, cooked meals, small party bookings, mill services for local grain, and cask sales for events. It is also an informal job board for work crews, bargemen, and out-of-town traders who would rather talk over a mug than in the square.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• The brewer swears this year’s moss sugar has a metallic aftertaste that is not dangerous, merely insulting.

• A wagon from Mirathorn overpaid for two full casks and asked too many questions about shelf life.

• Nim says someone has been buying cheap meal in unusual volume, which might mean refugees, smugglers, or someone planning a wedding badly.

# Hidden Tension

Jorren keeps a tiny locked rack of experimental brews made with rare moss strains and odd weatherwater collected during storms. Most are simply unusual. One or two are too interesting to serve publicly.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A fresh batch tastes wrong to Jorren, but no one else can yet explain why.

▸ Someone steals the brewery’s flow slate from the mill office and leaves the cashbox untouched.

▸ A patron swears the wheel stopped by itself for three breaths and restarted turning backward.

# Running the Location

Use the brewery for warmth, noise, labor politics, and grounded information. It is the place where a town explains itself without realizing it.
