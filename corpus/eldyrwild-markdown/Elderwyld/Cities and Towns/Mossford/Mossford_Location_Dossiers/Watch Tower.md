## Watch Tower

Old Signal Tower of Mossford

# At a Glance

The Watch Tower sits on Old Settlement Hill above the north bank, a leftover spine of the town’s first defensive era. In calmer years it served as a flood marker, beacon post, and storm watch. In anxious years it becomes the first place people look when the river rises, the paddies fail, or the forest starts behaving like it has intentions.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The air is colder up here than in the town below. Moss-damp wind slides over stone, carrying the smell of river silt, wet reeds, lamp oil, and distant smoke from the drying barns. Iron bells give off a dull mineral smell when rain is near. The tower interior always smells faintly of old rope, dust, and a dozen years of storms trapped in the wood.

# Physical Description

The tower is squat, practical, and older than almost anything else in Mossford. It rises from a ring of dressed stone, then narrows into a timber upper stage with shutters on all four sides. Marks chiseled into the outer wall show historic flood heights; locals touch the highest one for luck and the second-highest one for caution. The top platform holds a signal brazier, a bell, wind-streamers, a brass tube for sighting the river, and a simple slate board where each day’s wind and water conditions are recorded.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Edda Pikewind — Senior weather-reader

**Appearance: **A spare, white-haired dwarf woman in layered oilcloth, with pale brows, weather-cracked hands, and a face carved into careful lines by decades of watching horizons. Her left eye squints more than the right from a lifetime of sighting along brass tubes.

**Smell: **Cold wool, lamp oil, wet stone, and the faint cedar note of old signal chests.

**Voice: **Dry and low, like cedar rubbed smooth with sand. She speaks in complete sentences, wastes no words, and leaves little room for interruption.

**Attitude: **Measured, unsentimental, and almost painfully honest. She has patience for fear, but none for theatrics.

**Mannerisms: **Touches flood marks with two fingers when thinking; pauses mid-conversation to check a cloud edge; writes observations before she answers questions about them.

**What they want right now: **To determine whether the river and the forest are drifting into the same bad pattern she has only seen once before.

**Play them as: **Calm, exacting, and quietly alarming. She never raises her voice; the more worried she is, the more matter-of-fact she becomes.

**Useful private note: **She believes the official water board is already too optimistic and has started giving different advice privately to people she thinks will actually listen.

**Sample dialogue: **“Clouds don’t lie. People do. Water does neither; it only arrives.”

• “If you want comfort, go to the inn. If you want a forecast, stand still and listen.”

• “When I stop checking the horizon, that is when you should begin to worry.”

## Orim Reedscale — Night bell-ringer

**Appearance: **A bronze dragonborn veteran with an old shoulder that rides slightly high, a lantern scar across the snout, and polished brass rings braided into the edge of his crest. He dresses neatly even on night watch.

**Smell: **Old leather, bell rope fiber, river grit, and the sharp medicinal rub he uses on his shoulder.

**Voice: **Warm baritone with parade-ground clarity. He can bark a warning across town, but usually speaks with the tired precision of a man who has rung too many bad bells.

**Attitude: **Disciplined, suspicious of sloppiness, and unexpectedly kind to children and insomniacs.

**Mannerisms: **Checks knots compulsively; rubs his bad shoulder before storms; taps ledger corners square against the table before opening them.

**What they want right now: **To keep the signal records clean enough that no one can later pretend they were not warned.

**Play them as: **A practical soldier in a civilian post. He hates panic, hates vague orders, and hates being made to choose between them.

**Useful private note: **He keeps a second notebook because he no longer trusts Town Hall to release tower information without softening it first.

**Sample dialogue: **“If the bell rings twice after midnight, that means flood, fire, or fools. Usually the third one.”

• “I do not need bravery from you. I need you to move when told.”

• “The tower sees things early. That’s not the same as seeing them pleasantly.”

## Tella Fen — Signal runner

**Appearance: **A rangy teenage human with wind-burned cheeks, quick gray eyes, cropped hair hacked short with a knife, and knees perpetually scabbed from taking corners too fast.

**Smell: **Rainwater, chalk dust, cheap soap, and whatever she ate while running and forgot to finish.

**Voice: **Fast, bright, breathless even when she is not winded. She clips words when excited and slows down only when speaking to someone she considers important.

**Attitude: **Eager, irreverent, and determined to be taken seriously despite being the youngest person in most rooms she enters.

**Mannerisms: **Balances on one foot while waiting; repeats key facts under her breath so she doesn’t lose them; stares openly at strangers until someone introduces them properly.

**What they want right now: **To be the first person in town who knows what is really happening and the last person people think to thank for it.

**Play them as: **Fast-talking and observant. She is not polished, but she is sharp, and she resents anyone mistaking speed for carelessness.

**Useful private note: **She knows three unofficial routes off Old Settlement Hill and uses all of them depending on which clerk she is trying to avoid.

**Sample dialogue: **“I hear everything first. I am professionally underappreciated.”

• “Was that a warning, an order, or a mayoral suggestion? They travel at different speeds.”

• “If you need a message carried, say it once and say it clean.”

# What This Place Offers

Not a public service site in the usual sense, but locals come here for flood forecasts, ferry safety opinions, and to read the water board. A successful conversation with the weather-readers can give advantage on checks involving local weather, river approach, or line-of-sight scouting around Mossford.

# Town News & Rumor

• The water marks are being checked twice a day instead of once, which means the river wardens do not trust the current flow.

• Someone reported seeing lights far out along the north bank after moonrise; Edda says they were probably lantern bugs, but she wrote the sighting down anyway.

• The bell rope snapped three tendays ago during a dry, windless evening. Old people in town insist that is never meaningless.

# Hidden Tension

Orim keeps a second notebook locked in the signal chest. It records irregular forest movement, odd lights, and anything he thinks the mayor might soften before sharing publicly. The second notebook is more alarming than the official board by a wide margin.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A lantern goes up in the tower during clear weather, something that only happens when a watcher sees movement where nothing should be moving.

▸ A page has been torn from the flood ledger: the page covering the last time the river and forest both changed course in the same season.

▸ Tella swears she saw someone on Old Settlement Hill before dawn leaving flowers under the bell—fresh, expensive flowers no one in Mossford grows.

# Running the Location

Use this location when you want a sober, practical view of the whole region. It is a place for warnings, horizon-reading, and the kind of truths that arrive before proof.
