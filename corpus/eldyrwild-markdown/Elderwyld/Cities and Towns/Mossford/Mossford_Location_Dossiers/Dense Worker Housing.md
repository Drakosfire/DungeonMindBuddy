## Dense Worker Housing

North-bank labor rows and family courts

# At a Glance

This district is where much of the town actually lives: moldyard workers, brewhouse crews, carters, paddy foremen, apprentices, widows with boarders, and whole family chains tied to one trade or another. It is not picturesque in a tourist sense, but it is deeply human, deeply local, and full of the rhythms that keep Mossford itself from becoming merely its industries.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

You smell dinner before you see half the buildings: broth, frying onions, soap, damp laundry, old timber, fish stock, and hot iron from nearby repair yards. Children run through passages that still smell faintly of yesterday’s rain. At dusk the district sounds like doors, bowls, arguments, songs half-remembered, and somebody always fixing something with too much confidence.

# Physical Description

Rows of attached or near-attached mosswood houses cluster around narrow lanes, shared courtyards, rain cisterns, and wash lines. Buildings rise two and sometimes three stories, not because the town loves density, but because dry land costs labor. Staircases cling to outer walls, windows lean over alleys, and every spare patch of light grows herbs in pots, reed baskets, or broken vats. The district looks improvised until you notice how intelligently people have bent every available surface toward use.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Old Jessa Coil — Courtyard matriarch

**Appearance: **A widowed human woman with heavy braids wound like rope around her head, a stout body gone tough rather than soft with age, and eyes that can identify a liar by shutter sound.

**Smell: **Soap, frying onions, wool dried indoors, and the rosemary she hangs over her lintel.

**Voice: **Carrying, amused, and mercilessly clear. She can scold across two courtyards without seeming to raise her voice.

**Attitude: **Protective, nosy, and locally sovereign. She sees meddling as a civic duty performed with proper technique.

**Mannerisms: **Watches from stoops while shelling something; points with her chin instead of her hand; knows who’s coming by the pattern of their knock.

**What they want right now: **To keep her block intact while deciding whether a new tenant is merely odd or truly dangerous.

**Play them as: **A queen of the everyday realm. She offers hospitality and interrogation in the same bowl.

**Useful private note: **She knows three rooftop routes and two emergency hiding places older than some marriages on the block.

**Sample dialogue: **“People say ‘mind your own business’ like business isn’t exactly what keeps a block alive.”

• “If you’re going to lie to me, at least bring bread.”

• “No child on this court vanishes alone. Remember that before you invent explanations.”

## Fenrik Moss — Paddy foreman

**Appearance: **A tired dragonborn father of four with green-gray scales, broad hands ridged from field tools, and the posture of a man whose body keeps working after his thoughts sit down.

**Smell: **Wet earth, paddy water, reed sap, and the faint sourness of clothes dried too slowly.

**Voice: **Rough, tired, and steady. He speaks like a man saving breath for work but willing to spend it on something important.

**Attitude: **Pragmatic, long-suffering, and harder to rattle than his superiors would prefer.

**Mannerisms: **Dozes standing if allowed; scratches field layouts in dust while talking; counts children by sight before he enters his own door.

**What they want right now: **To keep his crew focused on the next shift while quietly getting family members closer to town in case the fields must be abandoned fast.

**Play them as: **Exhausted competence. He is not dramatic, which makes his worry more convincing when it shows.

**Useful private note: **He has already started moving some tools and keepsakes northward without telling the mayoral office.

**Sample dialogue: **“We do not need miracles. We need three dry days and a bridge that stays where it was built.”

• “A bad season is one thing. A wrong season is another.”

• “I’ll panic later if I can fit it in before supper.”

## Lio and Pemm — Sibling runners

**Appearance: **Twin children with identical dark curls, permanently dirty knees, and faces quick enough to swap innocence and mischief without warning.

**Smell: **Sun-warmed dust, jam, wet stone, and whatever alley they were just told not to visit.

**Voice: **Lio speaks first and faster; Pemm speaks flatter and more cuttingly. Together they create the effect of one clever creature in two small bodies.

**Attitude: **Mercenary, curious, delighted by adult underestimation.

**Mannerisms: **Finish each other’s sentences when lying; circle strangers like dogs evaluating boots; exchange looks that count as full conversations.

**What they want right now: **To turn errands and tiny truths into enough coin and relevance that no one sends them away from important conversations.

**Play them as: **Quick, overlapping, shamelessly observant. Use them as a duo unless separating them matters.

**Useful private note: **They know exactly which gap between two houses looks too narrow to matter and is not.

**Sample dialogue: **“For a copper we’ll tell you what happened. For two we’ll tell you what actually happened.”

• “You look like someone who needs directions and doesn’t want witnesses.”

• “No, we weren’t spying. We were passing through vertically.”

# What This Place Offers

Rooms to rent informally, home-cooked meals, seam work, laundry, neighborhood healers, child minding, hand repairs, and access to the real rumor network of the town.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• Several families are taking in cousins from the outer farms without announcing it publicly.

• There is a feud brewing over shared cistern access, which means water pressure is already changing somewhere upstream.

• A woman on Reed Court claims something knocked politely at her shutter from the roof in the middle of the night.

# Hidden Tension

The district contains at least three unofficial rooftop paths used by messengers, lovers, thieves, and practical grandmothers. None are mapped. Many are known.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A child disappears between two houses where there should not be enough space to hide in.

▸ Old Jessa asks for help because one tenant pays on time, speaks politely, and still unnerves the whole courtyard.

▸ A block supper becomes a crisis when someone recognizes an heirloom serving piece in the wrong family’s hands.

# Running the Location

Bring the district alive with doorways, voices, smells, and overlapping lives. This is where Mossford stops being a map and becomes a town worth saving.
