## Temple of the Nameless Stone

Hill Shrine and River Chapel

# At a Glance

Mossford’s temple is smaller and rougher than the grand spaces of Mirathorn, but no less sincere. It serves the Nameless Goddess in a local mode: not with polished ceremony, but with flood prayers, harvest vows, memorial candles, and the practical spiritual labor of a town that lives on unstable water and uncertain land.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The temple smells of wet limestone, beeswax, river herbs, and clean ash. The floors are cool even in summer. A basin near the door holds flowing water instead of still holy water, so there is always the sound of a constant trickle under prayer. During ceremonies the smell of cedar smoke and steeped moss tea hangs in the rafters.

# Physical Description

The building is low and thick-walled, with a steep roof and stone buttresses that make it look less like a chapel and more like something built to survive weather. Its altar is a plain block of river stone worn smooth by generations of hands. Instead of painted icons, the walls hold carved reliefs of hands, roots, waves, lanterns, and open gates. Behind the sanctuary is a garden of flat prayer stones, each one set down for a dead laborer, a lost child, a flood victim, or an unnamed traveler.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Sister Nemeia Silt — Presiding priest

**Appearance: **A middle-aged human woman with strong shoulders, broad brown hands, rolled sleeves, and dark hair braided tightly to keep it out of her work. Her face carries the soft heaviness of someone who has cried often and learned not to fear it.

**Smell: **Beeswax, cedar smoke, clean ash, and a little steeped moss tea clinging to wool.

**Voice: **Low, steady, and resonant; she sounds like she is speaking to one person even in a room full of mourners.

**Attitude: **Compassionate without softness, patient without indulgence. She does not confuse comfort with dishonesty.

**Mannerisms: **Keeps one thumb hooked in her belt while listening; wipes altar stone with the same cloth before every service; lets silence sit longer than most people can bear.

**What they want right now: **To keep the town spiritually intact if panic arrives before proof does.

**Play them as: **Grounded, direct, and warm in a hard-earned way. She gives people enough gentleness to continue, not enough to hide from the truth.

**Useful private note: **She believes one of the nameless stones in her drawer marks a death tied to the founding of the dam, and that someone influential still prefers that history remain blurred.

**Sample dialogue: **“Holiness is not refinement. It is endurance with memory intact.”

• “Sit. Breathe. Then tell me what you fear in words sturdy enough to carry.”

• “The Goddess has room for grief, anger, and bad weather. Bring all three if you must.”

## Brother Halven Thorne — Keeper of votive stones

**Appearance: **A broad-shouldered dwarf novice with a careful beard, stone dust always caught in the cuffs, and powerful hands made gentle by repetitive carving.

**Smell: **Wet limestone, dust, tea leaves, and the chalky coolness of newly worked stone.

**Voice: **Soft and gravelly, with the rhythm of a mason who thinks while his hands move.

**Attitude: **Earnest, observant, and quietly stubborn. He takes names, promises, and memorial work personally.

**Mannerisms: **Traces carved letters with his thumb after finishing them; asks people to repeat names twice; tilts his head when listening as though judging how a word will cut into stone.

**What they want right now: **To make sure the town records its dead honestly, especially the ones no one important wants remembered.

**Play them as: **Gentle but rooted. He can be embarrassed socially and utterly immovable morally in the same breath.

**Useful private note: **He checked the prayer basin after the farmer’s story and found her wrong about the details but right about the direction.

**Sample dialogue: **“You tell me the name. I’ll give it weight.”

• “Stone keeps better company than paper when the weather turns.”

• “If no one else will remember them, that only makes the carving more necessary.”

## Yara Mossbind — Temple herbalist

**Appearance: **A dragonborn widow with deep green scales faded silver at the jawline, a heavy apron full of herb knots, and mournful gold eyes that miss very little.

**Smell: **River herbs, bitter roots, lamp smoke, and drying leaves.

**Voice: **Smoky contralto, unhurried and practical. Even sympathy sounds like instruction when she gives it.

**Attitude: **Matter-of-fact, nurturing, and slightly severe with people who neglect themselves dramatically.

**Mannerisms: **Presses warm cups into hands before asking questions; sorts herbs by feel while speaking; studies the color under a person’s eyes more than their words.

**What they want right now: **To keep the town from mistaking exhaustion and dread for prophecy when simple care would solve half of it.

**Play them as: **Dryly maternal. She is the kind of healer who tells you to sit down in the same tone someone else might use to announce an execution.

**Useful private note: **She has begun brewing larger quantities of calming draught than the temple can publicly justify.

**Sample dialogue: **“Grief is thirsty work. Sit. Drink. Speak if you can.”

• “No, that is not an omen. That is what happens when you haven’t eaten since dawn.”

• “People pray better after tea. That’s a doctrine of mine, not the temple’s.”

# What This Place Offers

Blessings before difficult labor; funerary rites; safe-keeping of memorial objects; calm rooms for private prayer; herbal calming draughts; community mediation when a feud needs to end before it poisons the work season.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• The temple has run short on lamp oil because too many people are leaving candles for the same future disaster instead of a past loss.

• A farmer from the south fields insists the prayer basin ran backward for three breaths at dawn. Brother Halven says she was tired; he did not say he checked the basin himself.

• More strangers have visited the memorial garden this month than in the last three years combined.

# Hidden Tension

Nemeia keeps a drawer of stones without names—deaths no family claimed, bodies no one could identify, and losses the town never agreed how to record. She believes one of them is tied to the founding of the dam, and that the official story is cleaner than the truth.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A prayer stone left blank for years has developed fresh tool marks overnight.

▸ A mourner claims a voice answered from the memorial garden after midnight—kindly, but not human.

▸ The temple asks the party to escort a funeral barge downriver because the usual ferryman refuses the route.

# Running the Location

This is not a grand miracle site. It is a place of practical sanctity, where people come when they need a blessing sturdy enough to survive mud, grief, and work.
