## Mossford Inn

Bridgehouse lodging and public room

# At a Glance

The Mossford Inn is the place travelers imagine the whole town might be like before the rest of Mossford corrects them. It is warm, social, river-facing, and built to make strangers explain themselves politely. Merchants, surveyors, ferrymen, and anxious family visitors all pass through here, which makes it one of the best places in town to hear what matters a few hours before the square hears it.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The inn smells of lamb-fat candles, onion broth, clean straw, damp cloaks, and peat smoke. The common room has the warm dusty scent of old floorboards and new bread. In rainy weather the whole place smells faintly of wool drying on people. The kitchen window vents out pepper, yeast, and whatever stew decided the evening.

# Physical Description

The inn is a broad, comfortable building with an overbuilt porch facing the bridge road, a low-ceilinged common room, and upper rooms whose windows catch both river fog and morning light. A side yard holds a stable, wagon shed, and rain barrels. The signboard shows a stylized bridge under a crescent moon, though locals rarely call it by the full painted name and simply say the Inn. The tables are scarred but scrubbed, and the staff notice everything while pretending not to.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Maud Veller — Proprietor

**Appearance: **A broad-hipped human woman with silver-threaded hair coiled tight, intelligent dark eyes, and the kind of sturdy forearms built by carrying soup and difficult news in equal measure.

**Smell: **Peat smoke, wool, onion broth, and the lavender soap she insists the guest linens deserve.

**Voice: **Warm contralto with an edge that appears only when someone mistakes hospitality for permissiveness.

**Attitude: **Practical, shrewd, and gently interrogative. She likes travelers, just not surprises wearing boots.

**Mannerisms: **Polishes mugs while studying guests; asks one extra question after every answer she mistrusts; places keys on the bar with tiny judgments built into the sound.

**What they want right now: **To figure out why certain southern travelers are arriving watchful, underpacked, and too interested in local infrastructure.

**Play them as: **Welcoming but never casual. She notices everything and forgives selectively.

**Useful private note: **She keeps copies of traveler names and compares them against bridge traffic because debts taught her habits that danger later made useful.

**Sample dialogue: **“You may be tired, wet, and truthful, but I only need two of the three to find you a room.”

• “Eat first, lie later. The truth usually improves after stew.”

• “If you want to vanish in this town, don’t start by announcing yourself thirsty.”

## Kes Reedstep — Cook

**Appearance: **A young dragonborn with ember-red scales at the jaw, rolled sleeves, scar-nicked knuckles, and knives that look loved rather than feared.

**Smell: **Pepper, hot fat, bread steam, onions, and wood smoke from the kitchen range.

**Voice: **Quick, sharp, and expressive. His patience runs short, but never his competence.

**Attitude: **Protective of his kitchen, impatient with dithering, and secretly delighted when people truly appreciate his food.

**Mannerisms: **Points with the back of the knife, never the edge; seasons while listening; insults softly when chopping fast.

**What they want right now: **To keep everyone fed while pretending he is not worried by how many road meals and early breakfasts are suddenly being ordered.

**Play them as: **Fast-tempered but generous. He feels every disruption first as a menu problem and then as an emotional one.

**Useful private note: **Someone has been asking whether the inn cellars connect to older foundations, and he did not like the curiosity in the question.

**Sample dialogue: **“Eat first, explain later. Hungry people invent the worst versions of events.”

• “If you stand in my doorway, you’re volunteering for onions.”

• “Good stew solves more arguments than governance, though I admit the margins are thin.”

## Brin Hollow — Stable hand

**Appearance: **A one-armed halfling with weather-browned skin, bright foxlike eyes, a crooked grin, and a body that moves around horses with unconscious grace.

**Smell: **Hay, horsehide, rain barrels, and the apple peel he keeps in one pocket for nervous animals.

**Voice: **Light, teasing, and quick to drop into conspiratorial quiet.

**Attitude: **Friendly, observant, and impossible to surprise where animals are concerned.

**Mannerisms: **Whistles different patterns for different temperaments; checks tack while talking; listens to hooves on boards like other people read letters.

**What they want right now: **To identify who rode in before dawn and why their horse was lathered while they were nowhere to be found.

**Play them as: **Easy charm over sharp perception. He is the kind of witness people underestimate until too late.

**Useful private note: **He can identify several regular wagons by wheel sound alone and notices when one is missing from the road pattern.

**Sample dialogue: **“Animals tell on their owners faster than luggage does.”

• “That horse is honest. I wish I could say the same for the rider.”

• “You learn more in a stable than in a courtroom, if you can stand the smell.”

# What This Place Offers

Lodging, meals, stable space, private meetings, local introductions, wagon shelter, and discreet forwarding of messages if Maud decides you seem worth the trouble.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• Maud has begun asking all southern travelers the same question twice, which means she does not trust the first answer.

• A room on the second floor has been reserved for a buyer from Mirathorn who still has not arrived, but his deposit was paid in good coin.

• Kes says someone has been asking whether the inn cellars connect to older foundations. He did not like the tone of the question.

# Hidden Tension

Maud keeps copies of the names travelers sign and quietly compares them against bridge traffic and town hall permits. It began as a way to catch debtors and became a private security habit.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A guest vanishes overnight, leaving wet boot prints on the windowsill rather than the floor.

▸ Maud asks the party to identify a man who knew too much about sluice schedules and too little about pretending otherwise.

▸ Brin finds a horse lathered and riderless tied in the stable before dawn.

# Running the Location

The inn is ideal for grounded exposition and social pressure. The room is warm, but the questions are never casual.
