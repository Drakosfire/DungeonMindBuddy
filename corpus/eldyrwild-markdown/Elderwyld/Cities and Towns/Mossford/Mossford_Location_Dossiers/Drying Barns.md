## Drying Barns

South-bank curing rows and weather-sensitive storage

# At a Glance

The Drying Barns stand between raw wet growth and usable material. They are where cut moss slab, fiber bundles, binding reeds, and treated stock wait to become valuable. The work is slower here, more watchful, and more vulnerable to spoiled weather than in the Moldyards, which gives the district a nervous patience all its own.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The barns smell of warm plant fiber, drying tannins, straw, cured moss, and the faint sugary bitterness of rendered sap. Sun-warmed boards creak overhead. The air inside is drier than almost anywhere in Mossford, which makes newcomers notice their own throats and cracked lips quickly. Outside, wind rattles slatted vents with a dry clacking like hollow reeds.

# Physical Description

The district consists of long ventilated barns raised above wet ground on stone feet. Slatted sides and adjustable shutters allow workers to control airflow, while interior racks hold stock in ordered layers based on age, size, and treatment stage. Marker cords of different knots indicate which loads can move next and which must not be touched yet. The barns look plain from outside, but inside they form a maze of aisles, hanging strips, stacked frames, and warm shafts of dusty light.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Meren Ashreed — Drying overseer

**Appearance: **A severe human woman with sun-dark skin, close-bound hair, and sharp shoulders outlined by always-dry work clothes in a town that rarely offers the luxury.

**Smell: **Warm straw, cured moss fiber, tannin, and the bitter salve she rubs into cracked hands.

**Voice: **Thin, exact, and cutting without volume. She can make a single quiet word stop a whole barn aisle.

**Attitude: **Strict, vigilant, and absolutely intolerant of carelessness where moisture is concerned.

**Mannerisms: **Touches stock only with the backs of her fingers first; stops at thresholds to judge airflow; notices wet boots instantly.

**What they want right now: **To keep one suspicious barn stable long enough to prove whether the problem is weather, contamination, or something stranger.

**Play them as: **Controlled severity. She is not cruel—just operating where one small mistake becomes a week of loss.

**Useful private note: **She is afraid the recent changes in curing behavior are not random and has begun keeping workers out of one barn after dusk.

**Sample dialogue: **“Moisture is a thief. Sloppiness is its accomplice.”

• “Do not breathe on that stack unless you plan to pay for it.”

• “Dry work is patient work. If you’re in a hurry, leave.”

## Dov Kell — Vent tender

**Appearance: **A lanky dwarf with permanently chapped lips, a narrow chest, and long fingers blackened at the tips from shutter cords, pitch, and soot.

**Smell: **Dry dust, hot boards, rope fibers, and the fennel seeds he crunches to keep his throat from cracking.

**Voice: **Nasal and thoughtful, with the distracted confidence of a man always half-listening to airflow.

**Attitude: **Mild, precise, and deeply superstitious about drafts because superstition sometimes turns out to be observation in work like his.

**Mannerisms: **Licks a finger to test airflow; sniffs corners mid-sentence; speaks toward shutters as though they respond better to courtesy.

**What they want right now: **To isolate the one warm pocket in the west barns before it spoils more than stock.

**Play them as: **A dry humorist with obsessive sensory habits. He trusts his nose more than most people’s reports.

**Useful private note: **He can smell the difference between ruined, salvageable, and wrong—and recently added a third category because he had to.

**Sample dialogue: **“One bad damp pocket can rot a week of labor. Respect your invisible enemies.”

• “The barn talks if you know whether to listen with ears or lungs.”

• “If it smells sweet where it should smell dry, back away and fetch me.”

## Perrin Softstep — Barn guard

**Appearance: **A sleepy-looking halfling with loose-lidded eyes, a narrow chin, soft-soled shoes, and a posture so unthreatening that thieves routinely make terrible decisions around him.

**Smell: **Hay dust, clean wool, drying reeds, and the plum tobacco he keeps but rarely lights.

**Voice: **Quiet, unhurried, and soft enough that people lean in and tell him more than intended.

**Attitude: **Patient, thorough, and impossible to rush into making a wrong inference.

**Mannerisms: **Stands perfectly still for long stretches; remembers door sounds like other people remember faces; writes tiny, immaculate notes in a pocket ledger.

**What they want right now: **To work out why one barn keeps being opened after hours by people with proper keys and improper reasons.

**Play them as: **Stillness as power. He seems drowsy until you realize he misses nothing.

**Useful private note: **He records every after-hours visit, including some he has not yet shared with the overseer because the names complicate matters.

**Sample dialogue: **“Slow is not inattentive. Slow is thorough enough to remember.”

• “Doors tell on people. Most locks are innocent; the timing isn’t.”

• “I don’t chase often. It spoils the advantage of already knowing where they’ll go.”

# What This Place Offers

Bulk storage, curing oversight, drying leases for private growers, moisture inspections, and salvage grading after storm exposure.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• Two barns ran hotter than expected last week, and the workers are divided between blaming the weather and blaming contaminated slurry in the stock itself.

• A fox got into one of the west barns and left with its tail green from curing dust, which has become a small local omen by consensus.

• A merchant offered to buy semi-cured stock at discount rates, a request so foolish most people assume it concealed another purpose.

# Hidden Tension

Perrin quietly records every after-hours visit. One barn in particular has been opened three times by people with proper keys and improper reasons.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A section of properly dried stock turns soft again overnight as if it were freshly cut.

▸ A barn worker vanishes between aisles no wider than a wagon shaft.

▸ Meren asks the party to stay one night in the barns because theft is no longer the problem she fears most.

# Running the Location

This district should feel dry, controlled, and vulnerable. The danger here is often subtle: contamination, heat, theft, or the wrong thing changing state at the wrong time.
