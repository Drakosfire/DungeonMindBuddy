## Market Square

Commercial center and loudest public room in Mossford

# At a Glance

Market Square is the civic mouth of the town: where growers sell, bargainers posture, ferrymen complain, apprentices stare too long at strangers, and rumor outruns truth by half a day. The square feeds the whole north bank and ties the river economy to the paddies south of the bridge.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The square smells different by the yard: fresh-cut mosswood, wet wicker, fried river fish, onions, damp canvas, soap, leather, sour fruit peel, and horse sweat. There is always the slap of boots on wet planks, the crack of auction voices, the clatter of hooks and scales, and the occasional ringing laugh when someone gets fleeced respectfully.

# Physical Description

The square is broad but irregular, framed by overhanging eaves and covered stalls rather than a perfect civic plan. Mosswood racks lean along one side, fish tables line the cooler river edge, and a raised auction platform occupies the center where bulk materials are called and contested. Rain chains and drainage cuts run visibly through the paving because the town refuses to pretend water is not part of every design problem. On busy days, awnings overlap so thickly that the square becomes a patchwork ceiling of canvas, ropes, and hanging signs.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Vessa Pike — Auction caller

**Appearance: **A sharp-faced human woman with wind-stung cheeks, a striped auction coat, and a posture so straight it makes liars feel conspicuous.

**Smell: **Rain-damp wool, ink, apples, and the sour tang of her perpetual speaking tonic.

**Voice: **Knife-clean and carrying. She can cut through market noise without shouting and make hesitation sound like personal failure.

**Attitude: **Brisk, theatrical, and privately fair. She dislikes sentiment in business but hates cheating more.

**Mannerisms: **Raps the block twice before any serious sale; points with two fingers, never one; narrows one eye when a bid sounds strategic rather than honest.

**What they want right now: **To keep the square liquid and orderly while rumors about next season’s crop are trying to rot both.

**Play them as: **Fast, amused, impatient. She is a public performer whose real work is maintaining trust in the market’s rituals.

**Useful private note: **She quietly delays certain panic-inducing announcements until she can pair them with practical solutions.

**Sample dialogue: **“Bid louder or lose quieter. I am not mothering your price upward.”

• “I sell goods, not courage. Bring your own.”

• “If you want mercy, speak to the temple. If you want a fair lot, speak clearly.”

## Torren Drift — Fishmonger

**Appearance: **A broad-armed half-elf with river-tanned skin, rolled sleeves, a split eyebrow, and forearms scaled in tiny old knife nicks.

**Smell: **Clean brine, cold river water, scales, and the fennel he chews to prove he has standards.

**Voice: **Easy, amused, and rough at the edges, like a man who has spent years talking over gulls and ferrymen.

**Attitude: **Friendly, skeptical, and hard to fool about anything that moves by water.

**Mannerisms: **Checks gills mid-conversation; talks with his knife in hand but never threateningly; flicks bad fish eyes into a bucket without looking.

**What they want right now: **To figure out whether odd river traffic means profit, danger, or both.

**Play them as: **Grounded and teasing. He greets people like he already knows one embarrassing truth about them.

**Useful private note: **He can tell which ferry crews are lying about the river state because they repeat depth numbers too carefully.

**Sample dialogue: **“If it still looks surprised, it is fresh. If the seller looks surprised, it is stolen.”

• “I trust the river more than the people who describe it.”

• “Don’t wrinkle your nose. That’s the smell of a town feeding itself.”

## Mira Dovewick — Lantern and rope seller

**Appearance: **A tidy gnome with silver-threaded braids, half-moon spectacles, and hands so neat they make even coiled rope look disciplined.

**Smell: **Lamp oil, dry hemp, soap, and lavender sachets tucked into stock bins.

**Voice: **Neat, clipped, and maddeningly precise. She sounds polite in the way a ruler is straight.

**Attitude: **Courteous, exact, and faintly judgmental of anyone who confuses preparation with pessimism.

**Mannerisms: **Squares items after customers touch them; remembers people by what they buy badly; smooths stall cloth whenever conversation turns stressful.

**What they want right now: **To sell useful things to sensible people and keep fools from blaming rope for their own judgment.

**Play them as: **Controlled and dryly witty. She never wastes a sentence if a raised brow will do.

**Useful private note: **She has noticed too many strangers asking for maps, lanterns, and preserved food in the same transaction.

**Sample dialogue: **“You do not need stronger rope. You need better judgment. But I sell both.”

• “Lantern first, heroics second.”

• “I have twine, tar, and patience. One of those is more expensive today.”

# What This Place Offers

Produce, fish, cordage, carved tools, ferry bookings, casual labor pickup, messages left with stallholders, and bulk sales of mosswood and shaped stock on auction days.

# Goods & Services

# Town News & Rumor

• A whole lot of cheap iron tools arrived from downriver and half the smiths in town are offended on principle.

• Someone is spreading word that Mirathorn buyers are paying early against next season’s moss crop, which may be true and is definitely destabilizing negotiations.

• A child claims to have seen a fox with reeds growing from its back under the fish stalls before dawn.

# Hidden Tension

Several stallholders quietly coordinate to divert the worst panic before it reaches the public board. Market stability in Mossford is social engineering with baskets attached.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A scheduled auction lot arrives short by exactly one wagon, and everyone accuses a different district.

▸ A stranger pays in old Mirathorn coin and asks only for maps and preserved food.

▸ Vessa halts an auction because one bundle of mosswood has roots still slowly moving through it.

# Running the Location

This is your best location for overheard talk, soft rumors, visible class tension, and the feeling of Mossford as a functioning town rather than a quest node.
