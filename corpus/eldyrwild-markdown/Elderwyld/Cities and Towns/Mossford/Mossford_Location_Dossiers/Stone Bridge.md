## Stone Bridge

The south crossing and civic choke point

# At a Glance

The Stone Bridge is the most strategically obvious structure in Mossford. Everything crossing from the north-bank town into the southern farms or beyond to the Mirathorn road must reckon with it. It is at once ordinary infrastructure, local landmark, emergency line, and stage for every argument about what should or should not pass.

# Quick Reference

# Sensory Profile

The bridge smells of wet stone, horse sweat, wheel grease, and river air. Under heavy traffic it vibrates faintly through the soles. Spray rises under the arches during fast water and leaves a cool slickness on the lower rails. Every sound sharpens here: hoofbeats, axle complaints, shouted warnings, and the hollow clop of boots moving over fitted stone.

# Physical Description

It is a handsome, practical stone bridge with multiple arches, thick cutwaters, low parapets, and enough width for wagons to pass with care. Moss clings under the downwind side in dark strips where the sun hits least. Small guard niches stand at either end, more for watching loads and traffic than for martial grandeur. From the midpoint, the north-bank town looks denser and more impressive, while the south fields spread outward like a worked geometry of water and labor.

# People Here

Use the profiles below as quick-play references. Each is written so you can step into them at the table with minimal prep.

## Serit Vale — Bridge warden

**Appearance: **A tall half-elf with rain-dark hair braided under a clerk’s hood, a ledger tucked under one arm, and a face built for rules that save lives rather than flatter authority.

**Smell: **Wet stone, ink, horse leather, and the bitter tea she drinks between inspections.

**Voice: **Firm, clipped, and public-facing. She can make traffic control sound like moral instruction.

**Attitude: **Fair, unyielding, and very tired of wagons wider than declared.

**Mannerisms: **Measures axle width with her eyes before touching a ruler; blows her whistle once for warning and twice for disobedience; records arguments in the margins when annoyed.

**What they want right now: **To keep the bridge clear enough for evacuation if needed without saying the word aloud yet.

**Play them as: **Professional and tense. She is trying to keep order ahead of a scenario she suspects but has not been authorized to name.

**Useful private note: **She has started turning back overloaded carts more aggressively because she suspects someone will soon need the crossing kept open in a hurry.

**Sample dialogue: **“If it doesn’t fit, it waits. If it breaks, you pay. Those are old bridge laws because they remain good bridge laws.”

• “You are not stuck in traffic. You are traffic. Move accordingly.”

• “A bridge is a promise. Do not make me enforce it on you personally.”

## Hobb Kett — Maintenance mason

**Appearance: **A one-eyed dwarf with a flattened ear, forearms powdered pale from stone dust, and a permanent squint at anything vertical.

**Smell: **Wet mortar, cold stone, sweat, and the tobacco he swears he quit seasons ago.

**Voice: **Gruff, gravelly, and contemptuous in a way that somehow rarely feels personal.

**Attitude: **Skeptical, workmanlike, and emotionally loyal to stone before people.

**Mannerisms: **Knocks on parapets with his hammer while thinking; spits into the river before beginning repairs; mutters to cracked surfaces as if interrogating them.

**What they want right now: **To figure out why a few bridge cracks seem to vanish depending on the angle of light.

**Play them as: **A pessimist whose pessimism has saved infrastructure more than once.

**Useful private note: **He knows one lower maintenance niche can be used to hide contraband or people and checks it more often than his reports admit.

**Sample dialogue: **“Everyone notices the bridge when they need it. No one notices it when I save it from them.”

• “Stone tells the truth eventually. Wood lies sooner.”

• “If the crack moves when I move, that means I have two problems and only one hammer.”

## Fira Noll — South-bank runner

**Appearance: **A rangy human courier with windburn, cropped hair, narrow shoulders, and calves like twisted rope from sprinting the crossing ten times a day.

**Smell: **River spray, sweat, waxed packet cord, and mint leaves chewed to keep her throat open.

**Voice: **Breathy, quick, and sharp with urgency even when calm. She is used to speaking while already moving.

**Attitude: **Impatient, alert, and professionally allergic to delays.

**Mannerisms: **Keeps bouncing on her heels; turns to look south whenever someone lowers their voice; identifies wagons by ear before sight.

**What they want right now: **To keep message flow between farms and town moving faster than rumor.

**Play them as: **Fast and taut. She has no patience for dithering but a great deal for necessity.

**Useful private note: **She has heard one wagon pattern too many vanish from the road sequence these last few days.

**Sample dialogue: **“You can hear fear in a wheel long before you see it.”

• “If I’m running, make room. If I’m not, ask why I stopped.”

• “Do you need the message fast, discreet, or intact? Pick two.”

# What This Place Offers

Traffic control, wagon weights, emergency closure orders, maintenance inspection, and public notice posting on both ends of the crossing.

# Town News & Rumor

• The bridge has been kept clear more aggressively than usual, which means someone is thinking about evacuation whether they say the word or not.

• A loose parapet stone was found marked in charcoal with a spiral no one admits drawing.

• Serit started turning back overloaded carts two days in a row and now half the southern farmers think she knows something they do not.

# Hidden Tension

One of the lower maintenance niches opens into a crawlspace used by generations of bridge workers. It is not large, but it is enough to stash contraband, hide from weather, or listen to conversations above through the stone.

# Exploration Hooks

▸ A wagon reaches the bridge with no driver, only a horse that refuses to step onto the first span.

▸ A mason reports fresh cracks that vanish when viewed from another angle.

▸ A desperate farmer begs the party to get one final load across after the wardens have closed the route.

# Running the Location

The bridge is a pressure point. Use it for decisions, visible stakes, movement under time, and the physical sensation of a town narrowing into a single line.
