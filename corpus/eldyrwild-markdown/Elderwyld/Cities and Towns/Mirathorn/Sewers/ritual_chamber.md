### The Pre-Kaiju Ritual Chamber: Description and Mechanics

#### Description of the Cistern Chamber

As the players descend further into the depths, the air becomes thick with the stench of decay and damp meat. The narrow tunnels give way to a massive, cylindrical chamber—a corrupted cistern. The walls are coated in pulsating, fleshy growths, veined with a sickly green glow. The chamber is circular, with a wide, open platform 30 feet above the roiling, dark waters of the cistern below. Suspended in the center of the chamber, an enormous fleshy mass writhes and throbs, as though it has a heartbeat of its own.

Dark, oily tentacles extend from the pre-Kaiju mass, reaching toward the walls of the chamber. Cultists are being drawn into its writhing folds, absorbed without resistance. Above, a ring of head cultists chants in a guttural, otherworldly language, their voices amplified by the acoustics of the chamber. Their eerie synchronization creates a rhythmic pulse that seems to shake the very ground.

On the outer edges of the platform, corrupted guards stand at attention, scanning for intruders. Four guards are stationed evenly around the perimeter, while two elite corrupted guards patrol the central area, their eyes glowing with the familiar oily sheen. At the head of the ritual, the remaining cultist leaders stand in a circle, their bodies trembling with magical strain as they drive the ritual toward completion.

### The Wolf's Head as an Avatar

As the players battle through the corrupted guards and cultists, the Kaiju mass writhes violently, and a sickly green glow intensifies within its pulsating bulk. Slowly, a grotesque approximation of **The Wolf's head** begins to emerge from the top of the pre-Kaiju mass, its features distorted and exaggerated. The face is a nightmarish blend of humanity and otherworldly corruption: jagged teeth, too-large eyes that ooze with black ichor, and tendrils extending like a mockery of hair.

The head turns toward the players, its movements jerky and unnatural, as though a puppet being yanked by invisible strings. When it speaks, two distinct voices echo: one is the Shepherd's deep, resonant growl, filled with malevolent intent, while the other is the broken, pleading voice of the human Eirik—the man who once was The Wolf.

### Dialogue

#### As the head emerges

The Shepherd’s Voice:
“Ah, the bold fools who think they can cut the strings of destiny. You will fall like the rest, feeding my ascension! This vessel’s sacrifice was but the first of many.”

Eirik’s Voice:
“No... no, this is wrong... I didn’t want this! Please, someone stop it! Stop me!”

#### If players hesitate or converse

The Shepherd’s Voice:
“Pathetic. He clings to scraps of a soul already devoured. There is no Eirik. There is only my will, growing stronger with every moment you squander.”

Eirik’s Voice:
“You don’t understand... it’s... it’s using me! Kill it... kill me... before it’s too late...”

#### When a cultist or guard falls

The Shepherd’s Voice:
“Good. Their flesh was weak. They will be reshaped into my glory.”

Eirik’s Voice:
“No... stop this madness! Please, I didn’t... I didn’t mean for this!”

#### If the players focus on the ritual or the Kaiju mass

The Shepherd’s Voice:
“You think disrupting this will save you? I am inevitable! The city above will burn, its people will kneel, and the Elderwyld will become my dominion.”

Eirik’s Voice:
“It’s lying... It’s lying! The crystal... the crystal—destroy it!”

### Tactical Use of Dialogue

**The Shepherd’s Voice**: The Shepherd uses dialogue to mock and distract the players, drawing their attention away from the ritual’s weak points or pressuring them into rash decisions.

**Eirik’s Voice**: Eirik provides cryptic hints, torn between wanting to help the players and being forcibly silenced by the Shepherd. He might give subtle directions, like glancing toward the temple or the crystal when his voice fades out.

**Combining Voices**: Occasionally, both voices overlap, creating a cacophony of sound that unnerves the players and adds to the oppressive atmosphere.

### Mechanics: Time Pressure and Weak Points

**Weak Points**: If the players pay attention to Eirik’s lamentations, they can infer that the crystal in the temple is a critical weak point anchoring the Kaiju to the material plane. Destroying it will delay the summoning.

**Time Pressure**: Each round, more cultists arrive from hidden passageways, and the Kaiju mass grows larger, sprouting additional tentacles and releasing meat flies. If the players take too long, the Kaiju will fully form and burst upward into the city.

### Visual and Auditory Effects During the Fight

The chamber grows darker with each passing round, as the glow from the Kaiju mass intensifies and begins to strobe, creating disorienting shadows.

Eirik’s voice becomes fainter as the Shepherd's domination strengthens. By the time the players reach the ritual's climax, only the Shepherd’s voice remains, laughing triumphantly.

#### Mechanics for the Fight

This fight is designed to test the players’ resolve and build tension before the climactic battle. The enemies should present a significant challenge but remain manageable with good tactics.

## Enemies:

**4 Corrupted Guards**: Basic melee units, stationed at the edges of the platform.

HP: 15 each

Attack: Spear (melee/ranged), +3 to hit, 1d6 + 1 piercing damage

Ability: "Guard the Platform" – If a player tries to move toward the cultist leaders, a corrupted guard will attempt to block the path.

**2 Elite Corrupted Guards**: Tougher melee units patrolling the central platform.

HP: 35 each

Attack: Greatsword, +4 to hit, 2d6 + 2 slashing damage

Ability: "Dark Resilience" – Once per round, reduce damage from a single attack by 5.

Ability: "Rally" – On their turn, grant all nearby corrupted guards +1 to attack rolls for the round.

**3 Cultist Leaders**: Driving the ritual, but will engage if attacked.

HP: 45 each

Attack: "Corrupted Spell" (ranged), +5 to hit, 2d8 necrotic damage

Ability: "Summon Meatlings" – On their turn, they can summon a small meatling to harass a player.

Mechanics: The cultist leaders are the primary targets to disrupt the ritual. Each time one is killed, the ritual visibly falters, and the Kaiju mass writhes in pain.

**Environmental Hazards**:

**Tentacle Grabs**: Any player within 10 feet of the edge risks being grabbed by a tentacle. DC 13 Strength save to avoid being pulled 10 feet closer to the Kaiju mass. On a failure, take 1d8 necrotic damage.

**Ritual Explosions**: Every two rounds, the ritual creates an unstable burst of energy. All creatures in the chamber must make a DC 12 Dexterity save or take 2d6 force damage and be knocked prone.

## Battle Dynamics:

**Initial Phase**: The guards will focus on blocking the players' progress toward the cultists. Players will need to deal with the guards before they can effectively reach the ritual leaders.

**Escalation**: When the first cultist leader is killed, the remaining two will redirect some of their magical energy to defend themselves. This summons two meatlings (small, weak creatures) to harass the players.

**Pressure from Tentacles**: As the battle continues, the tentacles will become more aggressive, targeting players closer to the edges of the platform. The players will need to balance positioning and attacking the ritual leaders.

**Disrupting the Ritual**: Killing all three cultist leaders halts the ritual, causing the Kaiju mass to recoil in pain and temporarily halt its growth. This gives the players a clear sense of victory, but the mass remains alive, setting up the true final confrontation.

## Loot (Optional):

Each guard carries mundane weapons and corrupted insignias.

The cultist leaders drop:

**Scroll of Banishment**: A single-use scroll that casts Banishment (DC 14).

**Ritualist's Amulet**: Grants +1 to spell attack rolls.

**Potion of Greater Healing**: Restores 4d4 + 4 HP.

This encounter primes the players for the final Kaiju battle, offering a mix of combat, environmental hazards, and strategic decision-making. The visual horror and escalating tension should keep them fully immersed.
