# The Hidden Temple of the Nameless Goddess

## General Description

This forgotten temple is a remnant of the first settlers of Mirathorn. Its core structure is an ancient airship repurposed into a place of worship, now overgrown with the corrupted flesh of the sewer. The ship’s skeletal remains form the walls, while its engines hum faintly with residual planar energy.

The temple exudes an eerie blend of divinity and decay. Shattered stained glass windows depicting the Nameless Goddess are partially obscured by creeping tendrils of corruption.

The air is thick with the scent of ozone and rot, and the temperature fluctuates unnaturally, a reminder of its planar significance.

## Key Features

### Entrance

A massive, rusted airlock door barely clings to its hinges. It is covered in celestial glyphs faintly glowing with gold light, a stark contrast to the encroaching red-black corruption.

**Arcana Check (DC 14):** Recognizes the glyphs as an ancient ward against extraplanar influence. They have weakened but remain somewhat effective.

**Religion Check (DC 12):** Identifies the symbols as representing a feminine god, the Nameless Goddess, and her ideals of civilization and order.

### Central Hall

The main chamber is vast and circular, its walls formed from the ribs of the airship. The floor is made of cracked marble, partially overtaken by pulsating meat growths. A broken altar sits in the center, with a crystal fragment embedded in it.

A faint golden light emanates from the crystal, fighting against the encroaching corruption.

**Investigation Check (DC 15):** Reveals that this crystal was once a stabilizing core of the airship, likely tied to planar energy and now being sapped by the corruption.

**Arcana Check (DC 16):** Identifies that removing the crystal could weaken the summoning ritual but risks unleashing unstable energy in the room.

### Side Chambers

**Chamber of the First Council:** A small room containing decayed records and murals depicting the first settlers of Mirathorn arriving in Elderwyld.

Mural Details:

A grand airship surrounded by swirling planar storms.

The first council gathered in unity, holding the planar crystal.

**History Check (DC 13):** Confirms that the crystal was used to anchor Mirathorn's first teleportation circle.

Loot: A fragment of the mural (valuable to scholars or the council) and a broken amulet (can be repaired into an Amulet of Protection, granting +1 AC).

**Engine Chamber:** The remnants of the airship’s propulsion system.

A corrupted cultist (stealthing) attempts to sabotage the players here by triggering an explosive reaction from the unstable engines.

**Dexterity Save (DC 14):** To avoid being thrown by the blast if the sabotage is triggered.

Loot: Engine fragments that can be salvaged for crafting, potentially enhancing weapons with an elemental spark.

### Divine Reflection Pool

A small, recessed pool at the far end of the temple glows faintly with golden light. It reflects not the corrupted surroundings but an untouched vision of the temple as it once was.

**Insight Check (DC 12):** The pool offers a vision to anyone who gazes into it, showing the Nameless Goddess and her despair at the corruption of her ideals.

**Effects of the Pool:** Drinking from it restores 1d8 HP and grants inspiration for one hour.

## The Red Flag: The Crystal and the Corruption

The planar crystal embedded in the altar is tethered to the summoning ritual. Observing the crystal:

**Arcana Check (DC 15):** The corruption is actively feeding off the crystal's energy, channeling it toward the ritual site.

**Investigation Check (DC 13):** Removing the crystal will halt this channeling but risks drawing attention from the cultists.

## Encounters

### Guardian Apparition

As players approach the altar, a celestial apparition of one of the founding council members appears. Their form flickers between golden light and a corrupted red hue.

Dialogue: They beg the players to reclaim the crystal but warn of the danger.

Combat (Optional): If the players attempt to defile the altar or mishandle the crystal, the apparition attacks, summoning celestial constructs corrupted by the flesh.

### Cultist Saboteur

A lone cultist lingers in the Engine Chamber, attempting to siphon energy from the engines.

Combat: The cultist uses defensive spells and traps to delay the players.

Loot: Notes detailing how the cult has been using planar energy to enhance the summoning ritual.

## Loot

## Planar Crystal Fragment (if removed from the altar):

Grants the ability to disrupt planar energy at a ritual site, making it an invaluable asset against the Kaiju summoning.

However, the fragment is volatile and must be handled carefully to avoid damaging it.

**Amulet of Protection (broken):** Found in the Chamber of the First Council, it can be repaired by a skilled craftsman.

**Engine Fragments:** Useful for crafting elemental enhancements to weapons or armor.

**Mural Fragment:** A historical artifact that could be traded for gold or favor with the council.

## Decision Point

**Take the Crystal:** Risks alerting the cult and drawing reinforcements, but it weakens the ritual significantly.

**Leave the Crystal:** Avoids immediate danger, but the ritual’s power remains intact.

**Investigate Further:** The pool and side chambers offer additional lore and minor rewards but consume valuable time.

This temple serves as a key moment in the dungeon, blending lore, exploration, and high-stakes decision-making for the players.
