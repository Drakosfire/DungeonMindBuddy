Emergency Council of Mirathorn

### 1. Wizards' College Wants Magical Control:

**Proposal:** The wizards suggest an arcane lockdown of the city. They propose activating aged magical wards from the bad times at key city areas to trap or identify any tainted meat, corrupted individuals, or cult activity.

**Pros:** Provides immediate magical detection of any cultist or tainted goods. Powerful mages could prevent the kaiju summoning.

**Cons:** The wards would place severe restrictions on magic use, impacting the city's day-to-day functioning. The wards could also trap innocent civilians, causing panic and frustration. It’s also possible that the wizards could accidentally trigger other magical anomalies, such as shutting down important infrastructure or causing unwanted side effects in OutTown. This would effectively cancel the festival, and have long lasting ramifications on the underlying issue of people migrating out of the city and into the hinterlands.

## Outcome Branches:

Players may be called upon to help set up or maintain these magical wards, traveling across the city on a tight timeline.

A malfunction or misplacement of one of the wards could inadvertently speed up the cult’s plans, and the kaiju could be summoned anyway.

### 2. Barin Stonefoot Calls for Covert Ops:

**Proposal:** Barin suggests a series of quick, covert operations to root out the cult members without alerting the public. This would involve small, skilled teams (including the players) breaking into suspected guard hideouts and sewers.

**Pros:** This keeps public order, avoids panic, and focuses on surgical strikes against the cult and the corrupted guards.

**Cons:** The cult might detect the raids before they can be fully implemented, giving them time to accelerate their plans. Also, this requires good coordination between the council and various factions, which could create internal conflicts.

## Outcome Branches:

Players may be part of several coordinated attacks across the city, each with different objectives (rescue Torbin, seize tainted meat, capture a cult leader).

The plan might succeed in stopping the kaiju if players manage to reach key locations. If they fail to hit critical targets in time, the kaiju still emerges.

### 3. Grobnok the Goblin Suggests Guerrilla Tactics:

**Proposal:** Grobnok pushes for a more chaotic and less orthodox approach: guerrilla tactics from the undercity. He suggests using his network in the Grit and Grime to recruit goblin, bugbear, and undercity mercenaries to create distractions, sabotage cultist resources, and drive out their forces through unconventional means.

**Pros:** Grobnok’s plan utilizes the city’s underground networks, taking advantage of the cultists’ reliance on the sewers. His approach is unpredictable, which could throw the cult off balance.

**Cons:** Using the undercity's forces is risky—there’s a chance for collateral damage, or the goblins and bugbears might loot in the chaos. Grobnok’s loose plan may not account for the cult's full power or strategy.

## Outcome Branches:

The players can team up with Grobnok’s forces, setting traps in the sewer or helping guide them to key cultist hideouts.

The goblin forces might unintentionally cause havoc in the city, escalating the situation. This could lead to public backlash and increase tension within the council.

### 4. Merril Tealeaf Calls for Mass Purification:

**Proposal:** Merril, representing the agricultural union, pushes for an aggressive city-wide **purification ritual**. She suggests setting up purifying cauldrons in every district to magically cleanse food, water, and even people in an attempt to neutralize the tainted meat.

**Pros:** This would provide immediate protection to the population, especially before the BBQ. It’s a non-violent way of addressing the situation and minimizes damage to the city.

**Cons:** It’s time-consuming and resource-intensive, especially since they’ll need to purify the entire city. The cultists could sabotage or corrupt the cauldrons. Additionally, the magical purification may not reach the hidden tainted meat in time, making it an ineffective solution. It also brings an immediate end to the festival.

## Outcome Branches:

The players may need to defend the cauldrons from cultist sabotage or deliver key ingredients for the purification to different parts of the city.

The purification might prevent the kaiju’s summoning, but if the ritual is disrupted, the kaiju might emerge even more powerful, absorbing the magic from the cauldrons.

### 5. Commander Thalia Calls for Immediate Force:

**Proposal:** Commander Thalia suggests a direct military response—mobilizing the entire city guard to sweep through the sewers, guard barracks, and all cult suspect locations in one massive strike.

**Pros:** It’s an efficient use of force, designed to overwhelm the cult before they can finish their preparations.

**Cons:** Thalia is unknowingly under the Wolf’s influence, so her plan is deliberately designed to fail. The Wolf will ensure the strike team goes to the wrong locations or delays just long enough for the kaiju summoning to occur.

## Outcome Branches:

Players might help coordinate the strike teams, only to realize they’ve been sent to the wrong locations while the real summoning happens elsewhere.

The players will need to uncover and confront the Wolf before it’s too late, but doing so could cause major internal strife within the guard.

### 6. Torrin Flamescale Wants the People Armed:

**Proposal:** Torrin, representing the guilds, proposes handing out weapons and defensive tools to the populace, essentially preparing for a city-wide fight. He believes that the cult’s forces are too embedded for the guard to handle alone, and every citizen should be ready to defend themselves.

**Pros:** The people of Mirathorn get ready for a city-wide battle, and it empowers the non-corrupted guard and citizenry to fight back.

**Cons:** The plan might cause widespread panic and chaos. If the people are armed and ready to fight, there’s the potential for civil unrest or clashes between different factions within the city. It also risks giving the cult a chance to infiltrate armed groups. Ends the festival.

## Outcome Branches:

Players might need to help distribute weapons and rally support, possibly engaging in combat alongside citizens.

The city could descend into chaos before the cult strikes, making it easier for the kaiju to emerge and wreak havoc.

### 7. Rurik Stonehammer Proposes Infrastructure Defense:

**Proposal:** Rurik believes that the cult is using the city’s hidden infrastructure—sewers, tunnels, and forgotten chambers—to operate. He proposes sealing off key sewer tunnels and reinforcing defensive positions around the city.

**Pros:** It focuses on cutting off the cult’s access to the heart of the city, preventing the summoning ritual from taking place in the town square.

**Cons:** This plan will be too slow and might not stop the cultists from using alternative routes. It also risks leaving parts of the city isolated if key tunnels or access points are sealed.

## Outcome Branches:

Players could explore the tunnels, setting traps or trying to uncover hidden routes the cult is using.

The cultists may use alternative, more dangerous paths, such as summoning the kaiju from a less fortified part of the city.

### 8. Political Intrigue Among the Council:

**Proposal:** Certain factions of the council may not be fully convinced of the cult’s threat, despite clear evidence. They might propose holding back action until more information is gathered, or they may think this is an opportunity to shift power in the city.

**Pros:** This creates political tension, adding depth to the roleplay and making the players navigate the council’s differing interests.

**Cons:** Delaying action would likely give the cultists enough time to complete the summoning.

## Outcome Branches:

Players may need to sway key council members to act quickly, using diplomacy or intimidation.

If they fail to get the council moving, the kaiju will be summoned and the players will face a major battle.

### Conclusion:

Each of these proposals allows for different roleplay opportunities and outcomes, with the possibility of the flesh kaiju emerging as a result of delayed or failed action. The council members' different motivations can create tension and set up dynamic player interactions, making the session full of intrigue and potential combat.

The wizards college and agricultural district are aligned.

### Overall Structure:

## Time Pressure Mechanic:

Each round of discussion takes time, representing the growing risk of the **flesh kaiju summoning**. After each round, you can roll a **D4 or D6**, counting down to the kaiju’s arrival.

For example, every **10 minutes of in-game discussion** might bring the kaiju summoning closer. If the kaiju’s "countdown" hits 0, the cult succeeds, and the kaiju emerges.

Players must manage time effectively, with each round spent either **convincing council members** or **pushing for action**, while the clock ticks down.

**Convincing the Council:** The players will need to **convince the council members** of their value as advisors or participants in the decision-making process. Each council member has their own perspective, bias, or approach, and players will need to **persuade or intimidate** them depending on their personalities. This will help unlock different proposals or actions.

#### Step-by-Step Mechanics:

### Step 1: Initial Influence—Convincing the Council

**Objective:** Players need to demonstrate their expertise and urgency to gain the council’s trust.

**Mechanics:** Players can use skill checks to convince key council members of their value.

**Persuasion (Charisma):** For a direct appeal to logic or reason. ("We’ve fought the cultists, we know their methods!")

**Intimidation (Charisma):** To drive home the severity of the threat and press for immediate action. ("If you don’t act now, the city will fall!")

**Insight (Wisdom):** Players can make Insight checks to gauge a council member’s motivation, which allows them to tailor their argument.

**Arcana/History (Intelligence):** If they need to convince the Wizards’ College or knowledgeable council members with technical knowledge or evidence of the cult’s activities.

## Consequences:

**Success (DC 15 or higher):** The council members acknowledge the players’ input, giving them more weight in the discussion and opening up the opportunity to sway decisions.

**Partial Success (DC 10-14):** Some council members are swayed, but others remain hesitant, increasing the **time pressure** as discussions drag on.

**Failure (Below DC 10):** The council members dismiss the players’ input, considering them unimportant or unreliable. This wastes valuable time.

## Example:

A player rolls **Persuasion** to convince Headmaster Tinkerbright to take the players seriously. On a success, the Headmaster offers to use the **Wizards’ College detection spell** to locate the tainted meat, cutting investigation time down.

### Step 2: Discussion Rounds

Each **discussion round** represents a period of debate and back-and-forth among the council members. The players can contribute and try to guide the conversation toward **decisive action**.

## Mechanics:

**Skill Checks to Influence Specific Council Members:** Players can target specific council members to influence their vote or approach. This could be **Persuasion**, **Deception**, or even **Arcana** checks, depending on the argument being made.

## Time Management:

After each round of discussion (representing about 10 minutes in-game), roll a **D4 or D6** to count down toward the cult’s success. Each failed or unresolved debate brings the city closer to disaster.

Players might need to **multi-task**, sending one member of the party to investigate while others stay in the meeting.

## Example:

A player makes a **History check** to point out evidence of similar cult behavior, convincing Merril Tealeaf to vote for a purification ritual.

### Step 3: Unlocking Proposals and Actions

Once the players have earned their place in the discussion, they can **push for specific actions**. These proposals might unlock **specific objectives**, like raids on corrupted guardhouses, sealing off the sewers, or calling for covert operations.

## Proposal Mechanics:

**Skill Checks to Convince the Council:** Each proposal has a certain level of difficulty based on its risk, resources needed, and likelihood of success. Players must roll to push their preferred plan into action.

**Arcana (Intelligence):** Convince the council to rely on magical solutions (like the Wizards’ meat detection spell).

**Persuasion (Charisma):** Convince the council to act swiftly and decisively (like mobilizing the guard or a covert strike).

**Investigation (Intelligence):** If the players suggest investigating leads and uncovering cultists, this roll would speed up identifying cult hideouts.

## Example:

A player rolls **Persuasion** to push for a city-wide lockdown led by the guards. The **DC** could be higher (around **18**) due to concerns about causing a panic, but if successful, it unlocks a raid on a suspected cult location.

## Outcome:

**Success:** The council takes decisive action, initiating a raid, magical search, or covert ops, reducing the risk of the kaiju’s emergence.

**Failure:** Deliberation drags on, increasing the chance that the cult summons the kaiju or poisons the BBQ.

### Step 4: Complications and Sabotage

The Wolf or other cult sympathizers within the council (or even in the room) could be working to **sabotage** the decision-making process. These NPCs might make **counterarguments**, sow confusion, or delay votes.

## Mechanics for Sabotage:

The **Wolf** or other hidden cultists can use **Deception** checks to steer the discussion in the wrong direction or **Persuasion** to downplay the threat of the cult.

The players might notice subtle clues through **Insight** (Wisdom) or **Perception** (Wisdom) checks, revealing the influence of cult members in the council.

## Example:

The Wolf might roll a **Deception check** to convince the council to delay the raid on the guardhouse, subtly buying time for the cultists to complete their summoning. The players can attempt an **Insight check** to see through the deception and expose the Wolf's true intentions.

### Step 5: The Moment of Decision

At the end of the session, the council makes its **final decision** based on the players' success or failure in influencing them.

## Step 1: Proposal Phase

Lay out **5-7 proposal circles**.

Each council member begins with **3 influence tokens** that can be distributed between proposals.

Players need to target specific council members to increase their trust, sway their vote, and influence **how they allocate their tokens**.

#### Step 2: Player Influence

Each round, players attempt to sway specific council members using **Persuasion, Insight, Intimidation**, or even **Arcana** for magical-related proposals.

Each success increases the chance of a council member allocating more tokens toward the players’ desired proposal.

Failed rolls may shift influence to another plan or stall the decision-making process.

#### Step 3: The Wolf’s Sabotage

The GM rolls **Deception or Intimidation checks** for the Wolf each round to sabotage key plans.

The Wolf might redirect **Thalia’s votes**, convince other council members to withdraw their support, or create chaos that delays voting on specific proposals.

If the players succeed in uncovering The Wolf’s corruption (through Insight or Investigation), the Wolf’s influence diminishes, but this requires **significant effort**.

#### Step 4: Voting Phase

At the end of each round, the **influence tokens** in each proposal circle are counted.

If a proposal has 15 influence it is **actioned**.

If no plan reaches the required **influence threshold**, the council may stall for another round, causing **time pressure** to build.

#### Step 5: Consequences & Escalation

If certain plans are repeatedly stalled, **tensions rise**, and the players may have to take matters into their own hands, leading to potential conflict, kaiju summoning, or other escalating consequences.
