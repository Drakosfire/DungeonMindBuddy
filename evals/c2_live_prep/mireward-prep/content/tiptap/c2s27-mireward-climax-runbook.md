# C2 Session 27 Prep

## Session intent

This session is about increasing the tension. Bring the combat to a climax that pushes the players to move in a direction.

By the end of Session 27, remaining in the exact current status quo should clearly be impossible. No NPC chooses the party's final direction. Beats may compress, overlap, reorder, or be skipped if player action already resolved their purpose.

## Memory (world graph)

Summarize at the table from what is already established: what the party knows, unresolved threads, and likely pressure going into Session 27. Do not invent new facts merely to fill this section.

The enemy has already progressed: open-ground assault → burned remains and liquefaction → underground movement → Thrin abduction → civilian and foundation attacks → structural failure from beneath. The Meat Mind wants to break Mireward's fallback function and obtain Thrin / the forest-memory alive. Killing monsters and saving the wall are no longer identical objectives.

## Session 27

One siege-climax session. The spine is five Beats. The list labeled Scenes lives inside Beat 1; those are table options at the hole, not sibling Scenes.

Visible success means Mireward can survive the immediate crisis while being hurt. Visible failure can move the town to an inner line, evacuation, or collapse without automatically ending the campaign.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v1 kind=beat id=beat:survive-breach -->
### Beat 1: Survive the current breach

Session 27 begins **without a reset**, in or immediately adjacent to the active combat from Session 26.

Opening : Baergrom physically plugging the tunnel, Bonogo behind him, and the remaining swarm trying to drag a townsman away. The rest of the party is scattered between surface and tunnels. Oil and fire are already in the hole. Guards are here, not arriving later.

Do not introduce a fresh combat before resolving the immediate breach. The first demand on the table is the hole in front of them.

Scenes

- Save the townsman or let him disappear into the tunnel.
- Kill, drive off, trap, burn, or follow the surviving swarm.
- Get Thrin safely back into the defensive line.
- Pull Baergrom out before the tunnel becomes a tomb—or decide that him holding that point is exactly what they need.
- Investigate what the swarm was actually doing to the wall.
- Collapse, burn, flood, or otherwise interfere with the local tunnel.
- Reconnect the scattered party.

This Beat resolves when the immediate breach stops demanding initiative-level attention. It does not require every monster to be dead.

World Tick : Then the structural hum begins.

Not another creature approaching. The earth itself vibrates. Dirt dances. Mortar sifts out of seams. The existing crack grows somewhere nobody is currently fighting.

Table line: they are not coming through the tunnel; they are widening it.

That is where Lysandro comes back.

Lysandro arrives with ordinary Mireward people, timber, wagon jacks, rope, iron fittings, whatever they can physically carry. He is the person who can turn terrified neighbors into a work crew and understands mechanisms well enough to recognize how the wall is failing.

His re-entry should be energetic rather than sentimental. He sees Lysandra;

"Did you know your pops knew how to reinforce a wall against a horde?"  Now somebody tell me why my wall is sinking.”

Lysandra is confused, laughs a little and then surveys the scene and looks grim.

END : The problem is now the town.

### Beat 2: Fight the town-wide siege intelligently

Once the local hole is no longer the whole fight, widen the view.

A subterranean hum continues across more than this tunnel. Under-Hymn Brood and Latchlings work foundations, seams, supports, and refugee routes.

Present at least two competing pressures so the party cannot solve everything by standing in one square. Infrastructure, civilians/refugees, enemy intent, and wall geometry should compete for attention.

Guards and refugees should make the enemy's adaptation legible: the wall and town are a system under attack, not just a monster-encounter map.

The intelligent play is choosing which failure to stop. Killing the nearest swarm can be the wrong move if the wall, a refugee route, or a support is what is actually failing.

Do not add a new boss merely because this Beat exists. Whether Under-Hymn Brood remains the structural threat, or play justifies a larger presence, is still an open table question.

Keep this modular. If the party already collapsed the local tunnel, burned a work crew out of a seam, or evacuated a street, do not restage that problem just to have a town-wide fight.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v1 kind=beat id=beat:thrin-memory -->
### Beat 3: The forest-memory awakens under pressure

Thrin's seed is an autonomous living memory, not ordinary meat corruption. It resists incorporation. The awakening should be defensive and informational. It is not a deus-ex-machina solution to the siege.

Use Thrin to reveal and complicate, not to solve the siege. A defensive memory response becomes visible under pressure: abduction, proximity to the Meat Mind's work, or the party trying to get Thrin back into the line.

The memory resists the Meat Mind's attempt to absorb or control it. That resistance should be readable at the table without turning Thrin into a siege-ending weapon.

Useful table line: it wants what the forest saw.

It can communicate that the enemy's interest is the forest-memory itself. It may offer useful sensory or history fragments. It should not dictate the party's strategic direction.

Preserve uncertainty about exactly what the carrier knows and why the enemy needs it. Do not invent a complete answer here.

If the table never gives this Beat room, do not force a cutscene. If Thrin is in danger, that danger is already enough.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v1 kind=beat id=beat:wall-hinge -->
### Beat 4: The wall reaches catastrophic failure; Lysandro chooses to put himself in the hinge

This is the climax hinge. Structural failure becomes immediate and visible. Status quo is what dies here, not necessarily the town.

The existing crack, the hum, and the work Lysandro named become one crisis. A section, seam, or support reaches catastrophic failure where the party can see it.

Lysandro chooses to put himself in the hinge: the man with the work crew, the jacks, and the wall-sense goes to the failing point instead of staying in commentary.

He may die, but his death is not mandatory and must not be a predetermined cutscene. Any death must emerge from player-visible stakes and action. The choice to stand in the hinge is his; the outcome is the table's.

The party must decide what to save: a wall section, people, an access route, Thrin, infrastructure, Lysandro, or some unexpected combination.

Keep the hinge player-solvable. A clever engineering, magic, or social plan should be allowed to change the geometry of the crisis. The objective is to make remaining in the exact current status quo impossible, not to predetermine destruction.

<!-- dmb-playable-element:v1 kind=beat id=beat:aftermath-fork -->
### Beat 5: Mireward survives, counts its losses, and the players choose where the campaign goes

Once the immediate climax resolves, give a real breath before asking direction. Do not force a long-rest decision in prep unless table events make it obvious.

- Triage the wounded.
- Surface refugee status.
- Surface wall and tunnel condition.
- Surface supply, healing, and Hesta information if it is relevant and known.
- Surface what leadership actually knows.
- Surface any news or absence from Tealeaf, Grobnok, Mirathorn, or Edge only when that information is established or available.

This Beat is successful if the table has enough information to choose intentionally, even if the choice surprises the prep.

Decision forks

These are ordinary reminders, not locked branches. Let the players choose what problem they pursue next.

Go north by road.
Fastest and most legible. Pursue the strategic source and reach Edge.

Trace the underground network.
Dangerous, potentially faster, and much stranger. This could become a reconnaissance route rather than conveniently leading all the way to Edge.

Remain in Mireward and redesign its defense.
Totally viable. If they choose this, they can probably make Mireward much harder to attack again—but Edge's clock advances without them.

Evacuate / abandon the current defensive perimeter.
Possibly the smartest response if the wall is structurally compromised. Mireward becomes people rather than masonry.

If they stay, name likely consequences without turning them into a forced punishment: Edge continues to deteriorate; refugee pressure can increase; the enemy gains time to adapt; Mireward may become better defended if the party actually invests in it.

Exit ramps

Keep these available if play needs an off-ramp from the current geometry:

- northbound
- into the earth
- Mireward falls back to an inner line
- an unexpected player-created solution

Potential information sources include Nera, Salla, Brin, Thrin, and Lysandra / Lysandro as established at the table. Do not invent new facts merely to fill these names.
