At CR 5, Captain Lysandra Ironveil should feel less like a capable guard officer and more like the fixed point everyone else organizes around: upright, polished, and controlled, but with enough hard-earned weight in her voice that even a casual order lands like a decision already made. She does not need to shout to dominate a room; she carries command through economy, timing, and the sense that she has already measured the room’s exits, risks, and weak points before anyone else finished speaking. In battle, she reads as a disciplined front-liner whose confidence is scarred by strain rather than inflated by heroics—quieter, sharper, and more visibly burdened than a celebratory champion, but still unwavering. Her squad should feel sturdier under her than they do on their own: not coddled, not micromanaged, but suddenly able to move as a unit because she has given them clear roles, clean priorities, and the nerve to act. Her pressure on allies is not warmth so much as accountability; she expects competence, rewards it immediately, and corrects mistakes with a cold precision that makes everyone around her focus up. Against enemies, she should project the kind of battlefield authority that makes foes feel outnumbered even when the numbers are equal, as if her presence itself turns chaos into an organized advance and tells her people that retreat is no longer the only option.
