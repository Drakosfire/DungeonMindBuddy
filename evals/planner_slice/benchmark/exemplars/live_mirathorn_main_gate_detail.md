# Gold exemplar — Mirathorn main gates (table-ready)

Dense GM-facing prep aligned to **The City of Mirathorn** city guide: what the **main gates** feel like under festival pressure, how **toll** and **guards** shape the bottleneck, and how **Shepherd's Flock** colors the curb without turning the entrance into a combat set-piece.

## At the threshold (read-aloud spine)

You hear Mirathorn before you see its banners: cart axles, shouted names, a gate clerk’s stamp rhythm, and—threaded through it—chanting that does not sound like celebration.

The **main gates** read as civic theater: **grand and sturdy**, **reinforced wood and iron**, banded and studded so the leaves feel like they were grown to take a ramming. Flanking them, **statues of the city’s founders** lean into the role of hosts: open hands, watchful eyes, stone smiles that look welcoming until you notice how many petitioners avoid meeting their gaze.

Above and behind, **ancient stone** walls rise—tall, moss-softened, honest about age. Ivy maps old repairs. The gates are the polite smile; the walls are the reminder that Mirathorn survived worse than a crowded Tuesday.

## What PCs see and navigate

**Width and choke:** The opening is wide enough for two wagons only if everyone cooperates—which nobody does. The approach funnels trade goods, sheep-counts, performers’ props, and pilgrims into a single patient crush.

**Guards and flow:** Guards are not theatrical villains; they are **managing flow**, checking manifests, flagging contraband with the bored precision of people who have seen every smuggle trick twice. They break crowd surge into pulses: stop, shuffle, stamp, repeat.

**Toll as story lever:** The **toll** is **steep**—stated plainly at the booth, posted where illiteracy cannot excuse ignorance. It is framed as order; to many in line it reads as exclusion. That discontent is the living wire of the scene.

## Shepherd's Flock at the gate

Outside the **main gates**, **Shepherd's Flock** stages a **protest**: signs, pamphlets, call-and-response chants against the toll as moral injury. Leaders are charismatic agitators, not level-bosses—unless the table escalates.

Use them to:

- offer PCs a faction face without forcing recruitment,
- complicate “we just pay and walk in,”
- foreshadow council/cult tensions already in the city guide’s NPC stances.

## Festival of Expansion color

The **Festival of Expansion** raises stakes without rewriting geography: more bodies, more ribbon-color politics, more chances for a “harmless” customs check to become public drama.

If you use Longmont table history at the gate: ribbon choice can echo **Black and Gold** (Empire echo) vs **Burgundy** (city colors)—not as lore quiz, as visible tribal costume in the crowd pressure.

## Sensory kit (quick)

- **Sound:** iron hinges in wind, coin counting, distant rehearsal horns.
- **Smell:** river mud, fried dough, sweat, cheap ink from pamphlets.
- **Touch:** splinter-risk railings, sun-warmed stone, cold shadow when a founder statue swallows light.

## PC hooks (non-combat first)

- A guard recognizes a PC’s name from a warrant that should not exist.
- A protest pamphlet cites a law PCs know was repealed—who benefits from the lie?
- A founder statue’s plaque has fresh chisel marks—too small to be vandalism, too precise to be idle.

## Open questions (leave unanswered at the table)

- Is today’s toll spike policy, corruption, or festival surcharge—and who gets blamed first?
- Which gate sergeant is actually on Shepherd payroll vs simply tired?
