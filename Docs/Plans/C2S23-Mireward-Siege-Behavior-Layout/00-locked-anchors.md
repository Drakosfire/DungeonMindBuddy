## Tone anchors

Mireward should feel under pressure, not incompetent.

It is loud, free, improvisational, proud, anti-authoritarian, and socially elastic. Its strengths are mutual aid, cultural courage, music, quick local trust, and stubborn refusal to become a command-state. Its weaknesses are loose process, contested authority, inconsistent counts, emotional overcorrection, and the way care can create blind spots.

The siege should feel like civic Lovecraftian horror:

- people trying to treat neighbors who may be vectors,
- songs that might heal morale or carry the wrong rhythm,
- counts that do not match,
- kin recognized among the glassy-eyed,
- officials who are each correct about one danger and wrong about another,
- and a town that can still choose decency while becoming afraid.

## Session goal

By the end of the first siege-pressure beat, the GM should know:

- who is in charge of which pressure,
- where refugees go,
- where glassy-eyed people can disappear,
- where food, beds, water, and panic concentrate,
- which NPCs help, destabilize, minimize, or mobilize,
- which sites become tactical / social chokepoints,
- and which party choices improve Mireward's odds before the next combat beat.
